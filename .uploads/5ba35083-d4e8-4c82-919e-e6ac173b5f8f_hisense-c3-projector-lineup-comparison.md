# Which Hisense C3 Projector Should You Buy? Match Your Room in 30 Seconds.

Hisense launched the C3 family at IFA and put it on sale in the US this fall, and reviewers quickly noticed what it is: one excellent projector, sold at four brightness levels. Every model carries the 4K RGB triple-laser engine, the built-in gimbal, Dolby Vision and IMAX Enhanced, and a 65-to-300-inch range. The four Amazon listings carry 4.1 to 4.2 stars across roughly 800 combined reviews.

So the only real question is which brightness matches your room. Start here:

- **Dark bedroom or basement, budget firm:** the [C3](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H42SRKV3), 2,500 ANSI lumens.
- **Normal living room, evening viewing:** the [C3 Pro](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HJFSRHHV), 3,000 ANSI, currently $2,297.97 on Amazon. Most people's answer.
- **Living room with windows you can't control:** the [C3 Ultra](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HGLLY3JB), 4,000 ANSI.
- **Sun-drenched great room, or big-screen gaming:** the [C3 Ultra Max](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HGLHKGMH), 5,000 ANSI.

*Prices checked November 2026.*

## Why this family works

The C3 takes the triple-laser engine from Hisense's fixed laser cinema boxes, the line specialists have reviewed warmly for years, and mounts it in a rotating gimbal. Aim it at the bedroom ceiling, swivel it to a side wall, carry it to the backyard. Autofocus, auto keystone, screen fitting and obstacle avoidance re-square the picture after every move, and optical zoom (1.6x, rising to 2x on the Ultra Max) fills the wall optically, preserving full 4K sharpness where digital keystone quietly eats it.

The light source is the differentiator: dedicated red, green and blue lasers instead of the lamp-plus-color-wheel design of nearly every projector under $2,000. Notebookcheck's launch coverage put the family at up to 118% of the BT.2020 gamut, wider than nearly any TV. In practice, *Dune: Part Two's* desert holds searing, distinct color, and animation looks painted rather than projected.

The shared equipment list runs long: a seven-level iris holding up to 5,000:1 dynamic contrast, anti-glare processing for lit rooms, HDR10 and HLG under Dolby Vision and IMAX Enhanced, and serious built-in sound, JBL-tuned on the global C3, Devialet-tuned 56W 2.1 with a subwoofer on the Pro, Ultra and Ultra Max. Plenty of buyers run a C3 with no soundbar and never feel the gap.

## The four, in detail

**[Hisense C3](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H42SRKV3), 2,500 ANSI, the entry.** The complete package at the family's lowest price: triple-laser color, the gimbal, JBL sound, IMAX Enhanced, Netflix built in. In a room with controlled light it throws a rich 100-to-120-inch picture. The smart buy for first projectors and dark rooms.

**[Hisense C3 Pro](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HJFSRHHV), 3,000 ANSI, the people's pick.** $2,297.97 on Amazon right now, under its own list and the family's highest rating at 4.2 stars over 200+ reviews. The 1.6x optical zoom gives placement freedom, and the gaming spec is a surprise at this level: 240Hz VRR, 1ms latency, and FreeSync Premium certification, plus Hisense's Auto Magic AI setup. The default answer for living rooms.

**[Hisense C3 Ultra](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HGLLY3JB), 4,000 ANSI, the daylight model.** Moves to a four-laser-chip imaging design Hisense rates about 5% more efficient. This is the one that behaves like a television at noon: a 120-inch image holds saturation through afternoon sun that sends lesser projectors into gray.

**[Hisense C3 Ultra Max](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HGLHKGMH), 5,000 ANSI, the flagship.** The brightest compact projector Hisense sells, four-chip engine, 2x optical zoom for the widest placement range, 1ms gaming latency. For sun-drenched spaces and gamers who want 150 inches of low-lag screen, it's the ceiling of the category.

## Our pick, plainly

For most rooms the **C3 Pro at $2,297.97** is the buy: enough brightness for lamps-on evenings and drawn-curtain afternoons, the family's best rating, and a gaming spec nobody else in the gimbal class matches. Buy the Ultra when windows are the problem, the Ultra Max when you want the biggest, brightest, fastest of everything, and the base C3 when the room is already dark and the savings matter more.

## Against the gimbal class

JMGO's N1S family, currently $1,149-1,479 direct, is the budget path into rotating projectors, and pleasant enough. The C3 line holds every advantage that shows up after purchase: Devialet audio against smaller built-ins, Dolby Vision against its absence, brightness headroom to 5,000 ANSI, and now FreeSync Premium on the Pro. XGIMI's Horizon Ultra, around 2,300 lumens in a fixed chassis, doesn't gimbal at all. Rivals compete on price; nothing in the class competes on the complete package.

And if your big screen will live in one fixed spot, Hisense's [PX4 Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Hisense+PX4+Pro+projector) console-style laser cinema is the cleaner permanent install, with its own gaming pedigree; our full review covers it. One C3, one wall, one evening, and the living room stops being a living room at showtime.
