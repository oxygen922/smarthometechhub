### Resolution

In 2026, 2K should be your minimum. 1080p is still acceptable for budget cameras, but 4K is increasingly becoming the standard for premium models. Higher resolution means clearer details — you'll be able to read license plates and identify faces more easily.

### Night Vision

Color night vision is a must-have. Black-and-white infrared footage is fine for basic monitoring, but color footage gives you much more useful information, especially when identifying clothing or vehicle colors.

### Power Source

Battery-powered cameras offer flexibility but require charging. Wired cameras provide continuous power but limit placement options. Solar-powered cameras are a great middle ground if you have adequate sunlight.

### Local vs. Cloud Storage

Local storage (via a base station or SD card) saves you money on subscriptions and keeps your data private. Cloud storage offers off-site backup and easier access from anywhere. Many cameras support both.

### AI Detection

Look for cameras with on-device AI that can distinguish between people, animals, vehicles, and packages. This reduces false alerts and makes notifications more useful.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Smart Security Camera?

Smart security cameras are ideal for:

- **Homeowners** who want to monitor their property while away
- **Renters** who need a non-permanent security solution
- **Parents** who want to keep an eye on children or pets
- **Small business owners** monitoring entrances and inventory
- **Frequent travelers** who want peace of mind

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $100:** Basic 1080p cameras with limited features. Good for indoor use or as a secondary camera.
- **$100–$250:** Mid-range cameras with 2K resolution, AI detection, and decent smart home integration.
- **$250–$500:** Premium cameras with 4K video, advanced AI, and local storage options.
- **$500+:** Multi-camera systems or high-end models with professional-grade features.

Don't forget to factor in subscription costs. A $150 camera with a $5/month subscription will cost you $270 over two years. Subscription-free options like Eufy may cost more upfront but save you money long-term.

### Wired vs. Wireless

Wired cameras are more reliable and don't require charging, but installation can be tricky. Wireless cameras are easier to install and reposition but need regular charging or solar panels.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Tips

Most modern smart security cameras are designed for DIY installation. Here's a quick rundown:

1. **Choose your location.** Mount cameras 8–10 feet high, angled slightly downward. Avoid pointing them directly at the sun or reflective surfaces.
2. **Check your Wi-Fi signal.** Use your phone to test signal strength at the mounting location. A weak signal will cause dropouts and lag.
3. **Mount the camera.** Most cameras come with screws and anchors. For renters, adhesive mounts are available but less secure.
4. **Connect to the app.** Download the manufacturer's app, create an account, and follow the on-screen pairing instructions.
5. **Adjust motion zones.** Customize detection zones to avoid alerts from sidewalks, trees, or roads.

### Smart Home Integration

All three cameras we reviewed work with major smart home platforms, but there are differences:

- **Arlo Pro 6:** Works with Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple HomeKit. Best for cross-platform households.
- **Ring Spotlight Cam Pro:** Works exclusively with Alexa. Best for Amazon-centric homes.
- **EufyCam S4:** Works with Alexa and Google Assistant. No HomeKit support.

For the best experience, choose a camera that matches your existing smart home ecosystem. If you use an Echo Show as your primary display, Ring is the obvious choice. If you're an Apple household, Arlo is your best bet.
