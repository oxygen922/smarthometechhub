## Key Features to Consider (With Comparison Table)

Choosing a security camera involves more than just picking the one with the highest resolution. Here are the critical features to evaluate before making a purchase.

### Resolution and Field of View
**Resolution:** 4K is now the gold standard for outdoor cameras, providing the ability to zoom in on details like license plates. However, 2K is perfectly adequate for most indoor uses and offers a good balance between clarity and file size. 1080p is becoming outdated and should only be considered for the most budget-conscious buyers.
**Field of View (FoV):** A wider FoV (above 140 degrees) allows you to cover more area with a single camera, but it can introduce "fisheye" distortion. A FoV between 120-160 degrees is generally ideal for most residential applications.

### Smart Detection and AI
Modern cameras use AI to differentiate between motion triggers. Look for **Person Detection**, **Pet Detection**, **Vehicle Detection**, and **Package Detection**. This is crucial for reducing false alerts. More advanced cameras offer **Facial Recognition**, which can alert you when a specific known person (like a family member) arrives, versus an unfamiliar face. Crucially, check if this processing is done **on-device** (local) or in the **cloud**. Local processing is faster and often free, while cloud processing usually requires a subscription.

### Power Source and Connectivity
**Wired (PoE/USB):** Offers the most reliable power and often supports higher data rates. Installation is more complex and may require a professional.
**Wireless (Battery):** Offers ultimate flexibility in placement. The main drawback is battery life, which can range from a few weeks to several months depending on usage and features.
**Solar:** A great hybrid solution for outdoor cameras, eliminating the need for frequent charging.
**Connectivity:** Most cameras use Wi-Fi. Ensure the camera supports **5GHz Wi-Fi** for a more stable connection, especially if you have multiple smart devices. Some high-end models offer **dual-band Wi-Fi** or even Ethernet ports for a hardwired connection.

### Storage Options
**Cloud Storage:** Footage is uploaded to the manufacturer's servers. This is convenient and safe from theft or damage to the camera, but it almost always requires a monthly or annual subscription.
**Local Storage (microSD):** Footage is stored on a microSD card inside the camera. This is free and gives you full control over your data, but the card can be stolen or corrupted.
**Network Video Recorder (NVR):** For multi-camera systems, an NVR provides centralized, high-capacity storage, often with a hard drive. This is the most professional and secure setup.

### Privacy and Security
Look for features like **physical privacy shutters** (as seen in the BlueDot), **two-factor authentication (2FA)** for your account, and **on-device AI processing** to minimize data sent to the cloud. Always consider the manufacturer's data privacy policy.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Tips from a Pro
- **Placement is Key:** For outdoor cameras, mount them at a height of 8-10 feet. This provides a good downward angle that captures faces and is out of easy reach for tampering. Ensure the camera is sheltered from direct rain when possible, even if it's weatherproof.
- **Wi-Fi Strength:** Before permanently mounting a wireless camera, check the Wi-Fi signal strength at the desired location using your phone. A weak signal will lead to laggy video and frequent disconnections. Consider a Wi-Fi mesh system to extend coverage to your home's perimeter.
- **Wired Installation:** If you’re not comfortable working with electrical wiring, definitely hire a professional for wired cameras. Ensure your power source is on a dedicated circuit to prevent overloading.
- **Angle Adjustment:** Take your time to adjust the camera's field of view. Aim to capture the approach to your door, not just the door itself. Avoid pointing cameras directly at the sun or highly reflective surfaces to prevent glare and washed-out images.

### Smart Home Integration (2026)
In 2026, a security camera is not an island. The best models integrate seamlessly with your broader smart home ecosystem.
- **Amazon Alexa & Google Home:** All three of our top picks support basic integration with Alexa and Google Home, allowing you to view live feeds on Echo Show, Fire TV, or Nest Hub devices. You can also use voice commands like, "Alexa, show me the front door camera."
- **Apple HomeKit:** For Apple users, look for cameras with native HomeKit Secure Video support. This allows for end-to-end encryption of your footage and storage in your iCloud account, offering a high level of privacy and integration with the Apple Home app. The Aegis ViewPro 4K offers this feature, while the others do not.
- **Automation:** The true power of integration lies in automation. You can create routines such as:
    - **"Goodnight" Routine:** When you say "Goodnight" to your smart speaker, it can trigger your cameras to switch to "Away" mode and start recording.
    - **"Arrival" Routine:** When your phone's GPS detects you're home, it can disarm your cameras and turn off the floodlights.
    - **"Package Detected" Routine:** When your camera detects a package, it can flash a smart light bulb inside your home, letting you know without a phone notification.
