## Key Features to Consider

When selecting a robot mop, it is crucial to look beyond the marketing hype. Here is a breakdown of the critical specifications to evaluate.

### Mopping Technology
- **Vibration Scrubbing**: (e.g., Roborock) Uses a flat pad that vibrates side-to-side at high frequencies. Excellent for even pressure distribution.
- **Spinning Pads**: (e.g., Narwal, Dreame) Uses two rotating discs that spin at high speed. Often better for deep cleaning grout and textured surfaces.
- **Ultrasonic Mopping**: Some high-end models use ultrasonic sensors to detect the level of dirtiness and adjust the water flow and scrubbing intensity automatically.

### Obstacle Avoidance
- **LiDAR Navigation**: Uses a spinning laser to map your home. It is fast and accurate but can struggle with thin objects like cables.
- **AI Camera + 3D Sensors**: Combines a camera with structured light to recognize objects (shoes, cables, pet waste) and avoid them in real-time. This is essential for "set and forget" automation.

### Self-Cleaning Dock
This is the most significant feature upgrade in recent years. Look for:
- **Auto Empty**: Vacuums the dustbin into a larger bag (usually 2.5L to 3L).
- **Mop Washing**: Washes the mop pads after each cycle to prevent spreading dirt.
- **Hot Air Drying**: Dries the pads to prevent bacteria and odor (look for 40°C+ drying).

### Carpet Detection
If you have rugs, this is non-negotiable. The mop must **lift** (10-12mm) to avoid soaking the carpet, or **remove** the mop pad automatically when transitioning from hard floors.

## Buying Guide

- **For the Automation Enthusiast**: If you want the absolute best technology and have a budget of $1,500+, the **Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra** is the undisputed king. It is for those who want the robot to do literally everything, including emptying its own dustbin and cleaning its own mops.

You get 90% of the flagship experience—including the self-cleaning dock and excellent obstacle avoidance—for $400 less. ### Budget Considerations

- **Under $300**: You will find "robot mops" that are essentially spraying mops with a small water tank. These are best for small apartments and daily light maintenance. They lack navigation and self-cleaning features.
- **$500 - $800**: This is the "mid-range" sweet spot. You can find hybrid robots with good LiDAR navigation and a simple mop attachment, but you will likely have to manually remove the mop pad before vacuuming carpets.
- **$1,000+**: This is where the "flagship" tier begins. You get the full self-cleaning dock experience, which is essential for long-term, hands-free maintenance. This is the price bracket we recommend for serious smart home users.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Initial Setup

Setting up a modern robot mop is surprisingly simple. Most models require you to:
1.  **Place the Dock**: Find a permanent spot with a power outlet and about 1.5 feet of clearance on either side.
2.  **Fill the Water Tank**: Use distilled water to prevent mineral buildup in the pipes.
3.  **Map Your Home**: The robot will do a "Quick Map" run, which takes about 10-15 minutes. It uses LiDAR to create a floor plan of your home.

### Smart Home Ecosystem

- **Alexa & Google Assistant**: All three recommended models support voice control. You can say, "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen," or "Hey Google, start the Narwal."
- **Apple Home / Siri Shortcuts**: While native HomeKit support is rare, you can use Siri Shortcuts to trigger cleaning routines via the manufacturer's app.
- **Automation**: The real power lies in app-based automation. You can set the robot to clean specific zones (e.g., "Entryway") every day at 8 AM, or trigger a deep clean when you leave the house (using geofencing).

### Pro Tip for Integration
If you use **Home Assistant**, look for the **Xiaomi Home** or **Roborock** integrations. These allow for local API control, giving you granular control over the robot's power and water levels that the native apps sometimes hide.
