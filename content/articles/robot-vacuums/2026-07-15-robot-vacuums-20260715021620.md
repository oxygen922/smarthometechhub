### Suction Power and Cleaning Performance

Suction power, measured in Pascals (Pa), directly impacts how well a vacuum picks up debris. Higher suction is crucial for deep carpet cleaning, while hard floors require less. However, brush design matters equally—dual rubber brushes prevent hair tangling and agitate carpets effectively. Mop performance is another key factor; vibrating or rotating mops with downward pressure clean better than simple dragging pads.

### Navigation and Mapping

LiDAR navigation uses laser sensors to create precise maps, working well in darkness but potentially missing low objects. Camera-based systems like iRobot’s PrecisionVision can identify specific obstacles but require light. The best systems combine both technologies for reliable performance in all conditions. Look for features like no-go zones, room-specific cleaning, and multi-floor mapping.

### Smart Home Integration

Native support for Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant is standard, but Apple HomeKit support remains rare. Check compatibility with your existing ecosystem. App quality varies significantly; the best apps offer intuitive scheduling, detailed cleaning reports, and easy zone management. Some models include built-in voice assistants for hands-free control.

### Home Compatibility

Measure your home’s square footage and consider floor plan complexity. Open floor plans are easier for robot vacuums to navigate than homes with many rooms and tight corners. Check furniture clearance—most robots need at least 3.5 inches of height to pass under low furniture. For multi-story homes, ensure the model supports multi-floor mapping and can be easily moved between levels.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setting Up Your Robot Vacuum

Installation is straightforward for most modern robot vacuums. Start by unpacking the unit and placing the charging dock in a central location with at least 3 feet of clearance on each side and 1.5 feet in front. Connect the dock to power and ensure it’s on a level surface. For models with auto-empty stations, confirm the dust bag is properly installed.

Next, download the manufacturer’s app and create an account. Follow the in-app instructions to connect the vacuum to your Wi-Fi network (2.4GHz is recommended for stability). The first cleaning run will create a map of your home; allow the vacuum to complete this without interruption. After mapping, you can set up no-go zones, virtual walls, and room labels.

### Smart Home Integration

Integrating your robot vacuum with smart home platforms enhances convenience. For Amazon Alexa, enable the skill in the Alexa app, then link your robot vacuum account. You can then say commands like “Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen.” For Google Assistant, use the Google Home app to link the vacuum’s skill. Apple HomeKit integration is less common but available on select models like the Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra.

Advanced integrations include setting up routines—for example, having the vacuum start cleaning when you leave for work using your smart lock or geofencing. IFTTT (If This Then That) applets can trigger cleaning based on weather conditions or other smart home events. Some models also support voice commands for pausing, resuming, and checking battery status.

### Maintenance and Upkeep

Regular maintenance ensures optimal performance. Empty the dustbin after each cleaning if your model lacks auto-empty. Clean the brush roll weekly to remove hair and debris. Replace filters every 2-3 months and mop pads every 3-6 months. For self-emptying stations, replace dust bags when full (typically every 30-60 days). Clean sensors and charging contacts monthly with a dry cloth.
