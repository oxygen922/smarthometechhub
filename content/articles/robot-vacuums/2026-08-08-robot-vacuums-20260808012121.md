## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Mop?

- **The Busy Professional:** If you work long hours and value your weekends, a robot mop is a worthwhile investment. The self-cleaning docks on the premium models mean you'll only need to interact with the machine every few weeks to empty a bag or replace a filter.
- **Pet Owners:** If you have dogs or cats, your floors are constantly accumulating hair, dander, and muddy paw prints. A robot mop that can vacuum first and then mop is a lifesaver. Look for models with high suction and anti-tangle brushes.
- **Families with Young Children:** Kids are mess machines. From spilled juice to smeared food, a robot mop can run daily to keep floors hygienic without you having to sanitize them manually. The AI obstacle avoidance is crucial here to avoid running over toys.
- **People with Allergies:** Traditional mopping often just spreads dust around. Robot mops with self-cleaning docks trap allergens in the dirty water tank, preventing them from being released back into the air.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $300:** You will find basic robot mops that are essentially "wet swiffer" machines. They lack suction power, have no self-cleaning dock, and require manual pad washing. They are a step up from manual mopping but are not a true hands-off solution.
- **$300 - $600:** This is the "sweet spot" for value. You can find models with good suction, basic obstacle avoidance, and a vibrating mop plate. You will likely have to clean the mop pad yourself, but the cleaning performance is solid.
- **$600 - $1,000:** This is where you get into the "self-maintaining" territory. Docks that wash and dry mop pads become standard. AI navigation and obstacle avoidance are also common here.
- **$1,000+:** This is the premium tier. You get the best of everything: rotating mops, hot water washing, automatic dustbin emptying, and the most advanced AI. This is for those who want the absolute best and have no budget constraints.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Getting Started

Setting up a modern robot mop is generally straightforward, but there are a few steps to ensure optimal performance.

1.  **Unboxing and Dock Placement:** Place the charging dock on a level surface against a wall. Leave at least 3 feet of clearance on the left and right sides and 5 feet in front of the dock to allow the robot to easily find its way home.
2.  **Water Setup:** Fill the clean water tank in the dock with water. **Important:** Use only manufacturer-recommended cleaning solutions. Using generic floor cleaners can damage the internal water pump and void the warranty.
3.  **App Setup:** Download the manufacturer's app (Roborock, Narwal, or Dreame). You will need to connect to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network. The app will guide you through creating a "map" of your home.
4.  **Mapping Run:** Run the robot in "Map Mode" (usually with the mop pad removed). This allows the robot to scan your home using LiDAR to create a precise floor plan. This first run is slower, but it is essential for accurate navigation and room-specific cleaning.

### Smart Home Integration

All three of our top recommendations are compatible with the major smart home ecosystems:

- **Amazon Alexa:** You can say, "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen," and the robot will start a targeted cleaning session.
- **Google Home:** Similar voice commands work with Google Assistant. You can also create routines, such as "Goodbye" which triggers the robot to clean the entryway when you leave for work.
- **Apple HomeKit:** Support is becoming more common, though it's still not universal. The Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra supports HomeKit, allowing you to control it via the Apple Home app and Siri.

**Pro Tip:** Use "zone cleaning" in the app to set specific areas for mopping. For example, you can set a "High Traffic" zone in the entryway to be mopped twice daily, while the rest of the house is mopped every other day. This saves water and battery life.
