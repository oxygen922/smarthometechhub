## Key Features to Consider (With Comparison Table)

Before you buy any robot vacuum in 2026, you need to understand what separates a great one from a frustrating one. Here are the features that matter most:

### 1. AI Obstacle Avoidance
This is the single most important feature. A robot without good obstacle avoidance will still get tangled in cords, push pet waste across your floor, and choke on socks. Look for systems that use a combination of 3D structured light and cameras, rather than just a single sensor.

### 2. Self-Cleaning Dock
The days of manually emptying dustbins are over for premium models. A self-cleaning dock should auto-empty the dustbin (ideally into a sealed bag for hygiene) and wash/dry mop pads. Hot water washing (60°C or above) is better for sanitizing.

### 3. Suction Power
Measured in Pascals (Pa), this tells you how much pulling force the vacuum has. For 2026, anything below 4,000 Pa is entry-level. 6,000-8,000 Pa is good for most homes. 10,000 Pa+ is for heavy pet hair and deep carpets.

### 4. Navigation & Mapping
LiDAR-based navigation is the gold standard. It creates accurate maps of your home and allows for efficient, systematic cleaning patterns. Camera-based navigation (like iRobot) is also good but can struggle in low-light conditions.

### 5. Mop Functionality
Most premium robots now come with a mop pad that attaches to the bottom. The best ones can automatically lift the mop when transitioning to carpet, preventing wet carpet issues. Some can even return to the dock to rewash the mop mid-cycle.

### 6. Smart Home Integration
Does it work with Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple HomeKit? Does it have an API for advanced users? These matter if you want to integrate the vacuum into your broader smart home routines.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum is surprisingly simple, but there are a few things to keep in mind.

### Initial Setup

1. **Unbox and charge:** Most robots come partially charged. Plug in the dock, place it against a wall with at least 1.5 feet of clearance on each side and 5 feet in front, and let the robot charge fully before its first run.
2. **Map your home:** The first run should be a "mapping only" pass. Let the robot explore without cleaning so it can build an accurate map. This takes 30-60 minutes depending on your home size.
3. **Set no-go zones:** After mapping, use the app to set virtual boundaries for areas you don't want the robot to go (pet food bowls, play areas, etc.).
4. **Schedule cleaning:** Set a recurring schedule. Most users prefer daily cleaning in high-traffic areas and every-other-day in bedrooms.

### Smart Home Integration

All three robots I tested support Alexa and Google Assistant voice commands. You can say "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen" and the robot will target that specific room. The iRobot j9+ adds native Apple HomeKit support, which is a significant advantage if you're an iPhone user.

For advanced users, both Roborock and Dreame offer local APIs that allow integration with Home Assistant. This lets you create complex automations, like "If the front door unlocks after 5 PM, clean the entryway." This level of integration is a game-changer for smart home enthusiasts.

One important note: all three robots support Matter, the new smart home standard. This means they'll work with any Matter-compatible hub, including the latest Apple HomePod, Google Nest Hub, and Amazon Echo. This future-proofs your purchase.
