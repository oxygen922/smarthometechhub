## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Vacuum?

Robot vacuums are ideal for:

- **Busy professionals and families** who want daily floor maintenance without lifting a finger
- **Pet owners** dealing with constant shedding — models with 8,000+ Pa suction and tangle-free brushes are essential
- **Allergy sufferers** — HEPA-filtered robots capture fine dust and dander
- **Apartment and condo dwellers** with mostly hard floors, where mid-range models shine
- **Smart home enthusiasts** who want voice-controlled, scheduled cleaning integrated with Alexa or Google Home

You might **skip** a robot vacuum if you have mostly high-pile or shag carpet (uprights still win), live in a very cluttered space, or expect spotless deep cleans — robots are maintenance tools, not replacements for occasional deep cleaning.

### Budget Considerations

In 2026, the robot vacuum market splits into three clear tiers:

- **$200–$400:** Solid daily maintenance, basic mapping, manual emptying. Best for apartments and first-timers.
- **$400–$800:** The sweet spot. LiDAR navigation, decent suction, often self-emptying or mopping.
- **$800–$1,500:** Flagship automation — self-emptying, self-washing mops, AI obstacle avoidance, top-tier suction.

Don't forget **ongoing costs**: replacement bags ($15–$30 each, lasting 1–2 months), filters ($20–$40/year), brushes, and mop pads. Budget roughly $100–$150 annually for consumables on premium models. Also watch for seasonal sales — Prime Day, Black Friday, and back-to-school events routinely knock 20–30% off list prices.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum takes 10–20 minutes and requires minimal technical skill. Here's what to expect:

1. **Unbox and charge:** Most robots arrive partially charged; a full charge takes 3–4 hours.
2. **Place the base station:** Choose a wall-adjacent spot with Wi-Fi coverage and about 3 feet of clearance on each side. Avoid direct sunlight, which can confuse camera sensors.
3. **Download the app:** Pair via Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz is most reliable). The app walks you through initial mapping.
4. **Run a mapping pass:** Let the robot explore your home once with lights on. This creates the floor plan you'll use for room-specific cleaning and no-go zones.
5. **Set schedules and zones:** Most apps let you schedule per-room cleans, block off areas (like pet bowls or cable nests), and set suction/mop intensity.

**Smart home integration** is where these devices shine. All three recommended models support **Amazon Alexa** and **Google Assistant** for voice commands like "Alexa, ask the robot to clean the kitchen." Premium models add **Siri Shortcuts**, **IFTTT** automations, and even **Matter** compatibility for future-proofing. A popular automation: trigger a kitchen clean when your smart lock reports everyone has left the house. Just ensure your robot's Wi-Fi band matches your network — 5 GHz-only setups are a common frustration.
