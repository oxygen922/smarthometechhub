## Key Features to Consider

When choosing a robot mop, understanding the technology is crucial. Here are the key features you should evaluate:

| Feature | What it Does | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Navigation System** | Uses LiDAR (laser) or VSLAM (camera) to map your home and navigate. | LiDAR is more accurate in the dark and provides better mapping, while VSLAM is often cheaper. |
| **Mopping System** | The method used to clean floors (spinning pads vs. vibrating pad). | Spinning pads generally excel at scrubbing and edge cleaning, while vibrating pads are effective on stubborn spots. |
| **Self-Cleaning Dock** | The dock can wash, dry, and/or empty the robot automatically. | This is the ultimate convenience feature, reducing manual maintenance to almost zero. |
| **Carpet Detection** | The robot's ability to detect carpet and lift the mop pad. | Essential for homes with mixed flooring to prevent wet carpets. |
| **Obstacle Avoidance** | Uses cameras and sensors to avoid objects like cables, shoes, and pet waste. | Saves you from having to pre-clean your floors and prevents the robot from getting stuck. |
| **App & Voice Control** | The ability to control the robot via a smartphone app or voice assistant. | A good app allows for zone cleaning, scheduling, and customization. Look for Alexa/Google compatibility. |
| **Water Flow Control** | The ability to adjust the amount of water used for mopping. | Allows you to tailor the mopping to different floor types (e.g., less water for hardwood). |
| **Battery Life & Runtime** | How long the robot can clean on a single charge. | A longer runtime is crucial for larger homes. Look for a runtime of at least 120 minutes. |

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Getting your new robot mop set up is generally a straightforward process, but a few steps ensure optimal performance.

**Step-by-Step Installation:**

1.  **Unbox and Place the Dock:** Choose a location for the dock that is on a hard, level surface and has access to a power outlet. Ensure there is ample space on either side of the dock for the robot to enter and exit easily.
2.  **Install the App:** Download the manufacturer's app (e.g., Roborock, Dreame, iRobot) from the App Store or Google Play. Create an account if you don't have one.
3.  **Connect the Robot:** Power on the robot and place it on the dock. The app will guide you through connecting to your home's 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network. This is crucial, as most smart home devices do not support 5GHz networks.
4.  **Map Your Home:** The first time you run the robot, it will do a "mapping run." This involves the robot navigating your entire home to create a map. It's best to clear the floor of any obstacles like loose cables, toys, or pet bowls before this run.
5.  **Customize Your Settings:** Once the map is created, you can use the app to set "No-Go Zones" for areas you don't want the robot to enter (e.g., a play area or near a pet's food bowl). You can also set "No-Mop Zones" for carpets. Adjust water flow levels for different rooms and set a cleaning schedule.

**Smart Home Integration:**
All of our top picks integrate seamlessly with major smart home ecosystems. Here’s how they work:

- **Amazon Alexa & Google Assistant:** You can use simple voice commands like, "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen," or "Hey Google, start the vacuum." This allows for a truly hands-free experience.
- **Apple Home (Limited):** While native Apple Home integration is not always available, many of these robots now support Apple Watch and Siri Shortcuts. This allows you to trigger a cleaning routine using a custom Siri command on your iPhone or Apple Watch.
- **Automation with Other Devices:** You can create powerful automations using the app's built-in scheduling or through platforms like IFTTT. For example, you can set your robot to start mopping after your smart lock detects you've left for work, or when your smart speaker plays your "Goodbye" routine.
