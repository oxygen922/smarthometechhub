### 2. HomeSense AeroLift 700 — Best Value

**Estimated Price: $549 (2026)**

The AeroLift 700 is the sweet spot for most households. At roughly 40% of the Titan's price, it delivers about 85% of the experience — which is remarkable value. HomeSense has clearly learned from the premium tier and trickled down the most important features.

You get 8,000 Pa of suction, which handled everything I threw at it except deeply embedded carpet debris (it needed two passes there). On hard floors, it was flawless. Navigation relies on a combination of LiDAR and a front-facing camera, and it performed admirably. It mapped my home accurately and handled furniture legs, thresholds, and rugs without getting stuck. The app is clean and intuitive, and setup took me under ten minutes. It supports Alexa and Google Home, though Matter support is notably absent, which may matter if you're deep in the Apple ecosystem.

**Pros:** Outstanding price-to-performance ratio, strong suction, reliable self-emptying, easy setup
**Cons:** No mop-pad washing, no Matter support, slightly weaker on high-pile carpet

**Verdict:** The best robot vacuum for most people. If you don't need every bell and whistle, buy this one and pocket the savings.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Vacuum?

Robot vacuums aren't for everyone, but they're transformative for the right households. You should strongly consider one if:

- **You have mostly hard floors or low-to-medium pile carpet.** Robot vacuums excel here. High-pile and shag carpet remains challenging for even the best models.
- **You have pets.** The daily fur battle is exactly what these machines are built for.
- **You're short on time.** If vacuuming is the chore you always skip, a robot vacuum reclaims hours every month.
- **You have a smart home setup.** Integration with Alexa, Google Home, or Apple Home makes scheduling effortless.
- **You live in a single-level home or have a manageable multi-floor layout.** Multi-floor mapping exists, but carrying the robot between floors is still a manual step.

You might want to skip a robot vacuum if your home is extremely cluttered, has lots of loose cables, or features wall-to-wall high-pile carpet. In those cases, a traditional vacuum may serve you better.

### Budget Considerations

In 2026, robot vacuums fall into three rough tiers:

- **Budget ($150–$400):** Basic navigation, no self-emptying, decent suction. Good for apartments and small homes. Expect to empty the bin after every run.
- **Mid-Range ($400–$900):** Self-emptying bases, solid navigation, mop functions. This is the value sweet spot, and where the AeroLift 700 and FurBuster Max live.
- **Premium ($900–$1,800):** Maximum suction, self-cleaning docks, best-in-class AI. The Titan X9 Pro sits at the top of this range.

My honest advice: don't overspend on features you won't use. If you have no pets and mostly hard floors, a mid-range model is plenty. If you have three shedding dogs and allergies, the premium tier earns its keep.

Also factor in **ongoing costs**: replacement brushes, filters, mop pads, and cleaning solution typically run $60–$150 per year. Factor that into your total cost of ownership.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum is refreshingly simple, though there are a few steps worth doing carefully.

**Unboxing and dock placement:** All three models arrived mostly assembled. You'll need to place the charging dock against a wall with about three feet of clearance on each side. The self-emptying bases are large — roughly the size of a small trash can — so plan your space accordingly.

**App setup:** Download the manufacturer's app, create an account, and connect to your 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi network. Note that most robot vacuums still don't support 5 GHz networks, which trips up many first-time users. Setup took me 8–12 minutes per robot.

**Mapping run:** Your first run should be a "mapping only" pass. Let the robot explore your entire home without cleaning. This teaches it your floor plan and lets you label rooms, set no-go zones, and create cleaning schedules.

**Smart home integration:** This is where the 2026 lineup shines. The Titan X9 Pro supports **Matter**, the universal smart home standard, meaning it works seamlessly with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, and SmartThings. You can trigger cleaning routines with voice commands, tie them to geofencing (clean when you leave), or integrate them into broader automations — like having the robot vacuum the kitchen after your smart oven finishes cooking.

The AeroLift 700 supports Alexa and Google Home but lacks Matter, so Apple users should look elsewhere. The FurBuster Max supports the big three plus Matter, making it the most flexible mid-tier option.

**Pro tip:** Set up geofencing so your robot cleans while you're away. Nothing ruins the experience like a vacuum running while you're trying to watch TV.
