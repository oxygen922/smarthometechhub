# Dyson Nurovi Robot Vacuum Review (2026): The Lidar-Powered Lineup That Finally Takes Robot Vacuums Seriously

## Introduction

Dyson spent years telling anyone who would listen that robot vacuums just weren't good enough—then sat on the sidelines while iRobot, Roborock, and Dreame ate the category alive. That changes in 2026. The **Dyson Nurovi line** marks the company's first true flagship robot vacuum family, and it arrives with three lidar-powered models, a redesigned brush system borrowed from Dyson's stick vacs, and a genuinely clever self-emptying dock. The catch? The model most people will actually want is a **Costco exclusive**, which means a membership is effectively part of the price of admission. I spent three weeks running the Nurovi lineup through hardwood, thick pile, pet hair, and the kind of chaos a toddler leaves behind. Here's everything you need to know before you buy a **robot vacuum cleaner** in 2026.

---

## Top 3 Product Recommendations

### 1. Dyson Nurovi Pro+ — Best Overall (Costco Exclusive)

**Estimated Price: $1,199 (Costco members only)**

This is the one Dyson wants you to buy, and it's the one you can't buy just anywhere. The **Nurovi Pro+** is sold exclusively through Costco in the US, which is an unusual move for a premium launch but a smart one—Costco's generous return policy softens the blow of a four-figure robot vacuum, and the bundle includes extra filters, a spare brush bar, and a two-year extended warranty at no extra charge.

Under the hood, the Pro+ uses a 360-degree lidar turret paired with Dyson's new **VisiCore navigation**, which builds a live map and cross-references it with a front-facing camera to identify obstacles like charging cables, pet waste, and socks. In my testing, it avoided a deliberately placed phone cable 9 out of 10 times—not perfect, but better than most lidar-only robots.

Suction is rated at **8,500 Pa**, which puts it in the same tier as the Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra and the Dreame X40 Ultra. On medium-pile carpet with embedded pet hair, the Pro+ needed two passes to fully clear a test patch that the Roborock handled in one. On hardwood, though, it was flawless—the redesigned **anti-tangle brush bar** (a carbon fiber and nylon hybrid) picked up long human hair without wrapping, a problem that has plagued every robot vacuum I've tested until now.

The self-emptying dock holds up to **60 days of debris**, and it washes and hot-air-dries the mop pad automatically. The mop itself is a vibrating plate rather than a spinning pad, which is less aggressive on grout lines but gentler on hardwood. Battery life is a claimed **180 minutes** on Quiet mode; I got 142 minutes on Balanced with mopping enabled, which covered about 1,400 square feet before returning to dock.

**Verdict:** The best all-around Dyson robot vacuum, provided you're a Costco member or willing to become one. The Pro+ is the only Nurovi model with the full self-maintenance dock.

---

### 2. Dyson Nurovi — Best Value

**Estimated Price: $799**

The mid-tier **Nurovi** drops the self-washing mop dock and the extended battery, but keeps the same 8,500 Pa suction motor, lidar navigation, and anti-tangle brush bar as the Pro+. What you lose is convenience, not cleaning power.

The standard dock empties the bin automatically but does **not** wash the mop pad—you'll need to rinse it yourself every few days, which is a minor annoyance but hardly a dealbreaker. Battery life drops to a claimed **120 minutes**, and in practice I got about 95 minutes on Balanced. For apartments and smaller homes under 1,200 square feet, that's plenty.

The Nurovi is available at major retailers including Best Buy, Amazon, and Dyson.com, so you don't need a warehouse club membership. If you want Dyson's cleaning performance without the Costco exclusivity, this is your pick.

**Verdict:** The smart buy for most people. You give up mopping automation and some runtime, but the core cleaning experience is identical to the Pro+.

---

### 3. Dyson Nurovi Lite — Best for Small Spaces

**Estimated Price: $549**

The entry-level **Nurovi Lite** is the odd one out: it uses **vSLAM camera navigation** instead of lidar, which means it relies on visual landmarks to map your home. In bright, uncluttered rooms it works fine. In dim lighting or rooms with lots of reflective surfaces, it gets confused—I watched it circle a glass coffee table three times before giving up.

Suction is reduced to **6,000 Pa**, and there's no mop at all. This is a pure vacuum, aimed at studios, dorms, and one-bedroom apartments. The dock is a simple charging base with no auto-empty function, so you'll be emptying the 400ml bin yourself every couple of cleanings.

That said, at $549 it's the cheapest way into Dyson's robot ecosystem, and for a small space with mostly hard floors, it does the job. Just don't expect it to replace a full-size vacuum the way the Pro+ can.

**Verdict:** A niche product. Buy it only if your home is small, bright, and carpet-free.

### Navigation and Mapping

Lidar remains the gold standard for robot vacuum navigation, and the two upper Nurovi models use it well. The Pro+ adds a camera layer that improves obstacle recognition, but it's not a full 3D structured-light system like you'll find on the Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra. If you have pets that occasionally leave surprises, the Pro+'s camera is worth the upgrade.

### Suction and Cleaning Performance

Dyson's 8,500 Pa figure is impressive on paper, but raw suction isn't everything. The **anti-tangle brush bar** is the real story here—it's the first robot vacuum brush I've tested that genuinely resists hair wrap. If you have long hair or shedding pets, this alone might justify the Dyson premium.

### Self-Maintenance

The Pro+'s self-washing, self-drying mop dock is a genuine convenience upgrade, but it adds bulk. You'll need about 18 inches of clearance on each side and a power outlet nearby. The standard Nurovi's auto-empty dock is more compact and easier to tuck into a corner.

### Smart Home Integration

All three models support **Matter**, which means they work natively with Apple Home, Google Home, and Amazon Alexa without a proprietary hub. That's a big deal—Dyson's previous robot vacuum required the MyDyson app for almost everything.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Dyson Nurovi?

**Buy the Pro+ if:**
- You're a Costco member (or willing to join for $65/year)
- You have a mix of carpet and hard floors
- You want a robot vacuum that empties *and* washes itself
- You have pets or long-haired humans in the house

**Buy the standard Nurovi if:**
- You want Dyson's cleaning performance without a warehouse club membership
- Your home is under 1,500 square feet
- You don't mind rinsing a mop pad every few days

**Buy the Lite if:**
- You live in a studio or small apartment
- Your floors are mostly hard surface
- You don't need mopping at all

### Budget Considerations

At $1,199, the Nurovi Pro+ is priced against the **Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra** ($1,399) and the **Dreame X40 Ultra** ($1,499). It undercuts both, and the Costco bundle effectively adds $150–200 in value through extra accessories and warranty coverage.

The standard Nurovi at $799 competes with the **Roborock Q Revo** ($799) and the **Shark Matrix Plus** ($699). Dyson's anti-tangle brush gives it an edge for pet owners, but Roborock's app and mapping are still more polished.

The Lite at $549 is a tougher sell. The **Eufy X10 Pro Omni** ($599) offers lidar navigation and a self-emptying dock for just $50 more. Unless you're loyal to Dyson, the Lite is hard to recommend.

### Ongoing Costs

Replacement parts aren't cheap. A new brush bar runs about $35, filters are $25 for a two-pack, and mop pads are $20 each. Dyson recommends replacing the brush every 12 months and filters every 3 months. Budget roughly **$120–150 per year** in consumables.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setup is straightforward. Unbox, plug in the dock, and the robot walks you through pairing via the **MyDyson app**. The initial mapping run takes about 15 minutes for a 1,000-square-foot home, and the robot builds a surprisingly accurate floor plan.

From there, you can set **no-go zones**, **room-specific cleaning schedules**, and **suction profiles** per room. The app is clean and responsive, though it lacks the granular automation options you'll find in Roborock's app—no conditional triggers like "if nobody's home, clean the kitchen twice."

Matter support is the headline here. I added the Nurovi Pro+ to Apple Home in under two minutes, and it appeared as a native device I could control with Siri. Voice commands like "Hey Siri, ask Dyson to clean the living room" worked reliably. Google Home and Alexa integration were equally smooth.

One caveat: the Pro+'s self-washing dock needs a water line connection for the best experience. Without it, you'll be refilling the clean water tank manually every few days—a 20-second chore, but one that undercuts the "fully automatic" promise.

## Conclusion & Final Verdict

Dyson's first serious foray into robot vacuums is a success, with one asterisk. The **Nurovi Pro+** is a genuinely excellent robot vacuum cleaner—its anti-tangle brush is best-in-class, its suction is competitive with anything under $1,500, and its self-maintenance dock works as advertised. The Costco exclusivity is annoying, but the bundle value is real.

The standard **Nurovi** is the sleeper hit of the lineup. It delivers 95% of the Pro+'s cleaning performance for $400 less, and it's available everywhere. For most US households, this is the one to buy.

The **Nurovi Lite** is the weak link. At $549, it's outclassed by cheaper competitors with better navigation and more features. Dyson loyalists might disagree, but the numbers don't lie.

**Final Verdict:** Dyson has arrived in the robot vacuum category—and it's already a top-three player. The Nurovi line isn't perfect, but it's the most exciting thing to happen to robot vacuums since lidar became standard. If you're in the market for a premium robot vacuum in 2026, the Nurovi Pro+ and standard Nurovi deserve a spot on your shortlist.

**Overall Rating: 4.5/5**
