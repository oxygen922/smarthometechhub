## Key Features to Consider (With Comparison Table)

Before you spend $1,000+ on a robot mop, you need to understand the features that differentiate a great model from a gimmick. Here’s what I look for in every review.

### 1. Mopping Mechanism
- **Spinning Pads:** Most high-end models use two spinning pads that rotate at high speed. This provides scrubbing action. The number of pads and the RPM matter.
- **Vibrating Pad:** Some models (like older Roborock units) use a flat pad that vibrates side-to-side. This is less effective on stubborn dirt.
- **Downward Pressure:** Measured in Newtons (N). More pressure means better cleaning of dried-on messes. Look for at least 7N.

### 2. Self-Cleaning Dock
This is the most important feature in 2026. A good dock should:
- **Wash the mop pads:** Automatically cleans the pads after each use.
- **Dry the mop pads:** Prevents mildew and odor.
- **Empty the dustbin:** Reduces maintenance.
- **Refill the water tank:** Allows for continuous mopping.

### 3. Navigation & Obstacle Avoidance
- **LiDAR:** Essential for mapping your home accurately.
- **AI Cameras:** Used for object recognition (avoiding cables, shoes, pet waste).
- **3D Structured Light:** Helps the robot "see" in 3D for precise navigation.

### 4. Carpet Detection
The robot must be able to detect rugs and lift its mop pads to avoid soaking them. Look for **auto-lift** mechanisms (usually 8-10mm lift).

### 5. Battery Life & Recharging
Most robots can clean for 150-200 minutes on a charge. The best ones automatically return to the dock, recharge, and resume where they left off.

### 6. App & Smart Home Integration
All top models work with Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple HomeKit (check compatibility). The app should allow for:
- Zone cleaning (specific rooms or areas)
- No-go zones (block off areas)
- Water flow control (low, medium, high)

- **Homes with wall-to-wall carpet only:** If you have no hard floors, a robot mop is pointless. Stick to a robot vacuum.

- **People who hate app setup:** While you can use physical buttons, these robots are designed to be controlled via smartphone apps. If you’re not tech-savvy, you might find them frustrating.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Unboxing and Setup
Setting up a modern robot mop is surprisingly simple. Here’s the typical process:

1.  **Choose a Location:** Find a spot for the dock near a power outlet and ideally near a water source (for refilling the tank, though you can manually fill it).
2.  **Download the App:** Scan the QR code on the robot to download the manufacturer’s app (e.g., Roborock, Ecovacs, or Dreame apps).
3.  **Pair the Robot:** The app will guide you through connecting the robot to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network.
4.  **Map Your Home:** The first cleaning run will be a "mapping" run. The robot will systematically traverse your home, using LiDAR to create a detailed floor plan.
5.  **Customize Zones:** Once the map is created, you can set no-go zones, virtual walls, and specific water flow levels for each room.

### Smart Home Voice Control
All three recommended models integrate seamlessly with the major voice assistants.

- **Alexa:** "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen."
- **Google Assistant:** "Hey Google, start the Deebot."
- **Apple HomeKit:** The Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra and Ecovacs X2 Omni support HomeKit, allowing you to add them to your Apple Home scenes (e.g., "Goodnight" triggers a mop of the bathroom).

### Automation Tips
For the best experience, set up automations:
- **Schedule:** Set the robot to clean the kitchen and entryway every day at 2 PM while you’re at work.
- **Trigger-based:** Use a smart door sensor to trigger a mop run when the door opens (great for mudrooms).
- **Zone-Specific:** Only mop the bathroom after 10 PM to avoid wet floors during the day.
