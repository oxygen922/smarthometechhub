## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Vacuum?

Robot vacuums are ideal for:
- **Busy professionals** who don't have time for daily sweeping.
- **Pet owners** dealing with constant shedding.
- **Allergy sufferers** who need frequent HEPA-filtered cleaning.
- **Tech enthusiasts** who want a fully automated smart home.
- **Seniors or mobility-limited individuals** who find manual vacuuming difficult.

They're less ideal for:
- **Homes with very high-pile or shag carpet** (most robots struggle).
- **Cluttered floors** with lots of cables and small objects.
- **Tiny apartments under 400 sq ft** where a cordless stick vacuum is faster.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $300:** Expect basic navigation (bump-and-roll or gyroscope), no self-emptying, and drag-style mopping. Good for small apartments with hard floors.
- **$400–$800:** The sweet spot. You get LiDAR navigation, decent suction, and often a self-emptying dock. This is where most buyers should shop.
- **$900–$1,600:** Flagship features — AI obstacle avoidance, auto-wash mopping docks, and the strongest suction. Worth it if you have pets, carpets, or a large home.

**Hidden Costs:** Replacement parts (brushes, filters, mop pads, dust bags) cost $50–$150 per year. Factor that into your total cost of ownership.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum is surprisingly simple, but there are a few steps to get right.

**Unboxing and Dock Placement:** Place the charging dock against a wall on a hard, level surface. Avoid direct sunlight (it can confuse sensors) and keep at least 3 feet of clearance in front. Plug it in and let the robot charge fully — usually 3–4 hours.

**App Setup:** Download the manufacturer's app (RoboClean, EcoVacs Home, or iRobot Home). Connect to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network — most robots don't support 5GHz. The app will guide you through firmware updates, which you should always install before the first run.

**Mapping Run:** Let the robot do a "mapping only" run first. It will explore your home and build a floor plan. This takes 20–60 minutes depending on size. Once complete, you can label rooms, set no-go zones, and create cleaning schedules.

**Smart Home Integration:**
- **Amazon Alexa:** "Alexa, ask RoboClean to clean the kitchen."
- **Google Assistant:** "Hey Google, start vacuuming."
- **Apple Home (Matter):** Add the robot to the Home app for scene-based automation (e.g., "Goodbye" scene triggers cleaning).
- **IFTTT:** Create advanced automations, like starting a clean when your smart lock detects you've left.

**Voice Control Tips:** Name your rooms clearly in the app (e.g., "Living Room" not "LR") for better voice recognition. Set up routines so the robot cleans when you're away — most models can detect when you've left via geofencing.

**Maintenance Schedule:**
- Empty dustbin: weekly (or every 60 days with self-empty dock)
- Clean brushes: weekly
- Replace filters: every 2–3 months
- Replace brushes: every 6–12 months
- Clean sensors: monthly with a dry cloth
