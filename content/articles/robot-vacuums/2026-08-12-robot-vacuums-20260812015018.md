## Key Features to Consider

When selecting a robot vacuum, understanding the technical jargon is crucial. Here is a breakdown of the features that matter most.

### Navigation: LiDAR vs. Camera vs. Random
- **LiDAR (Laser):** Uses a spinning laser to create a precise map. It works in the dark, is fast, and offers the best systematic cleaning patterns.
- **Camera (VSLAM):** Uses visual cues to navigate. It can identify objects (shoes, cords) but struggles in low light.
- **Random:** No mapping; bumps around. Cheap but inefficient.

### Self-Emptying Docks
This is the single most important convenience feature. A self-emptying dock transfers dirt from the robot's bin into a larger base station bag (often holding up to 60 days of dirt), meaning you rarely have to touch the vacuum. Premium docks add mopping maintenance (washing/drying) and water refilling.

### Suction Power (Pa)
Measured in Pascals (Pa), this indicates raw suction. While marketing numbers are often inflated, generally:
- **2,000 – 3,000 Pa:** Suitable for hard floors and low-pile rugs.
- **4,000 – 6,000 Pa:** Good for mixed flooring and most homes.
- **8,000+ Pa:** High-end performance for deep carpet cleaning and high-traffic homes.

### Obstacle Avoidance (AI)
Modern flagships use cameras and AI to recognize objects. This prevents the robot from getting stuck on cables or smearing pet waste across your floor. This is a "must-have" feature if you have pets or kids.

### Mopping Capabilities
Many models now include a mop pad. Key features to look for:
- **Lifting Mechanism:** The mop pad lifts to avoid wetting carpets.
- **Agitation:** Does it just drag a wet cloth, or does it spin/vibrate to scrub?
- **Self-Cleaning:** Does the dock wash the mop pad to prevent smells?

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum is generally a 15-minute process, but there are nuances.

### Setup Process
1.  **Unboxing:** Find a location for the dock. It needs clearance on both sides (at least 1.5 feet) and a hard, level surface. Do not hide it under a low cabinet, as the robot needs to find it.
2.  **Charging:** Place the robot on the dock and let it fully charge. This can take 3-4 hours for the first charge.
3.  **App Configuration:** Download the manufacturer's app (Roborock, Dreame, iRobot Home, etc.). Create an account and connect to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network. **Note:** Many devices do not support 5GHz networks, which is a common connectivity issue.
4.  **Mapping Run:** Run a "Quick Map" or "Full Map" cycle. This allows the robot to scan your home. It is best to clear the floor of loose cables and clothes for this initial run.
5.  **Customization:** Once mapped, you can set "No-Go Zones" (to keep it away from pet bowls), "Invisible Walls," and specific room labels (e.g., "Kitchen," "Bedroom") for targeted cleaning.

### Smart Home Integration
- **Alexa & Google Assistant:** All three recommended models support voice control. You can say, "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen." The iRobot Essential, lacking Wi-Fi, does not support this.
- **Apple HomeKit:** Support is still hit-or-miss. Roborock and Dreame often require a bridge (like Homebridge) to work with Apple Home. iRobot offers native HomeKit support on some models, but not the Essential.
- **Automations:** The real power lies in automations. You can set triggers like "When I leave home, clean the house" or "When the sun sets, vacuum the living room." This is done through the app's automation section or via IFTTT (If This Then That).

**Troubleshooting Common Issues:** If the robot gets stuck, check for low-hanging furniture. If the map is misaligned, ensure the robot starts from the dock every time. If the Wi-Fi drops, restart the robot and your router.
