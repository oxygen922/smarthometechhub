## Key Features to Consider

Before you buy, it's important to understand which features actually matter—and which are marketing fluff. Here's a breakdown of the most important specs, followed by a comparison table.

### Suction Power (Pa)
Measured in Pascals (Pa), suction power determines how well a robot vacuum pulls debris from carpets and crevices. In 2026, anything above 8,000 Pa is considered strong. Budget models typically range from 2,000–5,000 Pa.

### Navigation Technology
- **LiDAR:** Uses laser mapping for precise, fast navigation. Great in low light.
- **Camera/VSLAM:** Uses visual mapping. Better at recognizing objects but struggles in the dark.
- **Gyroscope:** Basic navigation, best for small spaces.
- **Hybrid (LiDAR + Camera):** The best of both worlds—used in premium models.

### Self-Maintenance
- **Self-emptying base:** Holds weeks of debris.
- **Self-washing mop:** Cleans the mop pad automatically.
- **Self-drying:** Prevents mold and odors.

### Mopping Capability
- **Static mop:** Drags a damp cloth. Basic.
- **Vibrating mop:** Scrubs at high frequency.
- **Rotating mop:** Dual spinning pads. Best for deep cleaning.

### Battery & Runtime
Most 2026 models run 90–180 minutes. Larger homes may need auto-recharge and resume features.

### Smart Home Integration
Look for Matter support, Wi-Fi 6, and compatibility with Alexa, Google Home, and Apple HomeKit.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Vacuum?

- **Pet owners:** Models with strong suction and rubber brushes (like the Titan X9 Pro) handle pet hair best.
- **Busy professionals:** Self-emptying and self-washing models save the most time.
- **Families with kids:** Look for obstacle avoidance and no-go zones to protect toys and cords.
- **Renters and apartment dwellers:** A budget model like the Mini Q3 is ideal.
- **Smart home enthusiasts:** Prioritize Matter and HomeKit compatibility.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $300:** Expect basic navigation, no self-emptying, and limited mopping.
- **$300–$800:** The sweet spot. You'll get LiDAR, decent suction, and often self-emptying.
- **$800–$1,200:** Premium features like AI obstacle avoidance and advanced mopping.
- **$1,200+:** Flagship everything—self-washing, self-drying, maximum suction, and full smart home integration.

### Additional Tips

- Measure your home's square footage before buying.
- Check if replacement parts (brushes, filters, mop pads) are affordable and easy to find.
- Read the fine print on subscription services—some brands charge for cloud features.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum is far easier than it was five years ago, but there are still a few best practices.

### Unboxing and First Setup
1. **Charge the robot fully** before first use (usually 3–5 hours).
2. **Install the app** and connect to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network (most robots don't support 5GHz).
3. **Place the base station** on a level surface with at least 3 feet of clearance on both sides.
4. **Run a mapping cycle.** Let the robot explore your home without interruption to build an accurate map.

### Smart Home Integration
- **Alexa:** "Alexa, ask RoboClean to clean the kitchen."
- **Google Home:** "Hey Google, start vacuuming."
- **Apple HomeKit:** Use Siri shortcuts and automations.
- **Matter:** The newest standard, allowing cross-platform control and future-proofing.

### Automation Ideas
- Schedule daily cleans while you're at work.
- Trigger cleaning when everyone leaves home (geofencing).
- Integrate with smart locks or motion sensors for zone-specific cleaning.

### Maintenance
- Empty the bin or base every 1–2 weeks.
- Clean brushes and filters monthly.
- Replace filters every 2–3 months.
- Wipe sensors with a dry cloth to maintain navigation accuracy.
