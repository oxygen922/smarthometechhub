## Key Features to Consider

When shopping for a robot vacuum cleaner, not all features are created equal. Here's what matters most, along with a comparison table of our top three picks.

### Suction Power
Measured in Pascals (Pa), suction determines how well the vacuum picks up debris. Higher is better, especially for carpets. Look for at least 2,000Pa for hard floors and 5,000Pa+ for carpets.

### Navigation and Mapping
LiDAR and camera-based systems are the most reliable. LiDAR works well in low light and creates accurate maps, while cameras can identify obstacles. Some budget models use gyroscopes and bump sensors, which are less efficient.

### Self-Emptying Base
A self-emptying base automatically transfers debris from the robot's bin to a larger bag or bin in the dock. This reduces how often you need to empty it—from daily to every 30-60 days.

### Mopping Function
Many robot vacuums now include a mop attachment. Basic models drag a damp cloth, while premium models like the Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra vibrate or scrub for deeper cleaning. Some also self-wash and self-dry the mop pad.

### Battery Life and Runtime
Most robots run for 90-180 minutes on a charge. Larger homes may require a model with longer runtime or auto-recharge and resume features.

### Smart Home Integration
Alexa and Google Assistant are standard. Apple HomeKit is less common but available on some iRobot models. App features like no-go zones, scheduling, and room-specific cleaning are essential for convenience.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Vacuum?

Robot vacuums are ideal for:
- **Busy households:** If you lack time for daily sweeping, a robot vacuum maintains clean floors with minimal effort.
- **Pet owners:** Models with strong suction and tangle-resistant brushes keep pet hair under control.
- **Allergy sufferers:** Regular automated cleaning reduces dust and dander.
- **Tech enthusiasts:** Smart home integration and app control add convenience.

They are less suitable for:
- **Homes with mostly deep-pile carpets:** While high-end models handle carpets, they still can't match upright vacuums for deep cleaning.
- **Very cluttered spaces:** Obstacle avoidance helps, but too much clutter can still cause issues.
- **Tight budgets:** Entry-level models exist, but they lack advanced features and may require more manual intervention.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $300:** Basic models with random navigation and no self-emptying. Good for small apartments with hard floors.
- **$300-$600:** Mid-range models with LiDAR navigation, app control, and sometimes self-emptying. The Shark AI Ultra 2-in-1 falls here.
- **$600-$1,000:** Premium models with strong suction, AI obstacle avoidance, and self-emptying. The iRobot Roomba j9+ Pro is a prime example.
- **$1,000+:** Flagship models with advanced mopping, self-washing docks, and top-tier suction. The Roborock S8 MaxV Ultra leads this category.

Consider ongoing costs: replacement bags, filters, brushes, and mop pads. Self-emptying bases require bags every 1-2 months, costing $5-$10 each.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a robot vacuum is generally straightforward, but a few steps ensure optimal performance.

1. **Unbox and charge:** Most robots come partially charged. Place the dock on a hard, level surface against a wall, and let the robot charge fully before first use.
2. **Download the app:** Create an account and connect the robot to your Wi-Fi network (2.4GHz is usually required).
3. **Run a mapping cycle:** Let the robot explore your home to create a map. This may take a couple of runs. Use the app to label rooms and set no-go zones.
4. **Integrate with smart home:** Link your robot to Alexa, Google Assistant, or Apple HomeKit. You can then use voice commands like "Alexa, ask Roomba to clean the kitchen."
5. **Schedule cleanings:** Set a schedule that fits your routine. Many robots can clean specific rooms on demand.

For best results, clear floors of cables, small toys, and pet waste before each run. While obstacle avoidance has improved, it's not perfect.
