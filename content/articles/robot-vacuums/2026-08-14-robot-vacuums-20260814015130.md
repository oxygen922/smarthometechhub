## Key Features to Consider (With Comparison Table)

Understanding the technical jargon is crucial to making an informed decision. Here is a breakdown of the features that matter most in 2026.

### Navigation and Mapping
- **LiDAR (Laser) vs. Camera (VSLAM):** LiDAR is generally superior, working in the dark and mapping faster. VSLAM uses a camera and is cheaper but struggles in low light.
- **Obstacle Avoidance:** Look for AI-powered systems that can identify and avoid cables, shoes, and pet waste. This prevents the robot from getting stuck or smearing messes across your floor.

### Suction Power (Pa)
- **Pascals (Pa)** measure suction. 2,000-3,000 Pa is adequate for hard floors. 5,000-8,000 Pa is good for carpets. 10,000+ Pa is excellent for deep cleaning and high-pile rugs.

### Mopping
- **Flat Pad vs. Rotating Mops:** Rotating mops (like Dreame) scrub with more friction, but flat vibrating pads (like Roborock) are less likely to fling water.
- **Mop Lifting:** This is essential for hybrid units. The mop must lift automatically to avoid soaking carpets.

### The Dock (Self-Cleaning Base)
- **Self-Emptying:** The robot empties its bin into a larger bag in the dock. Look for bags that last 60+ days.
- **Self-Washing:** The dock washes the mop pad, refills the water tank, and dries the pad with hot air. This is a non-negotiable feature in 2026; without it, you are cleaning the mop by hand every day.

### Battery Life
- **Runtime:** Most robots last 120-180 minutes. For homes over 2,000 sq ft, look for a battery above 5,000mAh. The robot should also have "recharge and resume" functionality.

### Smart Home Integration
- **Matter Protocol:** This is the new standard. Ensure your robot supports Matter for seamless compatibility with Apple HomeKit, Google Home, and Alexa.
- **App Usability:** The app should offer 3D mapping, virtual walls, and the ability to set cleaning schedules and specific water levels for mopping.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum is simpler than installing a smart thermostat, but there are key steps to ensure optimal performance.

### Step 1: Placement of the Dock
- **Location:** Place the dock against a wall in an open area. Ensure there is at least 1.5 feet of clearance on both sides and 4 feet in front of the dock. This allows the robot to enter and exit without struggling.
- **Connectivity:** The dock needs to be near a power outlet. For models with auto-fill/drain (like the Roborock), you may need a plumber to connect a water line, but this is optional for most users who simply refill the clean water tank manually.

### Step 2: The "First Run" Learning Phase
Do not rush this. The first run is not about cleaning; it is about mapping. Pick up loose cables, pet toys, and small rugs that could confuse the sensors. Let the robot run its full mapping cycle without interrupting it. This takes about 30-60 minutes for an average home.

### Step 3: App Setup and Customization
- **Wi-Fi (2.4GHz vs. 5GHz):** Most robot vacuums **only** support 2.4GHz Wi-Fi. If your router broadcasts a single combined network (SSID), you may need to split the bands in your router settings to ensure the robot connects.
- **Matter Protocol:** If your robot supports Matter (like the Roborock), you can add it to Apple Home or Google Home via a QR code. This allows you to trigger cleaning with automations, such as "When I leave the house, start the vacuum."

### Step 4: Zone Mapping and Virtual Walls
Once the map is generated, use the app to:
- **Set No-Go Zones:** Block off areas with cables or pet water bowls.
- **Set Cleaning Schedules:** We recommend scheduling a daily vacuum for high-traffic areas and a weekly mop for hard floors.
- **Adjust Suction by Room:** You can set "Max" suction for the living room carpet and "Quiet" mode for the bedroom to reduce noise.

### Step 5: Integration with Other Devices
- **Voice Control:** "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen."
- **Routines:** Use the Google Home app to create a "Good Morning" routine that starts the vacuum in the kitchen while you make coffee.
