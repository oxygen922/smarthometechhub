### Navigation: LiDAR vs. Camera

LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) uses lasers to map your home in 3D. It works in complete darkness and is generally more accurate for room mapping. Camera-based systems (like iRobot’s PrecisionVision) can identify specific objects (e.g., “that’s a shoe”) but may struggle in low light. For most users, LiDAR is the better choice unless you have pets that leave messes on the floor.

### Suction Power

Suction is measured in Pascals (Pa). For reference:
- **1,500–2,500 Pa:** Good for hard floors and low-pile carpets
- **3,000–4,000 Pa:** Suitable for medium-pile carpets and pet hair
- **5,000+ Pa:** Necessary for high-pile carpets and deep cleaning

Don’t get obsessed with raw numbers—a well-designed brush system can compensate for lower suction. But if you have thick carpets, aim for 4,000 Pa or more.

### Self-Emptying vs. Non-Self-Emptying

A self-emptying base automatically transfers debris from the robot’s bin into a larger bag inside the dock. This means you only need to empty the base every 30–60 days, versus after every cleaning cycle. It’s a significant convenience upgrade and worth the extra $100–$200 for most users.

### Mopping Capabilities

Most robot vacuums offer mopping, but the quality varies dramatically:
- **Passive mopping:** A wet pad dragged behind the robot (ineffective for stains)
- **Vibrating pads:** Agitate the floor for better cleaning (e.g., Eufy X10 Pro)
- **Self-washing/refilling:** The robot returns to the dock to wash the pad and refill water (e.g., Roborock S8 Pro Ultra)

For serious mopping, invest in a model with active pad agitation. If you only need occasional damp cleaning, a basic mopping system will suffice.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Vacuum?

Robot vacuums are ideal for:
- **Busy professionals** who want clean floors without manual effort
- **Pet owners** who need daily hair removal
- **Families with children** who track in debris
- **Elderly or mobility-impaired individuals** who find vacuuming difficult
- **Tech enthusiasts** who love smart home automation

They are less suitable for:
- **Homes with very high-pile or shag carpets** (most robots struggle)
- **Multi-level homes without ramps** (you’ll need to move the dock)
- **People who prefer deep, manual cleaning** (robots are maintenance, not replacements for occasional deep cleans)

### Budget Considerations

| Budget Range | What You Get | Recommended Model |

### Ongoing Costs

Factor in the cost of replacement parts:
- **Filters:** $15–$30 every 3–6 months
- **Side brushes:** $10–$20 every 6–12 months
- **Main brushes:** $20–$40 every 6–12 months
- **Self-emptying bags:** $5–$10 per bag (last 30–60 days)

Over three years, expect to spend $150–$300 on consumables, depending on usage.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setting Up Your Robot Vacuum

1. **Unbox and charge:** Most robots take 3–4 hours for a full charge. Place the dock against a wall with at least 3 feet of clearance on each side.
2. **Download the app:** All major brands require a companion app for setup. Create an account and connect via Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz is more reliable than 5 GHz).
3. **Run the mapping cycle:** The first cleaning will be slower as the robot learns your home’s layout. Allow it to complete without interruption.
4. **Set no-go zones:** Use the app to mark areas you want the robot to avoid (e.g., pet bowls, children’s play areas).
5. **Schedule cleaning:** Most apps allow you to set daily or custom schedules.

### Smart Home Integration

- **Amazon Alexa:** “Alexa, ask [robot name] to clean the living room.”
- **Google Assistant:** “Hey Google, start vacuuming.”
- **Apple HomeKit:** Only iRobot models offer native Siri support, but you can use Homebridge for third-party integration.
- **IFTTT:** Create custom automations, like starting a cleaning when your alarm goes off.

### Pro Tips for Best Performance

- **Declutter floors:** Remove small objects like cables, socks, and toys before each cleaning.
- **Use virtual boundaries:** Most apps let you create invisible walls to block off areas.
- **Maintain your robot:** Empty the bin, clean brushes, and replace filters regularly.
- **Consider multiple floors:** If you have a multi-story home, buy a second robot or be prepared to move the dock.
