### Suction Power

Measured in Pascals (Pa), suction power determines how effectively a robot vacuum lifts debris from carpets and hard floors. Entry-level models offer around 2,000Pa, midrange models range from 4,000-6,000Pa, and premium models exceed 8,000Pa. For homes with carpets and pets, aim for at least 5,000Pa.

### Navigation Technology

LiDAR navigation uses lasers to map rooms with high precision, while camera-based systems rely on visual recognition. LiDAR generally performs better in low-light conditions and creates more accurate maps. Some premium models combine both for optimal results.

### Self-Emptying Base Stations

A self-emptying base automatically transfers debris from the robot's dustbin to a larger bag or container, reducing how often you need to empty it manually. Bases typically hold 30-60 days of debris. Some premium bases also wash and dry mop pads and refill water tanks.

### Mopping Capabilities

If you have hard floors, consider a robot vacuum with mopping functionality. Options range from basic drag mops to vibrating pads to spinning mops. Spinning mops with downward pressure provide the best cleaning, but vibrating mops are effective for light maintenance.

### Obstacle Avoidance

Advanced obstacle avoidance uses sensors and AI to identify and navigate around cables, shoes, pet waste, and furniture. This feature is crucial for homes with pets or cluttered spaces.

### Smart Home Integration

Most modern robot vacuums work with Alexa and Google Assistant. Apple HomeKit support is less common but growing. Matter compatibility ensures your device works with future smart home platforms.

### Battery Life and Runtime

Runtime varies from 60 to 180 minutes. Larger homes require longer runtimes or a robot that can recharge and resume cleaning. Most premium models offer 120-180 minutes of runtime.

## Installation and Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum cleaner is straightforward, typically taking 15-30 minutes.

### Unboxing and Physical Setup

1. **Unbox and charge:** Remove the robot and base station from packaging. Place the base station against a wall on a level surface with access to a power outlet. Charge the robot fully before first use (usually 3-4 hours).

2. **Install brushes and filters:** Attach side brushes and ensure the dustbin and filter are properly seated. If your model includes a mop pad, attach it according to the manual.

3. **Position the base station:** Ensure at least 3 feet of clearance on both sides and 5 feet in front. Avoid direct sunlight, which can interfere with camera-based navigation.

### App Setup and Wi-Fi Connection

1. **Download the app:** Install the manufacturer's app (e.g., RoboClean, EcoVacs Home, iRobot Home) from the App Store or Google Play.

2. **Create an account:** Register with your email and create a secure password.

3. **Connect to Wi-Fi:** Ensure your phone is connected to a 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network (most robot vacuums don't support 5GHz). Follow the in-app instructions to connect the robot.

4. **Run initial mapping:** Allow the robot to perform a mapping run. This creates a floor plan you can customize with no-go zones and room labels.

### Smart Home Integration

**Amazon Alexa:** Enable the manufacturer's skill in the Alexa app. You can then use voice commands like "Alexa, ask RoboClean to start cleaning the kitchen."

**Google Assistant:** Link your account in the Google Home app. Use commands like "Hey Google, tell EcoVacs to clean the living room."

**Apple HomeKit/Siri:** For compatible models, add the robot to the Home app. Create scenes and automations, such as starting a cleaning cycle when you leave home.

**Matter:** If your robot supports Matter, you can add it to any Matter-compatible ecosystem, including Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, and Samsung SmartThings.

**Automations:** Create routines that trigger cleaning based on your schedule. For example, set the robot to clean the kitchen every night at 10 PM or start cleaning when your smart lock detects you've left for work.
