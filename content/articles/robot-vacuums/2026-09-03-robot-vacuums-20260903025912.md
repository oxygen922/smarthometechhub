## Key Features to Consider

When shopping for a robot vacuum in 2026, you cannot simply look at suction power. The "intelligence" of the machine is now the primary differentiator. Here is a breakdown of the critical specs to analyze.

### Navigation and Mapping
This is the brain of the operation. The two main types are:
- **Laser (LIDAR):** Uses a spinning laser to map the room. Works in the dark and is generally more accurate for layout.
- **Camera (VSLAM):** Uses visual cues to navigate. Often cheaper but can struggle in low light.

### Obstacle Avoidance
This is the "AI" part. Does it avoid cables, pet waste, and shoes, or does it plow through them? Look for models with "Reactive AI" or "3D Structured Light" for the best results.

### Dock Functionality (The Base Station)
This is the biggest trend in 2026. Options range from:
1.  **Basic Charging Dock:** You empty the bin yourself.
2.  **Self-Emptying Dock:** The robot empties its bin into a larger bag in the dock (lasts up to 60 days).
3.  **Ultra Dock (All-in-One):** Empties the bin, washes the mop, dries the mop, and refills the water tank. This is the premium tier.

### Mopping Capability
Many models are now "hybrids." They can vacuum and then swap the bin for a water tank with a mop pad. Higher-end models have rotating mop pads that scrub, while cheaper ones just drag a wet cloth.

### Suction Power
Measured in Pascals (Pa). While higher is better, 4,000Pa is generally sufficient for most homes. Anything above 5,000Pa is for deep cleaning carpets or heavy pet hair.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum is generally a 15-minute process, but integration is where the magic happens.

### Initial Setup
1.  **Place the Dock:** Ensure it has at least 1.5 feet of clearance on either side and 3 feet in front for the robot to dock successfully.
2.  **Connect to App:** Download the manufacturer's app (Roborock, Dreame, Eufy). They all support 2.4GHz Wi-Fi. *Note: If you have a mesh network, ensure the 2.4GHz and 5GHz bands are split, or the robot may fail to connect.*
3.  **First Mapping Run:** Do not put obstacles in the way. Let the robot do a "clean" run without the mop attached to map the entire house. This takes about 30 minutes for a standard 2-bedroom apartment.

### Smart Home Ecosystem Integration (The 2026 Standard)

All three of our top picks support Alexa, Google Home, and Apple Home (via Matter or direct integration). Here are the best automations to set up:

- **Alexa/Google:** "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen." This is the most basic integration.
- **The "Away" Automation (The Pro Move):** Set up a routine in Google Home or Apple HomeKit: *When the last person leaves the house (based on phone location), trigger the robot to start cleaning.* This ensures the floors are clean when you return without the noise bothering you while you are home.
- **The "Dinner" Routine:** Link the vacuum to your smart speaker. When you say "Dinner is ready," the vacuum pauses and returns to the dock, and the lights dim. This is a great party trick but also genuinely useful to get the vacuum out of the way.
- **Matter Support:** The Eufy S3 Pro and Roborock now support Matter, which means they can be controlled natively in Apple Home without a third-party bridge. This is a massive improvement in reliability for iOS users.

**Troubleshooting Tip:** If the robot loses connection, do not reset it immediately. Most 2026 models have a "Local" mode that operates on Bluetooth when Wi-Fi is down, but you lose remote control. A quick router reboot usually solves the issue.
