## Key Features to Consider

When shopping for a robot vacuum in 2026, understanding the key features can make or break your experience. Here's what to look for:

### Suction Power
Measured in Pascals (Pa), suction power determines how well the vacuum picks up debris. For hard floors, 1,500-2,500Pa is sufficient. For carpets, look for 4,000Pa or more. Higher suction often means shorter battery life, so balance is key.

### Navigation Technology
- **LiDAR:** Uses lasers to map rooms in 3D. Highly accurate, works in darkness, and creates persistent maps. Found in premium models like the RoboClean Pro X7 and DEEBOT T30 Omni.
- **Camera-based:** Uses visual sensors to identify obstacles. Good for avoiding small objects but can struggle in low light. Used in the Roomba j9+.
- **Gyroscope-based:** Basic navigation that follows a random pattern. Cheaper but less efficient.

### Smart Home Integration
Look for **Matter protocol** support for universal compatibility. Most vacuums work with Alexa and Google Assistant, but Apple HomeKit support is less common. Real-time control infrastructure, like that inspired by open-source projects, ensures low-latency commands.

### Self-Emptying and Self-Cleaning
Self-emptying bases automatically transfer debris from the vacuum to a larger bin, reducing maintenance. Some models, like the DEEBOT T30 Omni, also wash and dry mop pads. This is a major convenience but adds to the cost.

### Mopping Capabilities
Many robot vacuums now include mopping. Look for models with oscillating mop pads (like the DEEBOT T30 Omni) that apply pressure, and those that lift the mop on carpets (like the RoboClean Pro X7).

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setting Up Your Robot Vacuum

1. **Unboxing and Charging:** Place the vacuum on its charging dock and let it charge fully (usually 3-4 hours). Ensure the dock is against a wall with clear space on either side.

2. **App Installation:** Download the manufacturer's app and create an account. Most apps guide you through Wi-Fi setup, which typically involves pressing a button on the vacuum and connecting via Bluetooth first.

3. **Mapping Your Home:** For LiDAR-based models, the first cleaning run will create a map. For camera-based models, the vacuum learns over time. Let the vacuum complete a full cleaning cycle to establish its map.

4. **Customizing Zones:** Use the app to set no-go zones (areas the vacuum should avoid), clean zones (specific rooms to focus on), and schedules. For multi-level homes, some models save separate maps for each floor.

### Smart Home Integration

- **Voice Assistants:** Connect to Alexa, Google Assistant, or Siri Shortcuts for voice commands. For example, "Alexa, tell RoboClean to clean the kitchen."
- **Automations:** Use IFTTT or the app's built-in automation to trigger cleaning based on time, location, or other smart devices. For instance, set the vacuum to start cleaning when your smart lock detects you've left.
- **Real-Time Control:** Vacuums with low-latency infrastructure (like the RoboClean Pro X7) allow you to control them from anywhere with near-instant response. This is great for spot cleaning or directing the vacuum to a specific area.

### Troubleshooting Tips

- **Wi-Fi Issues:** Ensure your router is on the 2.4GHz band (most vacuums don't support 5GHz). Place the dock within 30 feet of your router.
- **Mapping Errors:** If the vacuum gets stuck or loses its map, reset the map in the app and run a new cleaning cycle.
- **Battery Drain:** If battery life decreases, check for software updates. Some models have power-saving modes.
