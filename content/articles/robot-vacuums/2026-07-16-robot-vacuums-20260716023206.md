## Key Features to Consider (with Comparison Table)

When choosing a robot vacuum, understanding the following features is crucial:

### Navigation & Mapping
- **LiDAR:** Uses lasers for precise mapping, works in darkness.  
- **Camera-based (vSLAM):** Uses visual landmarks, can struggle in low light.  
- **Hybrid (LiDAR + Camera):** Best for obstacle avoidance and dark rooms.

### Suction Power
Measured in Pascals (Pa). For homes with mostly hard floors, 2,500-4,000 Pa is adequate. For thick carpets, look for 5,000+ Pa.

### Mopping Capabilities
- **Basic pad:** Drags a damp cloth.  
- **Rotating pad:** Applies pressure, better for stains.  
- **Self-cleaning dock:** Washes and dries mop pads automatically.

### Auto-Empty Dock
- **Bag-based:** Less mess, but requires bag replacements.  
- **Bagless (cyclonic):** More sustainable, but can be messy to empty.  
- **Self-washing:** For mopping models, includes water tank and drying.

### Smart Home Integration
- **Matter Protocol:** Future-proof, works across ecosystems.  
- **Voice Assistants:** Alexa, Google Assistant, Siri.  
- **Routines:** Trigger cleaning based on door lock, occupancy, or time.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setting Up Your Robot Vacuum

1. **Unboxing and Placement:** Choose a central location for the docking station near a power outlet and with at least 1.5 feet of clearance on each side. Avoid placing it under furniture or in direct sunlight.

2. **App Setup:** Download the manufacturer’s app (e.g., RoboClean Home, EcoVacs Home, iRobot Home). Create an account and connect the vacuum to your Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz band is more reliable for most models).

3. **Mapping Your Home:** The first cleaning cycle will be a “mapping run.” Let the robot explore freely. For LiDAR models, this takes about 10-20 minutes per 1,000 sq. ft. After mapping, you can label rooms (e.g., “Kitchen,” “Living Room”) and set no-go zones.

4. **Scheduling:** Use the app to set cleaning times. For example, schedule the vacuum to clean the kitchen daily at 10 AM and the living room every other day. Many apps support “zone cleaning” for targeted areas.

### Smart Home Integration Tips

- **Matter Protocol:** If your vacuum supports Matter, you can add it to Apple Home, Google Home, or Amazon Alexa directly without using the manufacturer’s app for basic commands. This simplifies automation.

- **Voice Commands:** Use phrases like “Alexa, tell RoboClean to clean the kitchen” or “Hey Google, start vacuuming the living room.”

- **Routines with Other Devices:**  
  - **Security:** Trigger cleaning when your smart lock detects “Away” mode.  
  - **Lighting:** Have the vacuum start when you turn off the lights at night.  
  - **Climate:** If your thermostat shows you’re away, the vacuum can start cleaning to save battery.

- **IFTTT Applets:** For advanced users, create custom triggers like “If the weather forecast says rain, delay mopping.”

### Troubleshooting Common Issues

- **Wi-Fi Connectivity:** Ensure your router is on a 2.4 GHz band (some vacuums don’t support 5 GHz). Consider a mesh network for large homes.

- **Obstacle Avoidance Failures:** Update the vacuum’s firmware regularly. For camera-based models, ensure adequate lighting.

- **Battery Drain:** If the vacuum struggles to complete a cleaning cycle, check for high-pile carpets that increase suction load. You may need to schedule shorter, more frequent cleans.
