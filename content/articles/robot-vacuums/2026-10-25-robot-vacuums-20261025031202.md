## Key Features to Consider

Before you buy, here's what actually separates a great robot mop from a frustrating one.

### Mopping Mechanism
There are three main types: **rotating dual pads** (scrubbing action, great on grime), **vibrating flat pads** (fast, good on light messes), and **self-cleaning rollers** (continuous fresh water, minimal streaking). Rotating pads generally scrub best; rollers stay cleanest.

### Self-Cleaning and Drying Dock
A dock that washes pads with hot water and dries them is a game-changer. Without drying, pads develop odor within days. Look for heated drying and auto-detergent dispensing.

### Carpet Detection and Pad Lifting
If you have rugs, this is non-negotiable. The mop must lift or retract pads when it detects carpet, or you'll end up with damp, stained rugs.

### Navigation and Obstacle Avoidance
LiDAR maps your home; cameras and AI handle obstacle avoidance. The best 2026 models recognize cables, socks, and pet waste. If you have pets or kids, prioritize strong AI avoidance.

### Water Tank and Dustbin Capacity
Larger tanks mean less babysitting. Self-emptying docks extend dustbin life to 60–75 days.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Mop?

**You'll love one if:** You have mostly hard floors (hardwood, tile, laminate, vinyl), you're tired of weekly mopping, you have pets or kids, or you want a vacuum-mop combo that handles daily maintenance automatically.

**You might skip it if:** Your home is 80%+ carpet (a robot vacuum is the better investment), you have very delicate or unsealed floors, or you enjoy the ritual of manual mopping. Also, if your home is cluttered with cables and small objects, even the best AI avoidance will occasionally struggle—though 2026 models are dramatically better than older ones.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $500:** Entry-level mops exist, but expect basic vibrating pads, no self-cleaning dock, and weaker navigation. Fine for small apartments with light messes.
- **$700–$1,000:** The value sweet spot. You get self-cleaning docks, heated drying, and solid navigation. The Eufy S1 Pro Omni sits here and punches above its weight.
- **$1,200–$1,500:** Flagship territory. You're paying for superior scrubbing, advanced obstacle climbing, the best AI avoidance, and maximum automation. Worth it for larger homes and busy households.

**Pro tip:** Prices on previous-generation flagships drop significantly when new models launch. A 2025 flagship at 30% off is often a smarter buy than a 2026 mid-ranger.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setup on all three models took under 30 minutes. The process is consistent: unbox the dock, place it against a wall with clearance on both sides, fill the clean water tank, plug in, and pair via the app. First mapping runs take 10–20 minutes depending on home size.

**App experience:** All three offer room-by-room scheduling, no-go zones, multi-level mapping, and mop intensity settings. Roborock's app is the most feature-rich but busiest; Eufy's is the most intuitive.

**Voice and platform integration:** All three work with Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant for basic commands ("start cleaning the kitchen"). The Eufy S1 Pro Omni stands out with **Matter support**, meaning it integrates natively with Apple Home, SmartThings, and other Matter-compatible platforms—a big plus for Apple HomeKit users who've been left out of the robot mop party for years.

**Automation ideas:** Schedule daily kitchen mopping after dinner, trigger a whole-home mop when everyone leaves (via geofencing), or run a "pet mess patrol" routine in high-traffic zones. Pair with a smart speaker announcement when the mop finishes.
