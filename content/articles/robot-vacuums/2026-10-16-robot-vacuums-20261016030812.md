## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Mop?

A robot mop is not for everyone. It's an excellent investment if:

- You have hard flooring (hardwood, tile, laminate, vinyl) in most of your home.
- You want to reduce daily or weekly manual mopping.
- You have pets or kids who track in dirt and spills.
- You value smart home integration and scheduling.
- You have a busy lifestyle and don't mind a higher upfront cost for convenience.

You might skip a robot mop if:

- Your home is mostly wall-to-wall carpet (a robot vacuum is a better fit).
- You have very uneven floors or lots of thresholds.
- You enjoy manual cleaning or have a small space where a quick Swiffer is faster.
- Your budget is under $150—cheap robot mops often disappoint.

### Budget Considerations

In 2026, robot mops fall into three tiers:

- **Budget ($200–$400):** Basic vibrating mops with gyroscope navigation. Good for small apartments with light messes. Expect to empty and refill frequently.
- **Mid-Range ($450–$750):** LiDAR navigation, better suction, and app features. The sweet spot for most families.
- **Premium ($800–$1,500):** Self-emptying and self-washing bases, AI obstacle avoidance, rotating mops. Best for large homes and maximum convenience.

Don't forget ongoing costs: replacement mop pads ($15–$30 per set), cleaning solution ($10–$20 per bottle), and dust bags for self-emptying bases ($20–$40 for a multi-pack). Over a year, these can add $100–$200 to your total cost of ownership.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot mop is surprisingly straightforward. Most models require you to:

1. **Unbox and charge:** Place the base station on a level surface near a power outlet and let the robot charge fully (2–4 hours).
2. **Download the app:** Connect the robot to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network. (Note: most robot mops don't support 5GHz, so check your router settings.)
3. **Run a mapping cycle:** Let the robot explore your home to build a floor plan. This takes 20–60 minutes depending on square footage.
4. **Customize:** Set no-go zones, room names, cleaning schedules, and water flow levels per room.

For smart home integration, most 2026 models work with Amazon Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple HomeKit (though HomeKit support is still less common). You can start, stop, and dock the robot with voice commands. Some premium models also support IFTTT and Matter, allowing automation like "start mopping when I leave for work."

A few tips for optimal integration:

- Place the base station where the robot has a clear path—avoid tight corners.
- Use virtual walls or no-go zones to keep the robot out of bathrooms with rugs or pet feeding areas.
- Schedule mopping after vacuuming for best results (many hybrids do both in one pass).
- Update firmware regularly; manufacturers often improve navigation and cleaning algorithms.

If you have a multi-story home, you'll need to carry the robot between floors or buy one per level. Most robot mops can't climb stairs.
