## Key Features to Consider

When shopping for a robot vacuum, understanding the key features can make the difference between a device that collects dust and one that actually saves you time. Here's what you need to know:

### Suction Power
Measured in Pascals (Pa), suction power determines how effectively the vacuum picks up debris. Budget models typically offer 1,500-2,000 Pa, while premium units exceed 5,000 Pa. For most homes, 2,000-3,000 Pa is sufficient for carpets, while 4,000+ Pa is ideal for homes with pets or heavy traffic.

### Navigation Technology
- **LiDAR:** Uses lasers to create precise maps. Best for accuracy and speed.
- **Visual SLAM:** Uses cameras and algorithms. Good but can struggle in low light.
- **Gyroscope:** Basic navigation. Affordable but less reliable.

### Battery Life
Most robot vacuums run for 60-120 minutes on a single charge. Consider your home's square footage—larger homes need longer battery life or fast recharging capabilities.

### Self-Emptying Base
This feature automatically empties the robot's dustbin into a larger container, reducing maintenance to once every few weeks. It's a game-changer for convenience.

### Mopping Functionality
Not all robot vacuums mop, and those that do vary widely. Basic models drag a damp cloth, while premium units use vibration, spinning pads, and automatic pad washing.

### Smart Home Integration
Compatibility with Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple HomeKit allows for voice control and integration with other smart devices. Some models also support IFTTT for advanced automation.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setting Up Your Robot Vacuum

1. **Unboxing and Charging:** Most robots come partially charged. Plug in the charging dock and place it against a wall with at least 3 feet of clearance on each side. Allow the robot to fully charge (typically 3-4 hours).

2. **App Setup:** Download the manufacturer's app and create an account. Follow the on-screen instructions to connect the robot to your Wi-Fi network (2.4 GHz is more reliable than 5 GHz for these devices).

3. **Mapping Run:** The first cleaning session is usually a mapping run. Remove obstacles like loose cables and small toys. Let the robot explore your home—this can take 30-60 minutes depending on size.

4. **Customize Settings:** After mapping, you can set no-go zones, schedule cleanings, and customize room-by-room settings. Most apps allow you to name rooms and set different cleaning modes for each.

### Smart Home Integration Tips

- **Voice Control:** Set up routines like "Alexa, start vacuuming" or "Hey Google, clean the kitchen." Most robots support basic commands without additional setup.

- **Automation:** Use IFTTT or the native app to trigger cleaning based on events. For example, have your robot vacuum start cleaning when your smart lock detects you've left the house.

- **Geofencing:** Many apps support geofencing, which automatically starts cleaning when you leave home and stops when you return. This is a fantastic way to keep floors clean without thinking about it.

- **Multi-Platform Support:** If you use Apple HomeKit, ensure your chosen robot supports it. The iRobot Roomba j9+ Pro is the only one of our top three that offers native HomeKit support.
