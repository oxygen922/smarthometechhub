## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Vacuum?

Robot vacuums are ideal for:

- **Busy professionals** who want daily cleaning without lifting a finger.
- **Pet owners** dealing with constant shedding (look for tangle-free brushes).
- **Allergy sufferers** who need frequent dust and dander removal.
- **Seniors or people with mobility issues** who find traditional vacuums difficult.
- **Smart home enthusiasts** who want voice-controlled, automated cleaning.

They are **less ideal** for:

- Homes with mostly high-pile or shag carpet (robot vacuums struggle here).
- Very cluttered spaces with many cables and small objects.
- Anyone expecting a robot to replace a full-size vacuum for deep carpet cleaning.

### Budget Considerations

**Under $400:** You'll get decent suction, basic navigation, and sometimes a self-empty base. Best for apartments, hard floors, and pet hair. Expect to empty the bin manually on cheaper models.

**$400–$800:** The sweet spot. You get reliable LiDAR mapping, self-emptying, and decent mopping. Ideal for most US homes with a mix of carpet and hard floors.

**$800–$1,500+:** Premium features: AI obstacle avoidance, rotating mops, self-cleaning and drying mop pads, Matter support, and the strongest suction. Worth it if you want maximum convenience and have a large home.

**Hidden costs to consider:** Replacement brushes ($15–$30), filters ($10–$20), mop pads ($10–$25), and dust bags ($5–$10 each). Budget roughly $80–$120 per year in consumables.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot vacuum takes 15–30 minutes. Here's what to expect:

1. **Unbox and place the base:** Choose a spot with Wi-Fi signal and at least 18 inches of clearance on each side. Avoid direct sunlight, which can confuse camera sensors.
2. **Charge the robot:** Most models arrive partially charged. A full charge takes 3–5 hours.
3. **Download the app:** Create an account, connect to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (most robots don't support 5 GHz), and scan the QR code on the robot.
4. **Run a mapping pass:** Let the robot explore your home. Keep doors open and floors clear for the first run.
5. **Set no-go zones and schedules:** Block off areas with cables, pet bowls, or fragile items. Schedule cleaning when you're away.
6. **Connect to smart home:** Link to Alexa, Google Assistant, or Apple Home. If the model supports Matter, setup is faster and works across ecosystems.

**Integration tips:**
- Use routines like "Goodnight" to trigger kitchen cleaning.
- Pair with smart locks so the robot starts when you leave.
- For multi-floor homes, look for models that store multiple maps.
- If you have a smart speaker, voice commands like "Start vacuuming the living room" work on all three models reviewed here.
