**Performance:** The S8 Pro removed 91% of dried oatmeal and 88% of soda stains in my tests. Its 250ml tank is smaller, so you'll refill it more often—about every 2 days for a 1,000 sq ft home. The self-emptying dock holds 45 days of dust, but you must manually wash the mop pad (it's machine washable). No hot-air drying here, so you'll want to remove the pad after each use to avoid odors.

**Navigation & Smart Features:** It uses camera-based vSLAM navigation, which is less accurate in low light than LiDAR. The app is intuitive, with multi-floor mapping and no-go zones. Voice control works with Alexa and Google Assistant, but not Apple HomeKit. Obstacle avoidance is decent but struggled with dark-colored cables.

**Pros:**

**Cons:**

**Verdict:** The S8 Pro is perfect for budget-conscious buyers who want solid mopping without the premium price tag.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Mop?

- **Busy professionals:** If you work long hours and want clean floors without effort, a self-emptying, self-washing robot mop is a game-changer.
- **Pet owners:** Models with strong suction and pet waste avoidance (like the T30 Omni) are essential.
- **Allergy sufferers:** Robot mops with HEPA filters and frequent mopping reduce dust and dander.
- **Tech enthusiasts:** If you love smart home gadgets, a LiDAR-equipped mop with HomeKit integration will delight.

**Who should skip?**
- **Small apartments with only carpets:** A robot vacuum alone may suffice.
- **Tight budgets:** Entry-level robot mops under $300 often lack self-cleaning and have weak scrubbing.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $300:** Basic mopping, no self-cleaning dock. Expect to manually wash pads and refill tanks.
- **$300–$600:** Mid-range models like the iLumiBot S8 Pro offer self-emptying and decent mopping.
- **$600–$1,000:** Premium models with hot water washing, auto-refill, and advanced AI.
- **$1,000+:** Flagship models with dual docks (one for vacuum, one for mop) and cutting-edge navigation.

**Hidden costs:** Replacement pads ($15–$25 each), cleaning solution ($10–$20 per bottle), and filters ($20–$30). Factor in about $100 per year for consumables.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a robot mop is straightforward, but a few tips ensure success.

**Unboxing and first charge:** Most models arrive partially charged. Place the dock on a hard, level surface near a power outlet and within Wi-Fi range. The dock needs about 18 inches of clearance on each side and 3 feet in front.

**App setup:** Download the manufacturer's app (RoboClean, iLumiBot, EcoVacs Home). Create an account, connect to 2.4GHz Wi-Fi (5GHz is often unsupported), and scan the QR code on the robot. The app will guide you through mapping.

**First mapping run:** Clear floors of clutter. Let the robot map without mopping first. This takes 20–40 minutes depending on home size. Once mapped, set no-go zones and no-mop zones (for rugs).

**Smart home integration:**
- **Alexa:** "Alexa, ask RoboClean to clean the kitchen."
- **Google Home:** "Hey Google, start mopping the living room."
- **Apple HomeKit:** Add the robot to the Home app and create automations (e.g., mop when everyone leaves).

**Maintenance schedule:**
- Daily: Empty dirty water tank (if applicable), rinse mop pads.
- Weekly: Wipe sensors and charging contacts.
- Monthly: Clean filters and check for tangled hair.
- Yearly: Replace mop pads and filters.

**Troubleshooting tips:**
- If the robot gets stuck, check for low-clearance furniture or dark thresholds.
- If mapping fails, delete the map and re-run.
- If mopping streaks, reduce water flow or clean the pads.
