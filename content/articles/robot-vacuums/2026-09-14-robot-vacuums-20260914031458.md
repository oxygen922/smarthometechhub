## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Vacuum Cleaner?

**Busy Professionals:** If you work long hours and don't want to spend weekends cleaning, a robot vacuum cleaner with a self-emptying dock is a game-changer. You'll come home to clean floors without lifting a finger.

**Pet Owners:** Homes with shedding dogs or cats benefit enormously from daily automated vacuuming. Look for models with high suction (6,000Pa+) and pet-specific modes that reduce noise.

**Allergy Sufferers:** Robot vacuums with HEPA filtration capture fine dust and allergens that manual sweeping misses. Run them daily to maintain cleaner air.

**Tech Enthusiasts:** If you're building a smart home, choose a robot vacuum cleaner with Matter support for seamless integration with your existing ecosystem.

**Who Should Skip:** If your home is extremely cluttered, has multiple floor levels with no safe drop-off protection, or is primarily high-pile shag carpet, a robot vacuum may struggle. Consider a stick vacuum instead.

### Budget Considerations

**Under $300:** Entry-level robot vacuums offer basic navigation (bump-and-roll) and modest suction. They're best for small apartments with hard floors. Expect to empty the dustbin after every run.

**$300-$700:** Mid-range models add LiDAR navigation, app control, and often self-emptying docks. This is the sweet spot for most households.

**$700-$1,000:** You'll get strong suction, reliable obstacle avoidance, and decent mopping. Some models include mop pad washing.

**$1,000+:** Premium robot vacuums deliver the full package: powerful suction, AI navigation, self-emptying, self-washing mops, and top-tier smart home integration. Best for large homes and maximum convenience.

**Ongoing Costs:** Factor in replacement bags ($15-$25 for a 3-pack), filters ($20-$30 annually), and mop pads ($15-$25 per set). Premium models cost more to maintain but save significant time.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Unboxing and Setup

Setting up a modern robot vacuum cleaner takes 15-30 minutes. Here's the typical process:

1. **Place the dock:** Choose a location with Wi-Fi signal and wall clearance (at least 3 feet on each side). Avoid direct sunlight, which can interfere with sensors.
2. **Power on and charge:** Most robots arrive partially charged. Allow 3-4 hours for a full charge before first use.
3. **Download the app:** Create an account and connect the robot to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network (most models don't support 5GHz).
4. **Run a mapping cycle:** Let the robot explore your home to build a floor plan. This takes 10-20 minutes depending on home size.
5. **Customize settings:** Set no-go zones, room names, cleaning schedules, and suction/mop preferences.

### Smart Home Integration Tips

**Alexa:** "Alexa, ask RoboClean to clean the kitchen." Voice commands work well for starting, stopping, and docking.

**Google Home:** Add the robot to your Google Home app for routine integration. You can trigger cleaning when you leave the house using presence detection.

**Apple HomeKit:** Only premium models support HomeKit. You can add the robot to scenes (e.g., "Goodbye" triggers cleaning).

**Matter:** The newest protocol enables cross-platform compatibility. If you're buying in 2026, prioritize Matter support for future-proofing.

**IFTTT:** Advanced users can create automations like "If I leave work, start vacuuming" or "If front door unlocks, stop cleaning."

**Pro Tip:** Create a "clean before guests arrive" routine that triggers 30 minutes before your calendar event. Your floors will be spotless without you thinking about it.
