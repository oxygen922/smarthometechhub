## Key Features to Consider

Before you buy a robot mop, here's what actually matters — and what you can safely ignore.

### Mopping Technology
There are three main types: **rotating/spinning pads** (best for scrubbing), **vibrating sonic plates** (best for delicate floors), and **static microfiber pads** (budget-friendly but less effective). Rotating pads generally deliver the deepest clean.

### Self-Cleaning and Self-Emptying Docks
A self-washing dock is a game-changer for convenience. Hot-water washing is superior to cold rinses because it breaks down oils and bacteria. Self-emptying bins extend hands-off time to 45–60 days.

### Navigation
LiDAR and 3D structured-light sensors are the gold standard. They enable accurate mapping, no-go zones, and reliable obstacle avoidance. Gyroscope-only navigation is fine for small spaces but won't cut it in larger homes.

### Water Tank Capacity and Refill Frequency
Larger tanks (300ml+) mean fewer refills. Docked models with auto-refill from a clean water reservoir can go weeks without attention.

### Carpet Detection and Mop Lifting
If you have rugs or carpet, mop lifting is essential. Look for at least 5mm of lift to keep carpets dry.

### App and Voice Control
Alexa and Google Assistant support is now standard, but check for advanced features like multi-floor mapping, scheduling, and zone cleaning.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Mop?

**Busy professionals and families:** If your schedule leaves little room for daily floor care, a self-cleaning robot mop like the RoboClean Pro M9 Ultra pays for itself in time saved.

**Hardwood and laminate owners:** The AquaJet S7 MaxV is the safer choice for delicate flooring, thanks to its controlled water flow and sonic cleaning.

**Renters and small-apartment dwellers:** The BudgetBot Mop Mini offers real value without the commitment of a large dock or a high price tag.

**Pet owners:** Look for models with strong suction, mop lifting, and obstacle avoidance to handle fur and occasional accidents.

**People with mostly carpet:** Honestly, a robot mop may not be your best first purchase — consider a robot vacuum first, or a hybrid model with strong carpet performance.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $400:** Expect basic mopping, manual pad washing, and simpler navigation.
- **$400–$800:** Mid-range models add mapping, carpet detection, and better scrubbing.
- **$800–$1,200:** Premium models include self-cleaning docks, hot-water washing, and self-emptying.

Don't forget **ongoing costs**: mop pads ($15–$30 per set), cleaning solution ($10–$20 per bottle), and dust bags ($10–$15 each). Budget roughly $80–$150 per year for consumables on a premium model.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a modern robot mop is surprisingly painless. Here's what to expect:

1. **Unbox and place the dock** near a power outlet with Wi-Fi coverage. Premium docks need about 18 inches of clearance on each side.
2. **Fill the clean water tank** and install the mop pads.
3. **Download the companion app** and connect via 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (most models don't support 5 GHz during setup).
4. **Run a mapping pass** — most LiDAR models complete this in 10–20 minutes.
5. **Set no-go zones, schedules, and room labels** in the app.

**Smart home integration** is where these devices shine. All three models support voice commands through Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant — "Alexa, ask RoboClean to mop the kitchen" actually works. The premium models also integrate with IFTTT and Apple Home via Matter support, letting you trigger cleaning routines when you leave the house or disarm your security system.

One tip: place your dock on a hard, level surface. Carpet under the dock can cause docking failures and water sensor errors.
