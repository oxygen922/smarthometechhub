## Key Features to Consider

When shopping for a robot mop in 2026, the hardware is only half the battle. Here is what you need to look for:

### 1. Mopping Mechanism
- **Vibrating/Sonic Pads:** These scrub back and forth rapidly (like the Roborock). They are excellent for hard floors and are generally quieter.
- **Spinning Pads:** These rotate at high speeds (like Narwal and Dreame). They are generally better at lifting stubborn, sticky grime.
- **Pressure:** Look for a downward force of at least 10N for effective scrubbing.

### 2. The Dock (The "Brains" of the Operation)
- **Self-Washing:** The dock must wash the mop pads automatically. Look for hot water washing (58°C or higher) to dissolve grease.
- **Self-Drying:** A hot air dryer (40°C+) is essential to prevent bacteria and mold growth on the pads.
- **Auto-Fill/Drain:** Premium models connect to your plumbing or have large water tanks. Check the capacity; a 4.5L tank is standard for a week of cleaning.

### 3. Obstacle Avoidance
- **LiDAR vs. Camera:** LiDAR is excellent for mapping in the dark, but a camera (RGB or 3D) is necessary for recognizing small objects like socks or cables. In 2026, the best units use a combination of both.

### 4. The "FCC Factor"
Due to recent FCC restrictions on foreign-made mobile robots, many 2026 models have shifted production or altered their wireless modules to comply. This has led to a slight price increase on "US-legal" units, but it also means better security protocols for data privacy. Ensure the unit you buy is explicitly labeled as "FCC Compliant" for the US market to avoid connectivity issues.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation
Gone are the days of complicated wiring. In 2026, installation is mostly "plug-and-play." However, the docks are large—roughly the size of a small trash can.

1.  **Placement:** Choose a spot against a wall with a clear 1.5ft clearance on either side for the robot to enter and exit.
2.  **Water Setup:** Most units have a manual water tank you fill at the sink. *Pro Tip:* Look for models with a "Water Inlet" port if you want to connect a water line (available for the Roborock and Narwal via a separate kit).
3.  **Initial Mapping:** Allow the robot to run a full "Quick Map" cycle. This takes about 20 minutes. Do not interfere with it during this time, as it is learning the layout.

### Smart Home Integration
All three recommended models support the "Big Three" ecosystems:
- **Amazon Alexa:** You can say, "Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the kitchen." The devices link via the Alexa app and allow for voice-activated zone cleaning.
- **Google Home:** Integration is seamless for the Dreame and Roborock. You can include the robot in "Routines" (e.g., "Goodbye" triggers the robot to start cleaning).
- **Apple HomeKit:** This is where it gets tricky. *Note:* The Dreame X40 Ultra supports HomeKit, but the Roborock and Narwal do not natively. You will need a Homebridge hub to bridge the gap if you are an Apple household.
