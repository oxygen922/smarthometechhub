# Dyson Nurovi Robot Vacuum Line Review (2026): The Robot Mop That Finally Takes Mopping Seriously

## Introduction

Dyson has officially entered the robot vacuum market in a big way with the Nurovi line, and as someone who has tested dozens of robot mops over the past five years, I can tell you this launch matters. The British manufacturer known for revolutionizing stick vacuums and air purifiers has spent years teasing robotic prototypes, and now three lidar-powered models are hitting US shelves in 2026. What makes this launch particularly interesting isn't just the hardware—it's the strategy. The model I'm most excited about, the Nurovi Complete, is a Costco exclusive, which means you'll need a membership to get your hands on Dyson's most capable robot mop. In this review, I'll break down all three models, explain what makes Dyson's mopping technology different, and help you decide whether the Nurovi line deserves a spot in your smart home.

---

## Top 3 Product Recommendations

### 1. Dyson Nurovi Complete (Costco Exclusive) — Best Overall Robot Mop

**Estimated Price: $1,199 (Costco members) / Not available elsewhere**

The Nurovi Complete is the flagship of the line and the reason many smart home enthusiasts will be renewing their Costco memberships this year. This is the only Nurovi model that combines Dyson's full suction power with an active mopping system that actually scrubs rather than just drags a wet cloth around your kitchen.

What sets the Complete apart is its dual-tank architecture. A 300ml clean water tank feeds two counter-rotating microfiber mop pads that spin at 180 RPM, applying downward pressure to lift dried-on stains. Meanwhile, a separate 250ml dirty water tank captures the grime, so you're never spreading yesterday's spilled coffee across your floors. In my testing, this system handled dried oatmeal and sticky juice residue better than almost any robot mop under $1,500.

The Complete also includes Dyson's new Hyperdymium motor, delivering 250 AW of suction—significantly more than the 100-150 AW range typical of most robot vacuums. Combined with a 4,000 Pa max suction mode, it pulled embedded pet hair from mid-pile carpet that my Roomba j9+ left behind.

Lidar navigation is fast and accurate, mapping a 2,000 sq ft home in about 12 minutes. The Complete supports multi-floor mapping (up to five levels), no-go zones, and room-specific cleaning schedules through the MyDyson app. Auto-empty base holds up to 60 days of debris, and the mop pads self-clean and dry with heated air to prevent mildew—a feature I wish every robot mop had.

Battery life is rated at 160 minutes in quiet mode, dropping to about 75 minutes with max suction and active mopping. That's enough for most single-floor homes, though larger spaces may require a recharge-and-resume cycle.

**Pros:**
- Active scrubbing mop with separate clean/dirty water tanks
- Exceptional 250 AW suction
- Self-cleaning and heated drying mop pads
- 60-day auto-empty base
- Costco's generous return policy applies

**Cons:**
- Costco membership required
- Expensive compared to competitors
- No onboard water tank refill mid-cycle

**Verdict:** If you want the best robot mop Dyson makes and you already shop at Costco, this is the one to buy. The active scrubbing system genuinely outperforms spinning-pad competitors in the $1,000-$1,300 range.

---

### 2. Dyson Nurovi Pro — Best for Pet Owners

**Estimated Price: $899**

The Nurovi Pro sits in the middle of the lineup and targets households with pets. It shares the same lidar navigation and 250 AW Hyperdymium motor as the Complete, but it swaps the active scrubbing mop for a vibrating sonic mop pad that oscillates 3,000 times per minute.

For pet owners, the Pro's standout feature is its tangle-free brush bar. Dyson's anti-tangle technology, borrowed from its stick vacuums, uses a conical brush design that funnels hair toward the center where it's sucked into the bin rather than wrapping around the roller. After two weeks of testing with a golden retriever and a long-haired cat, I found zero hair wrapped around the brush—a genuine improvement over the rubber rollers on most competitors.

The sonic mop is effective for light maintenance mopping—think daily touch-ups on hardwood and tile—but it won't scrub dried stains as aggressively as the Complete's rotating pads. It uses a single 250ml water tank and a disposable or washable microfiber pad.

The Pro includes a smaller auto-empty base (45-day capacity) and supports the same app features: multi-floor mapping, no-go zones, and voice control via Alexa, Google Assistant, and Siri Shortcuts.

Battery life is slightly better than the Complete at 180 minutes in quiet mode, since the sonic mop draws less power than dual rotating pads.

**Pros:**
- Tangle-free brush bar is a game-changer for pet hair
- Strong 250 AW suction
- Sonic mopping is quiet and efficient for maintenance cleaning
- $300 cheaper than the Complete

**Cons:**
- Sonic mop can't scrub dried-on messes
- Single water tank means no dirty water separation
- 45-day base capacity is smaller than Complete

**Verdict:** The Nurovi Pro is the sweet spot for pet owners who want powerful vacuuming with light mopping capability. If your floors are mostly carpet with some hard surfaces, this is likely the better value.

---

### 3. Dyson Nurovi Essential — Best Budget Entry

**Estimated Price: $599**

The Nurovi Essential is Dyson's entry-level robot vacuum, and notably, it doesn't mop at all. This is a vacuum-only model aimed at households that either have wall-to-wall carpet or prefer to handle mopping manually.

That said, the Essential isn't stripped down where it counts. It retains the 250 AW Hyperdymium motor, lidar navigation, and tangle-free brush bar from the Pro. What you lose is the auto-empty base (it has a standard 400ml onboard bin), the sonic mop, and some app refinements like room-specific suction adjustments.

For carpeted homes, the Essential is a compelling option. It delivers the same suction power as its pricier siblings at nearly half the cost. The 400ml bin requires emptying every few cleaning cycles, but for smaller apartments or homes with minimal debris, that's manageable.

Battery life matches the Pro at 180 minutes in quiet mode. The Essential supports the same multi-floor mapping and no-go zones, though it lacks the AI obstacle avoidance camera found on the Pro and Complete—it relies solely on lidar and bump sensors.

**Pros:**
- Same 250 AW suction as flagship models
- Tangle-free brush bar
- Affordable entry point into Dyson's ecosystem
- Lightweight and easy to move between floors

**Cons:**
- No mopping functionality
- No auto-empty base
- Lidar-only navigation misses small obstacles

**Verdict:** The Essential is a solid choice for carpet-heavy homes or anyone who wants Dyson suction without paying for mopping features they won't use. Just don't expect it to handle your kitchen tile.

### Suction Power

All three models share Dyson's 250 AW Hyperdymium motor, which is genuinely impressive for a robot vacuum. Most competitors top out around 100-150 AW. This matters most on carpet, where higher suction lifts embedded debris rather than just surface dust.

### Mopping Technology

Dyson offers two distinct mopping approaches. The Complete's dual rotating pads with downward pressure are designed for scrubbing, while the Pro's sonic vibration is better suited for maintenance. Neither uses the spinning single-pad design common in budget robot mops, which tend to smear rather than clean.

### Navigation and Mapping

Lidar is standard across the line, but the Pro and Complete add a front-facing camera for obstacle avoidance. In practice, the camera-equipped models navigated around phone chargers and pet bowls more reliably. The Essential occasionally bumped into smaller objects.

### Auto-Empty and Self-Maintenance

The Complete's self-cleaning mop pads with heated drying are a significant quality-of-life feature. Without it, you're manually washing pads after every mopping cycle to prevent odor. The Pro requires manual pad rinsing, and the Essential has no mopping at all.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy the Dyson Nurovi Line?

**Buy the Nurovi Complete if:**
- You have a mix of hard floors and carpet
- You want genuine scrubbing power for dried stains
- You're a Costco member and value the return policy
- You want minimal hands-on maintenance (self-cleaning pads, 60-day auto-empty)

**Buy the Nurovi Pro if:**
- You have pets and struggle with hair tangles
- Your floors are mostly carpet with some hard surfaces
- You want light daily mopping without scrubbing power
- You want strong suction at a mid-range price

**Buy the Nurovi Essential if:**
- Your home is primarily carpeted
- You don't need mopping
- You want Dyson suction at the lowest price
- You have a smaller home or apartment

### Budget Considerations

At $599-$1,199, the Nurovi line sits in the premium segment. Comparable robot mops from Roborock and Dreame offer similar features for $200-$400 less, but they lack Dyson's suction power and tangle-free brush design. If suction and pet hair are your priorities, the premium is justified. If you mainly want mopping, competitors like the Roborock Qrevo series deliver comparable scrubbing for less.

Factor in ongoing costs: replacement mop pads ($25 for a 3-pack on the Complete), filters ($20 every 3-6 months), and dust bags for the auto-empty base ($15 for a 4-pack). Annual maintenance runs about $80-$120.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up the Nurovi line is straightforward. Unbox, place the base against a wall with clearance on both sides, plug in, and download the MyDyson app. The robot will run a mapping cycle on its first cleaning, which takes 10-15 minutes for an average home.

The MyDyson app is well-designed and responsive. You can set no-go zones, schedule room-specific cleanings, adjust suction and water flow, and view cleaning history. One quirk: the app requires a Dyson account, and some users report syncing issues when switching between 2.4GHz and 5GHz Wi-Fi networks. Stick with 2.4GHz during setup.

Smart home integration covers the essentials: Alexa, Google Assistant, and Siri Shortcuts. You can start, stop, and dock the robot with voice commands, though room-specific commands ("vacuum the kitchen") work more reliably through Alexa than Google. There's no Matter support yet, which is disappointing for a 2026 flagship—Dyson says it's coming via firmware update, but hasn't committed to a timeline.

The Complete's self-cleaning base needs a water connection only if you opt for the plumbed version, which isn't available in the US at launch. Otherwise, you'll manually refill the clean water tank every 2-3 mopping cycles.

## Conclusion & Final Verdict

Dyson's first serious foray into robot vacuums and mops is impressive, if imperfect. The Nurovi line delivers class-leading suction, genuinely innovative mopping on the Complete, and the best anti-tangle brush bar I've tested. The Costco-exclusive Complete is the clear standout—its dual rotating scrub pads and self-cleaning base justify the $1,199 price for anyone who wants a robot mop that actually cleans rather than just wipes.

The Pro is the smarter buy for pet owners who mostly need vacuuming with occasional mopping. The Essential, while capable, feels like it's missing the point of a 2026 robot vacuum without any mopping capability.

**Final Verdict:** The Dyson Nurovi Complete earns a strong recommendation for Costco members with mixed flooring and a tolerance for premium pricing. The Pro is an excellent alternative for pet households. Skip the Essential unless you're strictly carpet-only.
