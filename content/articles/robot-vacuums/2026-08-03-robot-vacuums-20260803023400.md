## Key Features to Consider

Before you buy, here’s what actually matters when comparing robot mops:

| Feature | Why It Matters | Budget ($200-$400) | Mid-Range ($400-$800) | Premium ($800+) |
|---------|----------------|---------------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| **Mopping Type** | Rotating pads scrub; drag pads wipe | Drag pad | Rotating pads | Rotating + sonic vibration |
| **Water Tank** | Larger = more coverage | 200-300ml | 300-400ml | 400ml+ |
| **Self-Cleaning Dock** | Washes mop pads automatically | No | Optional | Yes (hot water) |
| **Obstacle Avoidance** | Camera/LiDAR vs. bump sensors | Bump sensors | LiDAR | LiDAR + camera |
| **Carpet Detection** | Lifts mop to avoid wetting rugs | Basic | Good | Excellent |
| **Suction Power** | Higher = better vacuuming | 2,000-4,000 Pa | 4,000-6,000 Pa | 6,000-12,000 Pa |
| **App Features** | Mapping, zones, scheduling | Basic mapping | Multi-floor maps | Room-specific + custom routines |
| **Voice Control** | Alexa/Google/Matter | Alexa/Google | + Siri Shortcuts | + Full Matter support |

**Mopping Technology Explained**

- **Drag-and-wipe:** A single pad drags across the floor with gravity pressure. Cheap and effective for light cleaning, but struggles with dried-on food.
- **Rotating pads:** Two spinning discs apply downward pressure. Much better for scrubbing. Look for 180+ RPM.
- **Sonic vibration:** The pad vibrates at high frequency to loosen grime. Best for hard floors.

**Dock Types**

- **Standard dock:** Robot returns to charge; you manually empty and clean the mop.
- **Auto-empty dock:** Robot empties its dustbin into a sealed bag (lasts 60 days).
- **Ultra dock:** Auto-empty + mop washing + water refill + hot-air drying. This is the "set and forget" experience.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Mop?

**You should buy one if:**
- You have 500+ sq ft of hard flooring (tile, hardwood, laminate)
- You hate mopping (obviously)
- You have pets or kids who track in dirt daily
- You have a busy schedule and value automation
- You have a smart home and want everything connected

**You should NOT buy one if:**
- Your home is 100% carpet (buy a robot vacuum instead)
- You have very uneven floors or high thresholds between rooms
- You have expensive antique hardwood that requires specialized care
- You’re on a tight budget (a $100 robot mop will disappoint)

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Unboxing & Setup

All three of our top picks took under 20 minutes to set up. Here’s the general process:

1. **Choose a dock location:** The dock needs 1.5-2 feet of clearance on either side and 6 inches in front. It should be near a power outlet and (for ultra docks) a water source if you opt for the optional water refill kit.
2. **Download the app:** Create an account and connect to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network. Note: Most robot vacuums do NOT support 5GHz networks.
3. **Map your home:** The first run is a "mapping pass." Let the robot roam freely without mopping to create a floor plan. This takes 30-60 minutes depending on home size.
4. **Set zones:** Use the app to define no-mop zones (for carpet), no-go zones (for areas with cables), and room labels.

### Smart Home Integration

**Alexa & Google Home:**
- Enable the skill and link your account.
- Use voice commands: *"Alexa, ask Roborock to clean the living room."*
- You can also trigger cleaning with routines: *"Goodnight"* can trigger a kitchen mop.

**Apple HomeKit (via Matter):**
- The Dreame X40 supports Matter, so it appears natively in the Home app.
- Create automations: *"When the last person leaves, start mopping."*

**IFTTT & Advanced Automation:**
- Connect your robot mop to IFTTT for triggers like *"When the temperature drops below 40°F, mop the entryway"* (great for snowy climates).

**Pro Tip:** If you have multiple floors, most premium robots let you save multiple maps. The robot will automatically detect which floor it’s on and load the correct map. This is a must-have feature if you have a multi-story home.
