## Key Features to Consider

Before you drop a grand or more on a robot mower, it's worth understanding what actually matters. Here's a breakdown of the features that separate a great experience from a frustrating one.

### Navigation Technology

This is the single most important factor. There are three main types:

1. **Boundary wire systems** — The traditional approach. You bury a wire around your lawn's perimeter, and the mower follows it. Reliable but labor-intensive to install.
2. **GPS/RTK systems** — Satellite-based navigation that creates a virtual boundary. No wires needed, but requires a clear view of the sky and often a reference station.
3. **Camera/AI systems** — The mower uses cameras and machine learning to navigate. Great for simple yards, less reliable in shade or clutter.

### Cutting Height and Width

Most robot mowers cut between 1 and 4 inches, with adjustable settings. Cutting width typically ranges from 7 to 10 inches. Wider cutting decks mean fewer passes and faster mowing, but they also require more powerful motors and larger batteries.

### Slope Handling

If your yard has any incline, pay close attention to this spec. Entry-level mowers handle up to 20–25% slopes, while premium models can tackle 40–45%. Always check the manufacturer's rating against your actual yard gradient.

### Battery Life and Charging

Battery life determines how much lawn the mower can cover per session. Most models run 60–270 minutes and recharge in 60–90 minutes. Many mowers use a "mow, recharge, resume" pattern, which is fine for larger yards but means the job takes all day.

### Weather Resistance and Smart Features

Look for at least IPX4 water resistance if you want the mower to handle light rain. Smart features like app control, scheduling, GPS tracking, and voice assistant integration are increasingly standard, even on budget models.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Lawn Mower?

Robot mowers aren't for everyone—at least not yet. Here's how to know if you're a good candidate:

**You should buy one if:**
- You have a lawn between 0.1 and 2 acres
- You're tired of spending weekends mowing
- Your yard is relatively simple (few obstacles, manageable slopes)
- You value a consistently manicured lawn (robot mowers cut frequently, which promotes healthier grass)
- You have a reliable Wi-Fi network covering your yard

**You might want to wait if:**
- Your yard is extremely complex with many flower beds, trees, and tight spaces
- You have a very steep or uneven terrain (over 45% slope)
- Your lawn is smaller than 500 square feet (a push mower is faster)
- You're on a tight budget and don't mind mowing yourself

### Budget Considerations

Robot mowers range from around $800 for basic models to over $5,000 for premium commercial-grade units. Here's a rough breakdown:

- **Budget ($800–$1,300):** Basic wire-free or wired models for small yards. Expect compromises on slope handling, cutting precision, and build quality.
- **Mid-Range ($1,500–$2,500):** The sweet spot for most homeowners. Good navigation, decent battery life, and solid app features.
- **Premium ($3,000–$5,000+):** Best-in-class navigation, large lawn coverage, superior build quality, and advanced features like GPS tracking and multi-zone management.

Don't forget to factor in installation costs if you go with a wired system (professional installation can run $300–$800) and ongoing maintenance like blade replacements ($20–$40 per set, typically every 2–3 months during mowing season).

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

One of the biggest improvements in recent years is how easy robot mowers have become to install. Wire-free models like the three reviewed above can be set up in under an hour: place the charging station, plug it in, and let the mower map your yard. Wired systems are more involved—you'll either need to bury the boundary wire yourself (a full weekend project) or pay a professional.

Smart home integration is where these mowers really shine. All three models support Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant, so you can say things like "Alexa, ask Husqvarna to mow the front yard." Most also offer IFTTT support, allowing you to create automations like "start mowing when I leave for work" or "pause mowing when it starts raining."

The apps themselves have improved dramatically. Husqvarna's Automower Connect app is the gold standard, offering detailed mowing maps, zone scheduling, and even GPS theft tracking. Worx and Segway's apps are close behind, with intuitive interfaces and reliable connectivity.

One tip: make sure your Wi-Fi network reaches your entire yard. If it doesn't, consider a mesh Wi-Fi system or a Wi-Fi extender. A stable connection is essential for app control and firmware updates.
