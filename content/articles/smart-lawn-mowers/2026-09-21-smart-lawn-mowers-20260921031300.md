### Cutting Height and Width

Cutting height determines how short your grass gets. Most robot mowers adjust between 1 and 3.5 inches. For cool-season grasses like fescue, a higher setting (2.5–3 inches) is healthier. For warm-season grasses like Bermuda, a lower setting (1–1.5 inches) is better. Cutting width affects efficiency: a wider deck means fewer passes and faster completion.

### Navigation Technology

There are three main types:
- **Boundary Wire:** Traditional, reliable, but requires installation.
- **RTK-GPS:** Uses a fixed reference station for centimeter-level accuracy. No wire, but needs clear sky view.
- **Camera + GPS Fusion:** Combines visual data with GPS for obstacle detection and mapping. No wire, but can struggle in low light.

### Slope Handling

If your lawn has hills, pay attention to the slope rating. Most mowers handle up to 35%, but steep slopes may require a specialized model. Always check the degree rating—not just the percentage—as they can be misleading.

### Smart Home Integration

All three mowers support Alexa and Google Assistant, but integration depth varies. Husqvarna and Segway offer more robust IFTTT and smart home routines. Consider whether you want voice control, geofencing, or integration with weather services.

### Battery and Charging

Runtime and charge time determine how quickly your lawn gets mowed. Larger lawns need longer runtime or multiple charging cycles. Lithium-ion batteries are standard and last 3–5 years.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Lawn Mower?

Robot mowers are ideal for:
- **Busy homeowners** who want a consistently cut lawn without weekly effort.
- **Elderly or mobility-limited individuals** who can't push a traditional mower.
- **Tech enthusiasts** who love smart home gadgets.
- **Homeowners with irregular lawns** that are hard to mow with a riding mower.

They are less ideal for:
- **Very small lawns** (under 0.1 acre) where a push mower is faster.
- **Extremely complex landscapes** with many narrow passages and obstacles.
- **Renters** who can't install boundary wires or permanent docks.

### Budget Considerations

- **Entry-level ($600–$1,200):** Small lawns, basic navigation, boundary wire. Expect 30–45 min runtime.
- **Mid-range ($1,200–$2,500):** Medium lawns, better apps, some wire-free options. 60–120 min runtime.
- **Premium ($2,500–$5,000+):** Large lawns, RTK-GPS, advanced obstacle avoidance. 120–270 min runtime.

Don't forget installation costs: professional wire installation can run $500–$1,500, while DIY kits cost $100–$300. Wire-free models save on this but cost more upfront.

### Other Costs to Consider

- **Replacement blades:** $20–$50 per set, every 2–3 months.
- **Battery replacement:** $100–$300 every 3–5 years.
- **Maintenance:** Minimal, but clean the charging contacts and check for debris.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation

**Wire-free models (Husqvarna EPOS, Segway Navimow):** Setup involves placing the charging dock, installing a reference station (Husqvarna only), and mapping your lawn via the app. This can take 1–2 hours. No digging required.

**Wire models (Worx Landroid):** You'll need to lay boundary wire around your lawn's perimeter, which can take 3–6 hours for a half-acre. You can bury the wire or stake it temporarily. Worx provides detailed instructions and a helpful app-guided setup.

### Smart Home Integration

All three mowers integrate with Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant, allowing voice commands like "Alexa, ask Husqvarna to start mowing." For deeper automation, Husqvarna and Segway support IFTTT, so you can trigger mowing based on weather forecasts or your location. Worx offers similar features through its app but lacks native IFTTT support.

**Pro tip:** Set up geofencing so the mower pauses when you arrive home—safety first. Also, link your mower to a smart weather station to skip mowing before rain.
