## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Robot Lawn Mower?

- **Busy professionals** – If you’d rather spend Saturday morning with family than behind a mower, a robot mower is a no-brainer.
- **Tech enthusiasts** – These mowers are genuinely fun to set up and optimize. The app dashboards, GPS maps, and AI learning make lawn care feel like a hobby.
- **Homeowners with irregular schedules** – Rain or shine, your mower can handle it—weather-adaptive models will return to the charger during storms and resume later.
- **People with physical limitations** – Push mowing can be tough on the back and knees. A robot mower eliminates that strain entirely.

### Who Should Skip?

- **Renters** – Most robot mowers are semi-permanent installations. If you move frequently, the setup effort may not be worth it.
- **Owners of very small lawns (<0.1 acres)** – The cost and complexity often outweigh the time savings. A manual reel mower might be more practical.
- **Those with extremely dense tree cover** – Satellite and GPS-based mowers can struggle under heavy canopy. Camera-based models (like the Worx Vision) fare better, but still have limitations.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $1,500** – Entry-level models from brands like Worx and Robomow. Good for small, simple lawns.
- **$1,500 – $2,500** – Mid-range options with wire-free navigation, better apps, and larger battery capacities. The sweet spot for most homeowners.
- **$2,500+** – Premium models with satellite positioning, multi-zone support, and top-tier build quality. Best for large, complex properties.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Overview

Gone are the days of burying boundary wires around your entire property. Most 2026 models are **wire-free**, relying on GPS, RTK, or camera-based mapping. Here’s what to expect:

1. **Place the charging station** – Typically at the edge of your lawn near a power outlet.
2. **Set up the base station** – For GPS/RTK models, you’ll need a clear view of the sky (mounting on a roof or pole may be required).
3. **Map your lawn** – The mower will do a few initial passes to learn the boundaries and obstacles. This takes 1–3 mowing cycles.
4. **Define no-go zones** – Use the app to mark off flower beds, driveways, or play areas.
5. **Schedule mowing** – Set days, times, and cutting height. Most mowers can handle multiple schedules per week.

### Smart Home Integration

All three of our top picks support **Alexa** and **Google Home** voice commands. For example:
- *“Alexa, ask Husqvarna to start mowing.”*
- *“Hey Google, tell Worx to return to base.”*

The Segway Navimow also integrates with **Apple Shortcuts**, allowing you to trigger mowing based on time, location, or even when your phone connects to your home Wi-Fi.

**Pro tip:** Create an automation that starts your mower when your smart weather station detects no rain for the next 4 hours. This ensures your lawn gets cut on the best days.
