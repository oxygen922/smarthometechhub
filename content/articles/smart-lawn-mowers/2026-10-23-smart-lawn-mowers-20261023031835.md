## Key Features to Consider

Before you buy any automatic mower, understand what separates a great experience from a frustrating one. Here's what matters most in 2026.

### Navigation Technology: Wire vs. Wire-Free

Older robotic mowers required burying a perimeter wire around your lawn—a sweaty weekend project that also meant repairing breaks whenever you aerated or edged. Modern automatic mowers increasingly use **RTK-GPS** (real-time kinematic GPS, accurate to a couple of centimeters) or **vision-based navigation** (cameras plus AI). Wire-free systems are dramatically easier to install, but RTK needs a clear sky view, and vision systems can struggle in low light. If your yard is heavily shaded, a hybrid or wire-based system may still be the safer bet.

### Lawn Capacity and Battery

Manufacturers rate mowers by maximum lawn area, usually assuming daily mowing. A mower rated for 0.3 acres can typically handle that area if it runs most days; if you only want it out twice a week, size up. Battery runtime ranges from about 60 to 180 minutes per charge, with automatic docking and resume. For most suburban lots (under half an acre), a mid-range model is plenty.

### Cutting System and Quality

Look for adjustable cutting heights (ideally 1.5–4 inches), mulching blades, and a floating deck that follows terrain. Systematic cutting patterns (like Husqvarna's) produce more even results than random navigation, though random systems work fine for small, simple lawns. Edge-cutting capability matters—some mowers leave a noticeable strip along borders.

### Obstacle Avoidance and Safety

Vision cameras, ultrasonic sensors, and bump sensors all contribute. The best systems detect and route around obstacles without stopping entirely. Also check for lift and tilt sensors (blades stop if the mower is lifted), PIN-code locks, and GPS theft tracking—automatic mowers are attractive targets for thieves.

### Smart Home Integration

At minimum, expect a solid app with scheduling, zone management, and rain delay. For deeper integration, look for Alexa and Google Home voice control, and ideally **Matter** support for Apple Home and cross-platform automations. IFTTT support is a bonus for tinkerers.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Automatic Mower?

Automatic mowers make the most sense for homeowners who:

- **Have a defined, mostly flat or gently sloped lawn** of a quarter acre or more. Small, cluttered yards with lots of beds and trees can be a poor fit for robotic mowing.
- **Value time over money.** If mowing costs you two hours every weekend, an automatic mower pays for itself quickly in reclaimed time.
- **Travel or have busy schedules.** Scheduled mowing that runs while you're at work—or away for a week—is the killer feature.
- **Want a healthier lawn.** Frequent mulching cuts (robotic mowers typically mow a little every day) feed nutrients back into the soil and produce a denser, greener lawn than weekly buzz-cuts.

They're a **poor fit** if your yard is extremely uneven, heavily wooded (GPS issues), or if you enjoy mowing as a hobby (yes, those people exist).

### Budget Considerations

In 2026, automatic mowers span roughly **$800 to $4,500**:

- **Entry level ($800–$1,300):** Wire-free vision models like the Worx Landroid Vision line. Great for first-timers and smaller lawns.
- **Mid-range ($1,300–$2,500):** Better navigation, larger capacity, stronger smart home features. The sweet spot for most buyers.
- **Premium ($2,500–$4,500):** Large-lawn capacity, systematic cutting, rugged builds, advanced GPS systems. Worth it for acreage or long-term reliability.

Don't forget ongoing costs: replacement blades (roughly $20–$40 per set, every 1–2 seasons) and occasional battery replacement after several years. Automatic mowers use far less energy than gas mowers, so operating costs are minimal.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Installation is where automatic mowers have improved most. Wire-free models like the Segway Navimow and Worx Landroid Vision can be set up in under an hour: place the dock, charge the mower, then use the app to map your lawn by driving the mower around the boundary (or, on some models, letting it explore). RTK systems require mounting an antenna with a clear sky view—usually on the charging station or a roof—which adds a bit of planning but eliminates wire burial entirely.

For smart home integration, here's what to expect:

- **App control** is universal: scheduling, zone mapping, cutting height, rain delay, and mower status.
- **Voice control** via Alexa and Google Home is now standard on mid-range and premium models. Commands like "start mowing" and "send mower home" work reliably.
- **Matter support** (currently on Segway models) unlocks Apple Home and cross-platform automations—for example, pausing the mower when the garage door opens or resuming when the sprinkler system finishes.
- **Weather integration** is built into most apps, automatically delaying mowing after rain. Some systems can tie into smart sprinkler controllers for coordinated watering and mowing schedules.

One practical tip: set your mower to run during daylight hours in the first few weeks so you can observe its behavior, and keep the lawn free of hoses and toys. Once you trust its obstacle avoidance, you can shift to early-morning schedules and largely forget it exists.
