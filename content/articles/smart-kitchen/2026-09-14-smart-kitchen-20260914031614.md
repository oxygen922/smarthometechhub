### Camera Quality

Resolution matters, but so does lens quality and field of view. A 2K camera with a wide angle will capture more detail and context than a 1080p camera with a narrow lens. If you plan to share photos or identify subtle species differences, invest in higher resolution.

### AI Identification Accuracy

This is the heart of the smart feeder experience. Look for feeders with large, regularly updated species databases and high claimed accuracy rates. Be aware that accuracy can vary by region—a feeder trained primarily on European birds may struggle with North American species.

### Power and Connectivity

Most smart feeders rely on Wi-Fi (2.4GHz is standard; 5GHz support is rare) and either rechargeable batteries or solar power. Solar models are ideal for sunny climates, while battery-powered models offer more placement flexibility. Check that your Wi-Fi signal reaches your intended mounting location.

### Subscription Costs

Nearly every smart feeder brand now gates advanced features—cloud storage, unlimited AI identification, and video history—behind a subscription. Factor this ongoing cost into your budget. Basic functionality (live view, notifications) typically remains free.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Setting up a smart bird feeder is straightforward, but a few tips will ensure success:

1. **Choose the right location:** Mount your feeder 5–6 feet off the ground, away from windows to reduce collisions, and within Wi-Fi range. A shepherd's hook or tree branch works well.
2. **Secure the mount:** Use a sturdy pole or bracket. Wind and large birds can topple lightweight setups.
3. **Connect to Wi-Fi:** Most feeders use 2.4GHz networks. If your router broadcasts a combined SSID, you may need to separate the bands temporarily.
4. **Update firmware:** Before mounting, power on the feeder and update firmware via the app for the best performance.
5. **Smart home integration:** Birdfy and Bird Buddy both offer limited integration with Amazon Alexa and Google Home for notifications. IFTTT support allows custom automations, such as flashing smart lights when a rare bird arrives. Full integration with Apple HomeKit is still absent across the category.
