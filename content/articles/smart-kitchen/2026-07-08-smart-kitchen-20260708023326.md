## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Considerations

**Refrigerators:**
- **Space Requirements:** Measure your space carefully. Smart refrigerators often have larger footprints due to touchscreens and additional sensors. Allow 1-inch clearance on sides and back for ventilation.
- **Water Line:** Many smart fridges require a water line for ice makers and water dispensers. Professional installation is recommended if you don't have an existing line.
- **Wi-Fi Setup:** Most smart fridges require a 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network. Some newer models support 5GHz, but 2.4GHz offers better range through walls and appliances.

**Ovens & Cooktops:**
- **Electrical Requirements:** Countertop ovens like the June can plug into standard 120V outlets. Built-in ovens may require 240V circuits. Always consult a licensed electrician.
- **Ventilation:** Smart ovens with air fryer functions generate more heat and moisture. Ensure your kitchen has adequate ventilation, especially for built-in models.
- **App Integration:** Download the manufacturer's app before installation. Most apps guide you through the setup process and update firmware automatically.

### Smart Home Integration

**Building Your Ecosystem:**
1. **Choose a Hub:** SmartThings (Samsung), Apple HomeKit, or Amazon Alexa are the most compatible. Google Home is also widely supported. For maximum compatibility, look for devices that support the **Matter protocol**, which allows cross-platform communication.

2. **Voice Control:** Set up routines like "Alexa, preheat the oven to 350°F" or "Hey Google, show me what's in my fridge." Most smart appliances support voice commands for basic functions.

3. **Automation Scenarios:**
   - **Morning Routine:** Smart coffee maker starts brewing when your alarm goes off; smart refrigerator displays your calendar and weather.
   - **Cooking Mode:** Smart oven preheats automatically when you start a recipe on your phone; smart range hood activates based on cooking temperature.
   - **Energy Savings:** Smart appliances can be set to run during off-peak hours, saving up to 20% on electricity bills.

4. **Security Best Practices:**
   - Use strong, unique passwords for each appliance.
   - Enable two-factor authentication where available.
   - Keep firmware updated to patch security vulnerabilities.
   - Disable remote access when not needed, especially for cameras.

**Troubleshooting Common Issues:**
- **Wi-Fi Disconnects:** Ensure your router is within 30 feet of the appliance. Consider a mesh Wi-Fi system for larger homes.
- **App Crashes:** Clear app cache and reinstall. Most manufacturers release updates monthly.
- **Voice Assistant Conflicts:** If using multiple assistants, designate one as primary to avoid command confusion.
