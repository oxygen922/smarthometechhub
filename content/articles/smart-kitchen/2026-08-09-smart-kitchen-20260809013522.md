# The Ultimate Guide to Smart Kitchen Ovens: 2026's Best Intelligent Ovens Reviewed

**Smart ovens are no longer a luxury—they're the new standard for modern kitchens. But with so many Wi-Fi-enabled, app-controlled models flooding the market, how do you choose the right one? ## Introduction

Gone are the days of guesswork, burnt edges, and forgotten timers. As we move through 2026, these devices have become more affordable and feature-rich than ever before. Whether you are upgrading your culinary arsenal or building your first smart home, this guide will help you determine which smart oven deserves a prime spot on your countertop.

### 2. Breville Smart Oven Air Fryer Combo (BOV900BSS) - Best for Versatility

Breville has been a trusted name in kitchen appliances for decades, and their "PolyScience" smart technology brings lab-grade precision to the home. The BOV900BSS is a massive countertop convection oven that integrates Element IQ technology, which intelligently distributes power across five quartz elements to maintain exact temperature control.

**Performance:** While it lacks the camera and AI food recognition of the June, the Breville excels in raw cooking power. It offers 13 preset cooking functions, including Air Fry, Bagel, Pizza, Roast, and Dehydrate. **Integration:** It is compatible with Amazon Alexa and Google Home for basic on/off and preheat commands, but it lacks HomeKit support.

**Pros:**
- Massive 1.0 cu ft capacity (fits a 13-inch pizza)

- "Cook From Frozen" preset is highly effective
- Durable, premium build quality

**Cons:**
- No internal camera
- App is clunky and lacks AI features

**Verdict:** For families or avid home cooks who need a large, powerful, and reliable convection oven, the Breville BOV900BSS is the best workhorse on the market.

### 1. Cooking Modes
Look for versatility. The best ovens offer Air Fry, Bake, Roast, Broil, Dehydrate, and Proof. The June and Breville are tied here, but the GE lags slightly in the "Broil" setting intensity.

### 2. Connectivity and App Stability
A smart oven is only as good as its software. The June app is the gold standard for UX, offering a live feed and time-lapse recording. The GE SmartHQ app is functional but less intuitive. The Breville app is the weakest link, often failing to sync on the first attempt.

### 3. AI and Automation
Do you want the oven to think for you? The June's camera-based AI is a game-changer. The GE's barcode scanning is a clever alternative, but it is limited to a pre-approved list of packaged foods. The Breville relies on manual selection.

### 4. Safety Features
All three models feature automatic shut-off and cool-touch exteriors. However, the June adds a "Child Lock" feature that is activated via the app, which is more secure than physical buttons.

### 5. Energy Efficiency
Convection ovens are generally more efficient than traditional radiant ovens. The Breville and June both use significantly less energy than the GE, which draws a lot of power due to its size.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Requirements
- **Countertop Models (June & Breville):** These are plug-and-play. They require a standard 120V outlet (15-amp). No special wiring is needed. Ensure you have at least 18 inches of clearance above the oven for heat dissipation. Do not place them under low-hanging cabinets unless you have a heat shield.
- **Built-In Model (GE):** This requires a 240V outlet and professional installation. It is designed to fit a standard 30-inch wall oven cutout. We strongly recommend hiring a licensed electrician to ensure the circuit can handle the load.

### Smart Home Ecosystem Setup
- **Alexa / Google Assistant:** For all three ovens, the setup involves enabling the respective "skill" or "action" in the Alexa or Google Home app. Once linked, you can use voice commands like "Alexa, set the Breville to 350 degrees." The June also supports HomeKit, which is a huge plus for Apple users. You can add the June to your "Good Night" scene, which will ensure the oven is off.
- **IFTTT (If This Then That):** The June and GE support IFTTT, allowing for custom automations. For example, you can set a rule: "If my phone GPS leaves work, start preheating the GE to 425°F."
- **Z-Wave / Zigbee:** None of these models support Z-Wave or Zigbee directly, so they cannot be connected to a hub like SmartThings without a cloud bridge. However, the June's HomeKit support covers most local automation needs.

**Pro Tip:** Ensure your Wi-Fi router is positioned close to the kitchen. A 2.4GHz connection is required for most smart appliances. 5GHz networks will not work. If your router is far away, consider a mesh Wi-Fi system to avoid connectivity drops during the cooking cycle.
