# Smart Kitchen Appliances in 2026: The Best AI-Powered Gear That Still Feels Human

*Last updated: March 2026*

## Introduction

The smart kitchen has entered its awkward adolescence. After years of appliances promising to "revolutionize" how we cook—only to deliver buggy apps and gimmicky features—2026 finally brings devices that feel genuinely useful rather than merely futuristic. The shift mirrors something happening across technology: as AI becomes invisible, the best smart kitchen appliances are the ones that don't shout about their intelligence. They simply work, adapting to how you actually cook instead of forcing you into a rigid digital workflow.

This review cuts through the marketing noise to find smart kitchen appliances worth your counter space and money. We tested ovens, ranges, refrigerators, and multi-cookers from the major players, focusing on real-world performance: Does the app crash? Does the AI actually help? Will this thing still work in five years? Whether you're building a connected kitchen from scratch or adding your first smart appliance, this guide will help you spend wisely on technology that serves the cooking, not the other way around.

## Top 3 Product Recommendations

### 1. June Intelligent Oven (4th Gen) — Best Overall Smart Oven

**Price: $1,499 (estimated 2026)**

The June Oven has always been the smart kitchen's answer to the question nobody asked: "What if my toaster oven had a camera and a neural network?" Four generations in, the answer is finally compelling. The 4th Gen model refines what worked and fixes what didn't, making it the most polished countertop smart oven we've tested.

The headline feature remains the food recognition system, which now identifies over 200 ingredients and dishes with impressive accuracy. Place a salmon fillet on the tray, and the oven suggests a cook profile within seconds—usually nailing the temperature and time. The built-in camera lets you monitor progress from your phone, and the app now includes a timelapse feature that's genuinely fun for documenting your culinary experiments.

What's changed in 2026 is the restraint. Earlier June models tried to be everything: air fryer, dehydrator, sous-vide circulator, and convection oven. The 4th Gen still does all of that, but the interface has been simplified dramatically. The touchscreen is more responsive, the menus are shallower, and the AI suggestions are less intrusive. You can now disable the "smart" prompts entirely and use it as a straightforward convection oven—a welcome acknowledgment that sometimes you just want to bake a potato without a lecture.

Performance is excellent. The 12 cooking modes cover everything from toast to roast chicken, and the air fry function produces genuinely crispy results thanks to improved airflow design. Preheat times are quick (under 4 minutes to 400°F), and temperature accuracy held within 5°F during our testing. The interior is roomy enough for a 9x13 pan or a whole chicken.

The downsides are familiar: it's expensive for a countertop oven, and the proprietary cookware can feel limiting. But for households that cook multiple meals a day and want genuinely helpful automation, the June 4th Gen earns its price tag. It's the rare smart appliance that makes you a better cook rather than just a more connected one.

**Pros:** Accurate food recognition, excellent cooking performance, simplified interface, useful app
**Cons:** Premium price, proprietary accessories, bulky footprint

### 2. Samsung Bespoke AI Oven with SmartThings — Best Smart Range

**Price: $2,899 (estimated 2026)**

If you're replacing a full-size range, Samsung's Bespoke AI Oven represents the current state of the art for connected cooking. This 30-inch electric range combines a genuinely capable convection oven with the most mature smart home integration we've seen in a major appliance.

The AI features here are subtle but useful. The oven uses a camera and sensors to detect what you're cooking and suggest settings, but more impressively, it learns your preferences over time. After a few weeks, it started preheating to our preferred temperature for sheet-pan dinners without being asked. The "AI Recipe" feature pulls from Samsung's database and adjusts cook times based on internal temperature probes—a genuinely helpful feature for roasting meats.

SmartThings integration is where this range shines. It works seamlessly with Samsung's ecosystem and plays nicely with Alexa and Google Assistant. You can preheat from your car, get notifications when the roast hits temperature, and even diagnose issues remotely. The built-in air fry mode is legitimately good, and the flex-duo oven configuration lets you split the cavity for different temperatures—perfect for cooking a casserole and cookies simultaneously.

The cooktop is a standard electric glass surface, which won't satisfy gas enthusiasts, but heats evenly and cleans easily. The range's fingerprint-resistant finish held up well during our testing, and the controls are intuitive without requiring the app.

Samsung's track record on appliance software updates has been spotty in the past, and the Bespoke line's proprietary nature means you're locked into their ecosystem. But for Samsung households or anyone wanting a full-size range with genuine smart features, this is the best option in 2026.

**Pros:** Excellent SmartThings integration, learning capabilities, flexible oven configuration, solid cooking performance
**Cons:** Ecosystem lock-in, electric only, premium price

### 3. Instant Pot Smart Pro 2.0 — Best Budget Smart Multi-Cooker

**Price: $179 (estimated 2026)**

Not every smart kitchen appliance needs to cost four figures. The Instant Pot Smart Pro 2.0 proves that meaningful connectivity and useful automation can come at a reasonable price. This 8-quart multi-cooker adds Wi-Fi and app control to the classic Instant Pot formula, and while it won't replace your oven, it's the smart appliance we found ourselves using most often.

The app-based controls let you start cooking remotely, monitor progress, and receive notifications—genuinely useful for slow-cooker meals or pressure cooking that you want to start before you get home. The recipe library is extensive and includes one-tap "send to pot" functionality, which eliminates the guesswork of translating a recipe into Instant Pot settings.

The "smart" features are modest but well-executed. There's no AI food recognition here, just solid app control and a good recipe database. For many home cooks, that's exactly the right level of intelligence. The Smart Pro 2.0 also includes a temperature probe for precise cooking of meats and custards, a feature usually reserved for more expensive models.

Performance matches the Instant Pot reputation: reliable pressure cooking, good slow-cooker results, and decent air frying (though the air fry lid is sold separately). The app occasionally requires re-pairing, but it's been stable in our testing.

For anyone curious about smart kitchen appliances without committing to a major purchase, this is the ideal entry point. It's also a genuinely good multi-cooker regardless of its smart features.

**Pros:** Affordable, reliable cooking, useful app, temperature probe included
**Cons:** Limited "smart" features, air fry lid extra, app occasionally finicky

## Key Features to Consider

Before buying any smart kitchen appliance, understand what the technology actually adds—and what it doesn't. Here's how the major feature categories compare across our recommendations and the broader market.

| Feature | What It Means | June 4th Gen | Samsung Bespoke | Instant Pot Smart Pro 2.0 |
|---------|--------------|--------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| **AI Food Recognition** | Camera/sensors identify food and suggest cook settings | Excellent (200+ items) | Good (basic detection) | None |

| **Voice Assistant** | Works with Alexa, Google, or Siri | Alexa, Google | Alexa, Google, Bixby | Alexa, Google |
| **Recipe Integration** | Send recipes directly to appliance | Yes | Yes | Yes, extensive |

The most important insight from our testing: AI food recognition is a nice convenience but rarely essential. The features that matter most are reliable app control, good recipe integration, and—critically—a company with a track record of supporting its software. An appliance that loses app support in three years becomes a dumb appliance with extra complexity.

Also consider the "smart home tax." Connected appliances typically cost 20-40% more than equivalent dumb models. Sometimes that premium buys genuine utility; sometimes it buys a feature you'll use twice. Be honest about which category you're in.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy Smart Kitchen Appliances?

**Enthusiastic home cooks** benefit most from smart ovens and ranges. If you cook most days and appreciate precision (temperature probes, consistent results), the investment pays off. The June Oven, in particular, genuinely improves outcomes for anyone willing to use its suggestions.

**Busy households** get real value from remote monitoring and control. Starting dinner from the office or getting a notification when the roast is done solves a genuine problem. The Samsung range excels here.

**Tech-curious beginners** should start small. The Instant Pot Smart Pro 2.0 offers a low-risk introduction to connected cooking. If you find yourself using the app features regularly, you'll know smart appliances are for you.

**Skip smart appliances if:** you rarely cook, you're happy with your current setup, or you're uncomfortable with connected devices in your home. The "dumb" versions of most appliances work perfectly well and cost significantly less.

### Budget Considerations

For 2026, expect these price ranges:

- **Entry-level smart appliances:** $150-$400 (multi-cookers, air fryers, small ovens)
- **Mid-range:** $500-$1,500 (countertop smart ovens, smart refrigerators)
- **Premium:** $1,500-$4,000+ (full-size smart ranges, high-end smart ovens)

Our advice: allocate your budget toward the appliance you use most. A $1,500 smart oven makes sense for daily cooks; a $3,000 smart refrigerator that mostly just shows you the weather is harder to justify. Also budget for potential subscription costs—some manufacturers are moving recipe databases and advanced features behind paywalls, a trend worth watching.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Installation complexity varies dramatically by appliance type. Countertop smart ovens like the June require only a standard outlet and Wi-Fi connection—genuinely plug-and-play. Full-size ranges like the Samsung Bespoke require professional installation, a 240V circuit, and careful Wi-Fi setup (the range's metal body can interfere with signals, so router placement matters).

For smart home integration, the ecosystem question is unavoidable. Samsung's SmartThings works best with other Samsung devices and offers the most mature experience. June's app is excellent but isolated—it doesn't integrate deeply with broader smart home platforms. The Instant Pot works with Alexa and Google for basic commands but isn't a smart home hub in any meaningful sense.

**Practical tips from our testing:**

1. **Check your Wi-Fi signal** in the kitchen before buying. Dead spots behind refrigerators and ranges are common.
2. **Set up the app before installation day.** Nothing's worse than a new range you can't connect because you're waiting on a verification email.
3. **Consider Matter support** if you're building a long-term smart home. It's still spotty in kitchen appliances, but it's the standard that will matter most in coming years.
4. **Keep the manual.** App-based controls fail; physical controls are your backup.

The honest truth: smart kitchen integration remains fragmented in 2026. No single ecosystem dominates, and cross-platform compatibility is limited. Buy for the appliance first and the smart features second—that way, you're never disappointed.
