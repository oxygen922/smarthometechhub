## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Smart Kitchen Appliance?

Smart kitchen appliances make the most sense for three groups:

**Frequent cooks and meal-planners.** If you cook five or more meals a week, features like AI cook detection and inventory tracking earn their keep. You'll use them daily, and the convenience compounds.

**Households on time-of-use energy plans.** If your utility charges different rates by hour, a Matter-connected dishwasher or oven that schedules itself around peak pricing can pay for its smart premium in a year or two.

**Smart home enthusiasts already invested in an ecosystem.** If you've built an Apple Home or Google Home setup, adding Matter-compatible appliances is a natural extension — and the integration is genuinely seamless in 2026.

### Who Should Skip Them?

**Occasional cooks.** If you microwave most meals, a $3,499 smart range is wasted money. A $1,800 conventional induction range cooks identically.

**Anyone in a rental or planning to move soon.** Smart appliances are heavy, hardwired, and expensive to relocate. Buy for the home you'll stay in.

**Budget-focused buyers.** The smart premium runs $200–$1,500 per appliance. If that money matters, put it toward a better base appliance instead — a great conventional oven beats a mediocre smart one.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $1,200:** Best value tier. The AquaSense 800 dishwasher and mid-range smart microwaves live here.
- **$1,200–$2,500:** Smart refrigerators and mid-tier connected ranges. Expect solid apps but fewer AI features.
- **$2,500–$4,000:** Flagship territory. AI cook detection, camera inventory, and premium build quality.
- **Hidden costs:** Dedicated 240V circuits ($300–$800 if not already present), extended warranties (usually skip these), and potential subscription fees for premium app features (rare, but check before buying).

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### What to Expect During Installation

**Smart ranges** require a 240V dedicated circuit and typically a licensed electrician or installer. Most retailers offer haul-away and installation for $100–$200. Budget 1–2 hours.

**Smart refrigerators** need a water line for ice makers and roughly 36 inches of clearance. Measure your doorway before delivery — this is the most common installation failure. Installation is usually included with purchase.

**Smart dishwashers** are the easiest DIY install if you're replacing a same-size unit. Expect 2–3 hours for a first-timer, including water line and drain hookup.

### Connecting to Your Smart Home

Here's the good news: **Matter changed everything in 2026.** If your appliance supports Matter (the ChefLine Pro 36 and AquaSense 800 both do), setup is remarkably simple:

1. **Open your smart home app** (Apple Home, Google Home, or Alexa).
2. **Tap "Add Device" and scan the Matter QR code** on the appliance or in its manual.
3. **Assign it to a room** and name it ("Kitchen Oven," "Dishwasher").
4. **Set up automations** — for example, "Run dishwasher when electricity rate drops below $0.12/kWh."

The FrostGuard Hub 4 lacks Matter, so you'll connect it through the FrostGuard app and link that to Alexa or Google via a skill. It works, but setup is clunkier and you lose Apple Home support.

### Automation Ideas That Actually Help

- **Off-peak scheduling:** Run the dishwasher and preheat the oven only when energy is cheapest.
- **Away-from-home safety:** Get an alert if the oven is left on when everyone leaves.
- **Morning routines:** Start the coffee maker and preheat the oven as your alarm goes off.
- **Maintenance reminders:** Auto-schedule filter replacements and self-clean cycles.
