# The Best Intelligent Ovens of 2026: A Complete Smart Kitchen Review

## Introduction

The intelligent oven has quietly become the most transformative appliance in the modern American kitchen. Unlike the smart speakers and video doorbells that defined the first wave of home automation, smart ovens solve a genuinely frustrating problem: getting dinner right without hovering over a heating element for forty-five minutes. In 2026, these appliances use computer vision, precision temperature probes, and machine learning algorithms to identify what you're cooking, adjust heat on the fly, and push a notification to your phone when your salmon is perfectly flaky.

The category has matured dramatically over the past three years. Early smart ovens were expensive novelties with glitchy apps and unreliable cameras. Today's models from June, Brava, and Samsung deliver restaurant-quality consistency at prices that increasingly make sense for everyday households. Whether you're a busy parent juggling soccer practice and dinner, a home chef chasing perfect sourdough crusts, or a tech enthusiast building out a fully connected home, there's now an intelligent oven worth considering. This review breaks down the three best options on the market, the features that actually matter, and how to decide which one belongs in your kitchen.

## Top 3 Product Recommendations

### 1. June Intelligent Oven (2026 Edition) — Best Overall

**Estimated Price: $1,799**

The June Oven has been the flagship of the intelligent oven category since its debut, and the 2026 Edition is the most polished version yet. June has refined rather than reinvented, and that's exactly the right call.

The headline feature remains the built-in HD camera paired with a food-recognition system that now identifies over 250 ingredients and dishes. Place a ribeye on the included roasting pan, and the oven suggests a cook program within three seconds. The 2026 model bumps the internal camera resolution and adds a second thermal sensor, which meaningfully improves browning consistency on the top rack. In testing, frozen chicken wings came out uniformly crisp without a single flip—a claim most smart ovens can't back up.

The 7-inch touchscreen is responsive and genuinely useful, offering step-by-step guided recipes with video clips. The companion app has improved substantially; you can now monitor a live feed of your food, receive a "your food is ready" push notification, and adjust cook time remotely. Wi-Fi 6E support means the connection is rock-solid, a welcome upgrade from the occasional dropouts we experienced with older June models.

What sets June apart is its versatility. It air fries, dehydrates, broils, roasts, bakes, and even reheats leftovers with a "Reheat" mode that revived day-old pizza better than a toaster oven ever could. The 0.8-cubic-foot capacity fits a 9x13 baking dish, a whole chicken, or a 12-inch pizza.

Downsides? It's expensive, and the interior requires hand-washing since it isn't dishwasher-safe. The nonstick coating on the included pans has held up well over months of testing, but June recommends replacing them annually. For households that cook three or more meals a day, the June pays for itself in reduced takeout and fewer ruined dinners. **Rating: 9.2/10**

### 2. Brava Glass Pro — Best for Speed and Precision

**Estimated Price: $1,295**

Brava's approach to the intelligent oven is radically different: instead of a single heating element, the Brava Glass Pro uses high-intensity halogen lamps that cook food with targeted light. The result is speed that borders on absurd. A salmon fillet cooks in seven minutes. Roasted vegetables finish in nine. A full sheet-pan dinner is on the table in under twenty.

The 2026 Glass Pro model adds a larger cooking cavity (roughly 30% more usable space than the original Brava), a redesigned interior that fits a 9x13 pan, and an upgraded food probe. The touchscreen interface is clean and intuitive, and the accompanying app offers a solid library of chef-designed recipes. The zone-based cooking system lets you cook proteins and sides simultaneously at different temperatures within the same cavity—a genuine party trick that no traditional oven can match.

Where Brava stumbles is browning and baking. Because it cooks with light, achieving a deep, caramelized crust on bread or a properly browned casserole takes more fiddling than with June. The included metal pans are required for most recipes, which limits what cookware you can use. And at $1,295, it's a serious investment for what is essentially a countertop appliance.

That said, for busy professionals who want dinner fast and don't want to think about it, the Brava Glass Pro is unmatched. The food probe is the most accurate we tested, hitting target temperatures within one degree. **Rating: 8.7/10**

### 3. Samsung Bespoke AI Oven with Vision AI — Best Full-Size Smart Range

**Estimated Price: $2,899 (slide-in range)**

For homeowners who want intelligence built into a full-size range rather than a countertop appliance, Samsung's Bespoke AI Oven is the clear leader in 2026. This is a 30-inch slide-in electric range with a built-in camera above the cooktop and Samsung's Vision AI, which recognizes food and suggests cooking modes automatically.

The standout feature is the AI Pro Cooking mode, which uses the internal camera to monitor browning and adjust temperature in real time. We tested it with a whole roasted chicken and a tray of chocolate chip cookies; both came out evenly cooked with no intervention. The range also integrates deeply with SmartThings, Samsung's smart home platform, allowing you to preheat from your phone, receive notifications when a timer ends, and even sync cooking with other Samsung appliances.

The 6.3-cubic-foot oven capacity is generous, and the cooktop offers four burners plus a warming zone. The 7-inch LCD display is bright and easy to navigate, though the touch controls can be finicky with wet fingers—a common complaint with glass cooktops.

The biggest caveat is ecosystem lock-in. The best features require a Samsung account and work most seamlessly with other SmartThings devices. If you're already invested in the Samsung ecosystem, this is a no-brainer. If you're an Apple Home or Google Home household, you'll get basic functionality but miss some of the AI magic. **Rating: 8.9/10**

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Intelligent Oven?

**Busy families** benefit most from countertop models like the June, which can replace a toaster oven, air fryer, and microwave in one device. If you're reheating leftovers and cooking quick weeknight meals, the convenience justifies the cost.

**Home cooks and food enthusiasts** will appreciate the precision of the Brava Glass Pro, particularly its probe and speed. If you cook protein-heavy meals and value consistency, it's worth the investment.

**Homeowners remodeling a kitchen** should look at the Samsung Bespoke AI Oven. If you're replacing a range anyway, the incremental cost of intelligence is far lower than buying a separate countertop appliance.

**Tech enthusiasts** building a smart home will find the Samsung integrates most deeply with existing ecosystems, while June and Brava offer cleaner standalone apps.

### Who Should Skip It?

If you primarily cook with a stovetop, rarely bake, or are happy with a $150 toaster oven, an intelligent oven is overkill. Likewise, if you're uncomfortable with app-based controls or prefer appliances that work without Wi-Fi, stick with a traditional oven.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $500:** No true intelligent ovens exist at this price. You'll find "smart" ovens with app control but no camera or AI.
- **$1,000–$1,500:** The Brava Glass Pro sits here and offers the best speed-to-price ratio.
- **$1,500–$2,000:** The June Intelligent Oven is the sweet spot for most households.
- **$2,500+:** Full-size smart ranges from Samsung and LG. Best for kitchen remodels.

Remember to factor in the cost of replacement pans and accessories, which can add $100–$200 annually for countertop models.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

**Countertop models (June, Brava):** These require no installation beyond clearing counter space and plugging into a standard 120V outlet. Setup takes about 15 minutes: download the app, connect to Wi-Fi (2.4GHz or 5GHz both work on 2026 models), and run the calibration cycle. Both integrate with Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant for voice control, though the voice commands are limited to basics like preheating and checking status.

**Full-size ranges (Samsung):** Installation requires a licensed electrician or appliance installer, especially for slide-in ranges that need a 240V connection. Expect $150–$300 for professional installation if you're not comfortable doing it yourself. Samsung's SmartThings integration is the deepest of the three, allowing automations like "preheat the oven when I leave work." It also works with Alexa and Google Assistant, but Bixby offers the most granular control.

**A note on privacy:** All three ovens include cameras. Review each company's privacy policy and consider whether you're comfortable with a camera in your kitchen. All three allow you to disable the camera when not in use.

**Integration tips:**
- Create a dedicated 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network if your router supports it; smart appliances often struggle with band-steering.
- Set up notifications for cook completion so you're not checking your phone constantly.
- Pair your oven with a smart speaker in the kitchen for hands-free timers and preheating.

At $1,799, it's a significant purchase, but for households that cook daily, it earns its keep.

Whichever you choose, you're buying into a category that's improving rapidly. Software updates have already added new recipes and features to all three ovens we tested. The intelligent oven you buy today will be smarter next year—and that's a rare thing in kitchen appliances.

**Final Verdict:** If you can afford it, the June Intelligent Oven is the one to buy. If speed is your priority, choose Brava. If you're outfitting a whole kitchen, go Samsung.
