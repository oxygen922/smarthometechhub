### 1. June Oven 2.0 (The Best Overall)

The June Oven has long been the poster child for smart cooking, and the 2.0 iteration solidifies its reign. This countertop device is not just an oven; it is a multi-functional appliance that replaces your toaster, air fryer, dehydrator, and slow cooker. ### 2. Brava Glass (The Speed Demon)
**Price Estimate (2026): $1,195**

If speed is your priority, the Brava Glass is in a league of its own. Unlike conventional thermal heating, the Brava utilizes **high-intensity visible light** to cook food. This is not microwave technology; it uses targeted light waves to sear, bake, and roast without drying out your food. The "Glass" model features a sleek, see-through cooking chamber that looks like a piece of modern art, but it performs like a race car.

During our test, we cooked a chicken breast from raw to perfectly juicy (165°F internal) in just **8 minutes**—a feat that takes 25-30 minutes in a standard oven. The Brava uses an internal thermometer and "Pure Light" technology to cook the exterior and interior simultaneously. The accessory kit includes specialized metal trays that are essential for the light reflection to work correctly, which adds to the initial cost. The app is robust, offering guided recipes that automatically adjust the light intensity zones, allowing you to cook a steak and roasted vegetables at the same time, in the same chamber, with different doneness levels.

**Pros:**
- Unmatched cooking speed (up to 3x faster than conventional).
- Precise "zone" cooking for multi-component meals.
- Energy efficient—uses up to 80% less energy.

**Cons:**
- Requires proprietary cookware (included, but replacements are pricey).
- Learning curve for adjusting traditional recipes.

## Key Features to Consider

When shopping for an intelligent oven, the marketing jargon can be overwhelming. Here is a breakdown of the "must-have" technologies versus the "nice-to-haves."

### 1. AI Vision vs. Temperature Probes
The core of the "intelligence" lies in how the oven senses your food. AI Vision (like the June) uses cameras to identify food and estimate doneness visually. This is great for hands-off cooking of common items. However, temperature probes are more accurate for proteins, as they measure the actual internal temp. The best ovens offer both.

### 2. Heating Technology
Standard convection uses fans to circulate hot air. Newer models (like Brava) use light or steam. Light-based cooking is faster but requires specific cookware. Convection remains the most versatile for baking bread and pastries.

### 3. Connectivity and App Ecosystem
The app is your remote control. Look for ovens with apps that offer **live video monitoring**, push notifications, and a robust database of guided recipes. The app should feel like a necessary tool, not a clunky afterthought.

### 4. Capacity and Form Factor
Decide if you want a countertop model (portable, smaller) or a built-in wall oven (permanent, larger). Countertop models are easier to install but limit you to smaller portions.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Intelligent Oven?

**The Busy Professional:** If you work long hours and don't have time to hover over a stove, the automation offered by these ovens is a game-changer. You can prep ingredients, load the oven, and control it from your desk via your smartphone.

**The Tech Enthusiast:** If you love gadgets and data, the analytics provided by these appliances (cooking times, temperature curves, recipe suggestions) offer a new layer of engagement with cooking.

**The Novice Cook:** If you are intimidated by cooking, the guided recipes and automatic temperature settings virtually eliminate the risk of undercooked chicken or burnt cookies.

**Who Should Skip It?**
If you are a purist who enjoys the tactile process of manually controlling heat and checking on food, the automation might feel restrictive. Additionally, if you are on a tight budget, the premium price of these units (starting at $600+) is hard to justify when a $400 convection oven with a reliable thermometer can achieve 80% of the results with more effort.

### Budget Considerations (2026 Pricing)

- **Entry-Level (Under $500):** You will find "connected" ovens that allow remote control via an app, but they lack AI recognition. They are essentially standard toaster ovens with Wi-Fi.
- **Mid-Range ($600 - $1,200):** This is the sweet spot. You get AI features, good build quality, and reliable apps (June, Brava).
- **High-End ($1,500+):** You are paying for capacity (double wall ovens) or brand prestige (Café, Wolf). The cooking tech is similar to the mid-range, but the size and aesthetics justify the cost.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Requirements
- **Countertop Models (June, Brava):** These require no special installation. They need a standard 120V outlet. However, due to their high wattage, it is recommended that you plug them directly into a wall outlet rather than a power strip to avoid tripping breakers. Ensure you have at least 15 inches of clearance above the unit for steam ventilation.
- **Built-In Models (Café):** This requires professional installation. You will need a dedicated 240V circuit. If you are replacing a standard 30-inch oven, the cutout dimensions are usually standard, but always verify the specs. We strongly advise hiring a licensed electrician to ensure the wiring meets local code.

### Smart Home Ecosystem
All three of our top picks are compatible with major ecosystems, but the depth of integration varies.

- **Amazon Alexa & Google Assistant:** You can use voice commands to preheat the oven ("Alexa, tell June Oven to preheat to 400 degrees") and to ask for timers. The integration is voice-forward; you cannot change the cooking mode via voice (for safety reasons).
- **Apple HomeKit:** Currently, the June Oven is the only one of the three with native HomeKit support, allowing you to view the camera feed in the Home app and create automation scenes (e.g., turning on the kitchen lights when the oven timer finishes).
- **IFTTT (If This Then That):** This is for advanced users. You can create automations like "If I check in at my gym, start preheating the oven."

**Pro Tip:** Ensure your Wi-Fi router is strong near the kitchen. These ovens rely heavily on 2.4GHz networks for stability. A mesh Wi-Fi system is highly recommended to prevent connectivity drops during a critical cook cycle.
