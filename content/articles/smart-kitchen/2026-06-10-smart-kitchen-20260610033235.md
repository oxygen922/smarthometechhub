**Rating:** 4.7/5 stars

Samsung’s latest flagship oven is a masterclass in practical AI integration. **Price:** $2,199 (MSRP, 2026)
**Rating:** 4.5/5 stars

Induction cooking has long been the gold standard for precision and efficiency, but GE’s latest model brings genuine smart features to the surface. The cooktop features four zones that can be combined into larger cooking areas, and each zone has a built-in temperature probe that connects to the GE SmartHQ app. The game-changer is the “AutoChef” mode: select a recipe from the app—say, a delicate hollandaise sauce—and the cooktop adjusts power levels automatically to maintain the perfect temperature window.

Integration with Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant is flawless. You can say, “Alexa, set the front left burner to 350 degrees,” and it happens instantly. The cooktop also includes a “Keep Warm” feature that syncs with your oven’s estimated finish time, ensuring everything is ready simultaneously. Safety features are robust: the surface detects when a pan is removed and automatically reduces heat, and a child lock is standard.

**Pros:** Unmatched temperature precision, excellent voice control, safety-first design
**Cons:** Requires compatible cookware (magnetic), no built-in camera, app can be slow to sync

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy?

**Busy Families:** The Samsung Bespoke AI Oven is the clear winner for households with packed schedules. Its ability to parse emails and calendars means you spend less time planning meals and more time with your kids. The self-cleaning feature is a godsend for families who cook multiple meals daily.

**Serious Home Cooks:** The GE Profile Induction Cooktop is ideal if you value precision. It’s perfect for sous vide-style cooking, delicate sauces, and multi-step recipes that require exact temperatures. The lack of a camera is a minor trade-off for professional-grade control.

**Apartment Dwellers or Small Kitchens:** The June Intelligent Oven is the best choice for limited counter space. It replaces five separate appliances and offers genuine AI that learns your preferences. It’s also the most affordable option for those dipping their toes into smart cooking.

### Budget Considerations

- **Entry-Level Smart Kitchen ($500-$1,000):** Look for smart slow cookers or Wi-Fi-enabled rice cookers. These offer basic app control and recipe libraries but lack true AI.
- **Mid-Range ($1,000-$2,000):** Countertop smart ovens like the June or smaller induction cooktops from brands like Breville or Cuisinart.
- **Premium ($2,000+):** Full-size smart ovens and cooktops with AI, camera integration, and ecosystem connectivity. The Samsung and GE models represent the best in class.

**Hidden Costs:** Don’t forget about installation (induction cooktops may require a 240V outlet), compatible cookware (magnetic for induction), and potential subscription fees for premium recipe services.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Considerations

**Full-Size Ovens:** Professional installation is recommended. The Samsung Bespoke AI Oven requires a standard 240V outlet and proper ventilation. Ensure your kitchen has a dedicated circuit to avoid tripping breakers.

**Induction Cooktops:** These require a 240V connection and a flat, non-magnetic surface. Check that your cookware is compatible—if a magnet sticks to the bottom, you’re good. Some older kitchens may need an electrician to upgrade wiring.

**Countertop Ovens:** The June Oven is plug-and-play with a standard 120V outlet. No special installation needed, but ensure adequate counter space and clearance for heat dissipation.

### Smart Home Integration

All three products support Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant, but there are differences:

- **Samsung:** Deepest integration with SmartThings ecosystem. If you have Samsung appliances, TVs, or Galaxy phones, the Bespoke AI Oven becomes a central hub. It also works with IFTTT for advanced automation (e.g., “If I arrive home, preheat oven to 350°F”).
- **GE:** Best for mixed-brand smart homes. The SmartHQ app plays well with other platforms, and voice commands are responsive. Limited IFTTT support.
- **June:** Most consumer-friendly integration. The app offers pre-built automations like “Start preheating when I leave work.” Works with Apple HomeKit, which is rare for smart ovens.

**Pro Tip:** For a cohesive smart kitchen, choose appliances from the same ecosystem. Samsung SmartThings is the most robust, but GE offers better flexibility for non-Samsung homes.
