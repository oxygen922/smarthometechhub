## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy Smart Kitchen Appliances?

Smart kitchen appliances make the most sense for three types of buyers:

1. **Busy households that cook regularly.** If you're juggling work, kids, and meal prep, the time savings from auto-cook ovens and guided recipes add up fast. The June Oven is ideal here.
2. **Home cooks who love data and precision.** Induction cooktops with temperature probes and app-based monitoring appeal to anyone who wants restaurant-level control.
3. **Smart home enthusiasts.** If your home already runs on Alexa, Google, or Apple Home, adding Matter-compatible kitchen appliances creates useful automations—like your oven preheating when your phone's geofence says you're five minutes from home.

### Who Should Skip Them?

If you rarely cook, or if you're on a tight budget, a standard appliance will serve you fine. The premium for smart features is typically 20–40% over comparable non-smart models. Also skip smart appliances if you're not comfortable with app setup and Wi-Fi troubleshooting—though the best 2026 models have simplified this considerably.

### Budget Considerations

- **Entry-level smart appliances:** $300–$800 (smart air fryers, smart coffee makers, connected sous-vide)
- **Mid-range:** $800–$2,000 (smart countertop ovens, smart dishwashers)
- **Premium:** $2,000–$5,000+ (smart refrigerators, induction cooktops, full ranges)

A smart strategy is to upgrade one appliance at a time, starting with the one you use most. For most people, that's either the oven or the refrigerator. Also check for **utility rebates**—many US states offer $100–$500 rebates for switching from gas to electric induction cooking, which can offset a significant portion of the cost.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Basics

- **Countertop smart ovens (June):** Plug into a standard 120V outlet. No installation required. Place on a heat-resistant counter with at least 4 inches of clearance on all sides.
- **Smart refrigerators (Samsung):** Measure your space carefully—Bespoke models are large, and you'll need clearance for doors to open fully. Professional delivery and installation is recommended, especially for water line hookup if you want the ice maker and water dispenser.
- **Smart induction cooktops (GE Profile):** Requires a 240V circuit and professional installation. If you're replacing a gas cooktop, you'll need a licensed electrician to run the new circuit, which can add $500–$1,500 to your project.

### Smart Home Integration Tips

1. **Choose one ecosystem as your hub.** SmartThings, Apple Home, or Amazon Alexa—pick one and build around it. Matter support means cross-compatibility, but a single hub simplifies management.
2. **Set up automations that matter.** Examples: preheat the oven when your commute starts, turn on the hood fan when the cooktop activates, or add items to your shopping list via voice as you notice you're low.
3. **Secure your network.** Smart appliances are IoT devices. Use a separate Wi-Fi network or VLAN for them if your router supports it, and keep firmware updated.
4. **Test voice commands before relying on them.** "Alexa, preheat the oven to 375" works on all three of our picks, but phrasing varies by brand. Set up your most-used commands during the first week.
