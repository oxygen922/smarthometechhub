## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy Smart Kitchen Appliances?

Smart kitchen appliances are no longer just for tech enthusiasts. Here's a quick breakdown:

- **Busy professionals:** Smart ovens and sous-vide save time and reduce decision fatigue.
- **Families:** Smart refrigerators help manage groceries, reduce waste, and coordinate meals.
- **Home cooks and foodies:** Precision cooking tools elevate results consistently.
- **Smart home enthusiasts:** Matter-compatible appliances integrate with your existing ecosystem.
- **Seniors or accessibility-focused users:** Voice control and app reminders add real value.

### Budget Considerations

Smart kitchen appliances range from $100 to over $5,000. Here's how to think about spending:

- **Under $300:** Sous-vide, smart scales, smart thermometers. High impact, low cost.
- **$300–$1,000:** Smart air fryers, multicookers, and entry-level smart ovens.
- **$1,000–$2,500:** Premium countertop ovens, smart ranges, and built-in appliances.
- **$2,500+:** Full-size smart refrigerators, wall ovens, and integrated kitchen systems.

**My advice:** Start with one high-impact appliance (like a smart oven or sous-vide). Add more only when you find a genuine pain point they solve. Don't buy smart features you'll never use — a $4,000 fridge with a screen you ignore is just an expensive fridge.

Also factor in **energy costs**. Modern smart appliances are generally more efficient, but always check Energy Star ratings. A smart refrigerator can save $50–$100 per year compared to an older model.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Basics

- **Countertop appliances (ovens, sous-vide):** Plug-and-play. No professional installation needed. Just ensure adequate counter space and ventilation.
- **Refrigerators:** Measure your space carefully. Samsung's Bespoke line requires a water line for ice makers and proper clearance for doors. Professional installation is recommended for built-in models.
- **Ranges and wall ovens:** Usually require a licensed electrician or installer, especially for 240V connections.

### Smart Home Integration

All three products I recommend support major platforms, but the experience varies:

- **Matter:** The universal standard. If an appliance supports Matter, it works with Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, and Samsung SmartThings. The June Oven Pro and Samsung Family Hub+ both support Matter.
- **Wi-Fi vs. Bluetooth:** Wi-Fi enables remote control; Bluetooth is for local setup. Prefer Wi-Fi for full functionality.
- **App quality:** Anova and June have best-in-class apps. Samsung's SmartThings is powerful but occasionally overwhelming.

**Pro tip:** Set up a dedicated 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network for smart appliances. Many devices struggle with 5GHz or mesh networks that switch bands.

### Automation Ideas

- Preheat your oven when your phone's GPS shows you're 10 minutes from home.
- Get a notification when your sous-vide reaches temperature.
- Have your fridge add items to a shared grocery list automatically when inventory runs low.
- Sync your smart lights to turn on when the oven finishes cooking.
