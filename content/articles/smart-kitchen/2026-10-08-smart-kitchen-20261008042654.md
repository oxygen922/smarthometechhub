They recognize what you put inside, adjust temperature and humidity in real time, and sync with your smart home ecosystem for voice control and remote monitoring.

In 2026, the category has matured dramatically. Prices have dropped, cooking accuracy has improved, and manufacturers have finally solved the app-experience problems that plagued early models. Whether you're a busy professional who wants dinner ready when you walk in the door or a home baker chasing consistency, there's an intelligent oven built for you. In this guide, we review the three best intelligent ovens on the market right now, break down the key features to consider, and help you decide which model deserves counter space in your kitchen.

### 2. Brava Glass Smart Oven — Best for Speed Cooking

Brava's approach to the intelligent oven is radically different: instead of a single heating element, the Brava Glass uses high-intensity halogen lamps arranged in zones, each controlled independently by the oven's computer. The result is cooking speeds that rival a microwave but with the texture and browning of a real oven. In our tests, a full sheet-pan dinner of chicken and vegetables was done in roughly 12 minutes.

The 2026 Brava Glass adds an improved **Pure Light** cooking mode and a redesigned touchscreen interface that's noticeably more responsive. The oven uses a combination of pre-programmed cook modes and a temperature probe to hit target doneness, and the app lets you monitor progress from another room. Because it heats instantly, there's no preheat wait — a meaningful quality-of-life improvement for weeknight cooking.

The trade-off is capacity and learning curve. The Brava's zone-based cooking is powerful but takes a few sessions to understand, and it can't accommodate a large roast or a full-sized baking sheet. It's best thought of as a high-speed second oven rather than a primary one.

**Pros:** Extremely fast cooking, no preheat, versatile cook modes, energy efficient
**Cons:** Small capacity, steeper learning curve, expensive
**Price (2026 estimate):** $995–$1,295

**Verdict:** Ideal for small households and anyone who values speed above all else.

## Key Features to Consider

Before you buy an intelligent oven, understand what actually separates a great one from a mediocre one.

**AI Food Recognition vs. Sensor Cooking.** Some ovens (like June) use cameras to identify food and pick a cook program. Others (like Samsung) use temperature and humidity sensors to adjust cooking in real time. Both work; camera-based systems are more hands-off, while sensor-based systems tend to be more accurate for large items like roasts.

**Connectivity and App Quality.** A smart oven is only as good as its app. Look for reliable remote preheat, push notifications, and a recipe library that actually syncs with the oven. Check recent app-store reviews, not just launch coverage.

**Voice Assistant Support.** Alexa and Google Assistant support is now table stakes. Apple HomeKit support remains rare — if you're an Apple household, verify compatibility before buying.

**Capacity and Form Factor.** Countertop intelligent ovens typically range from 0.5 to 1.0 cubic feet. Full-size wall ovens run 4.5 to 5.8 cubic feet. Match capacity to your household size and cooking habits.

**Cooking Modes.** Air fry, dehydrate, slow cook, steam, and proof are common. More modes mean more versatility, but only if you'll use them.

**Price and Value.** Countertop models run $300–$1,300; full-size intelligent wall ovens run $1,800–$3,500. Decide whether you want a primary oven or a smart second oven.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Intelligent Oven?

**Busy professionals and couples** benefit most from countertop intelligent ovens. The combination of food recognition, remote monitoring, and fast cooking means dinner is genuinely easier, not just more connected.

**Home bakers** should look for models with precise temperature control and steam functions. The Samsung Bespoke AI Oven's sensor cooking is particularly strong for breads and pastries, while the June handles smaller bakes well.

**Smart home enthusiasts** already running Alexa or Google ecosystems will get the most out of any of these ovens. If you're deep in the Samsung ecosystem, the Bespoke AI Oven's integration is unmatched.

**Large families** should prioritize capacity. A countertop intelligent oven works as a second oven, but a full-size wall oven is the better primary cooking appliance.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $500:** Basic smart countertop ovens with app control but limited AI. Fine for reheating and simple bakes.
- **$500–$1,300:** The sweet spot for countertop intelligent ovens. You get real AI cooking, good apps, and reliable results.
- **$1,800–$3,500:** Full-size intelligent wall ovens. Budget for professional installation, which typically runs $150–$400.

Don't overlook energy costs. Countertop intelligent ovens use far less energy than a full-size oven for small meals, which can offset their price over several years of daily use.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

**Countertop models** are plug-and-play. The June and Brava require only a standard 120V outlet and a few inches of clearance. Setup takes about 10 minutes, including Wi-Fi pairing and app login.

**Wall ovens** require professional installation, a dedicated 240V circuit, and cabinet modifications in most cases. Expect to pay $150–$400 for installation on top of the appliance price.

For smart home integration, all three ovens support **Amazon Alexa** and **Google Assistant** for voice commands like preheating and timer setting. The Samsung Bespoke AI Oven integrates most deeply with **SmartThings**, including recipe suggestions based on fridge inventory and automation routines (for example, preheating when your phone's geofence says you're heading home). The June and Brava apps offer solid remote control and notifications but fewer cross-device automations.

If you use **Apple HomeKit**, none of these ovens support it natively in 2026. You'd need a bridge or a HomeKit-compatible smart plug for basic on/off control, which limits full functionality.

One practical tip: connect your oven to a **2.4GHz Wi-Fi network**. All three models had connectivity hiccups on 5GHz during our testing, a common issue with smart kitchen appliances.
