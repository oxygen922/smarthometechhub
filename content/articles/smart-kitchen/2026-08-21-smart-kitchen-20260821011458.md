## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Countertop vs. Built-In Installation

The first decision you'll face is whether to choose a countertop model (like the June or Breville) or a built-in unit (like the Samsung). Countertop ovens are plug-and-play—simply place them on your counter, plug into a standard 120V outlet, and you're ready to go. They're ideal for apartments and kitchens with limited space.

Built-in ovens require professional installation, typically involving a 240V electrical connection and precise cabinetry measurements. This adds $200-$500 to your total cost but provides a seamless, integrated look that increases your home's value. If you're building a new kitchen or doing a major renovation, a built-in smart oven is worth the investment.

### Smart Home Ecosystem Compatibility

Before purchasing, verify that the oven integrates with your existing smart home setup:

- **Amazon Alexa:** All three recommended models support Alexa. You can say, "Alexa, preheat the June oven to 400 degrees," and it will comply.
- **Google Home:** The June and Samsung ovens have native Google Home integration. The Breville requires a third-party skill but works reliably.
- **Apple HomeKit:** As of 2026, none of the major intelligent oven brands offer native HomeKit support, but you can bridge them using a hub like HomeBridge.
- **IFTTT (If This Then That):** For advanced automation, check if the oven supports IFTTT applets. For example, you can create an automation that preheats your oven when you leave work, based on your phone's GPS location.

### Wi-Fi and Connectivity Considerations

All smart ovens require a stable 2.4GHz Wi-Fi connection. If your kitchen is far from your router, consider investing in a mesh Wi-Fi system to ensure reliable connectivity. Additionally, keep your oven's firmware updated to access the latest features and security patches.
