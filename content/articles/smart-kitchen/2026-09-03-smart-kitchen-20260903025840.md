## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Intelligent Oven?

**Best for:**
- **Busy Professionals:** The ability to start cooking remotely and receive notifications when food is ready is invaluable for those with demanding schedules.
- **Cooking Novices:** AI-powered food recognition and preset programs eliminate the intimidation factor of cooking unfamiliar dishes.
- **Families:** Large-capacity models like the Samsung can handle family-sized meals while ensuring consistent results.
- **Smart Home Enthusiasts:** Those who want their kitchen to integrate seamlessly with their existing smart home ecosystem.

**Consider alternatives if:**
- **You're on a tight budget:** Traditional convection ovens offer excellent cooking performance without the smart features.
- **You prefer complete cooking control:** Some chefs find AI presets limiting and prefer manual temperature management.
- **Your kitchen lacks reliable Wi-Fi:** Most intelligent features require a stable internet connection.

### Budget Considerations

**Entry-Level ($400-$700):**
The Tovala Smart Oven ($449) offers basic smart scanning of pre-packaged meals, while the Cosori Air Fryer Max ($399) includes app connectivity and 12 presets. Both provide entry points into smart cooking without the premium price tag.

**Mid-Range ($700-$1,500):**
The June Intelligent Oven at $899 offers the best value in this category, providing comprehensive AI features at a reasonable price point. The Brava Glass at $1,295 appeals to those prioritizing precision cooking.

**Premium ($2,000+):**
Full-size intelligent ovens from Samsung, LG ($2,899 for the InstaView with AI), and GE Profile ($2,399) dominate this segment. These offer the most capacity and integrate into kitchen cabinetry but require professional installation.

**Hidden Costs to Consider:**
- Professional installation (built-in models): $200-$500
- Extended warranty: $100-$300
- Smart home hub (if not using existing): $50-$150
- Increased energy usage: $30-$60 annually

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Considerations

**Countertop Models (June, Brava):**
These require minimal setup—simply place on a sturdy countertop near an outlet and connect to Wi-Fi. Ensure at least 4 inches of clearance on all sides for proper ventilation. The June's exterior remains cool to the touch, making it safe for households with children.

**Built-In Models (Samsung):**
Professional installation is strongly recommended. The oven requires a dedicated 240V circuit, and older homes may need electrical upgrades costing $500-$1,000. Verify cutout dimensions match your existing cabinet space before purchasing. Most manufacturers offer installation services through certified technicians.

### Smart Home Ecosystem Compatibility

**Amazon Alexa:**
All three recommended models work with Alexa, allowing voice commands like "Alexa, preheat the oven to 375 degrees." You can also create routines—for example, having the oven preheat when you say "Alexa, start dinner."

**Google Home:**
June and Samsung integrate with Google Assistant, though Brava currently lacks Google connectivity. Commands are similar to Alexa, with the added benefit of displaying oven status on Google Nest Hub screens.

**Apple HomeKit:**
Unfortunately, none of the top three models natively support HomeKit. Third-party solutions like HomeBridge can bridge the gap for technically savvy users, but expect limited functionality.

**Matter Protocol:**
The 2026 Samsung models support Matter, the new smart home standard. This provides more robust cross-platform compatibility and future-proofing. June has announced Matter support in upcoming firmware updates.

### App Experience

The companion apps have improved dramatically over the past year. June's app offers the most polished experience, streaming live video from the oven's camera so you can check your food's progress from anywhere. Samsung's SmartThings app integrates oven controls with your broader smart home dashboard, while Brava's app focuses primarily on recipe guidance and zone control.
