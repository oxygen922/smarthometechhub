## Key Features to Consider

When shopping for a smart oven in 2026, you'll encounter a dizzying array of features. Here's a breakdown of what matters most:

### Cooking Modes and Versatility
Look for ovens that offer multiple cooking modes: convection bake, broil, air fry, dehydrate, slow cook, and proof. The more modes, the more you can do with a single appliance.

### AI and Food Recognition
High-end models like the June use cameras and sensors to identify food and set cooking parameters automatically. This is a game-changer for busy households, but it comes at a premium.

### Connectivity and App Support
Check whether the oven supports your preferred smart home ecosystem. Most modern ovens work with Alexa and Google Assistant, but Apple HomeKit support is less common. A good app should offer remote monitoring, voice control, and recipe integration.

### Temperature Accuracy and Sensors
Wireless temperature probes are essential for roasting meats. Some ovens also include humidity sensors and thermal cameras for precise cooking.

### Interior Capacity
Countertop ovens typically range from 0.5 to 1.2 cubic feet, while full-size ranges offer 4-6 cubic feet. Consider your typical cooking needs and kitchen space.

### Installation Requirements
Countertop ovens are plug-and-play, but full-size ranges may require 240V outlets and professional installation. Check your kitchen's electrical setup before purchasing.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Tips

**Countertop Ovens:** These are the easiest to install. Simply place the oven on a heat-resistant surface, plug it into a standard 120V outlet, and connect to your Wi-Fi network. Make sure there's at least 6 inches of clearance above and behind the oven for ventilation.

**Full-Size Ranges:** Professional installation is recommended. You'll need a 240V outlet with a dedicated circuit. Some models require a 50-amp breaker, so consult an electrician if you're replacing an older oven. The GE Profile, for example, comes with a detailed installation guide, but it's best left to the pros.

### Smart Home Integration Steps

1. **Download the App:** Most smart ovens have a dedicated app (June, GE Kitchen, Breville+). Create an account and follow the on-screen setup instructions.
2. **Connect to Wi-Fi:** Ensure your oven is within range of your router. For best performance, use a 5GHz band if supported. The June 2026 model supports Wi-Fi 6E, which offers faster and more reliable connections.
3. **Add to Smart Home Ecosystem:** In the app, link your oven to Alexa, Google Home, or Apple HomeKit. You can then create routines—for example, "Alexa, preheat the oven to 350°F" or "Good morning, set the oven to warm mode."
4. **Calibrate Sensors (if applicable):** Some ovens, like the June, require a brief calibration process to ensure accurate food recognition and temperature readings. This usually takes less than 10 minutes.
5. **Explore Guided Recipes:** The app will likely offer a library of recipes. Try a few to get familiar with the oven's capabilities.

### Troubleshooting Common Issues

- **Wi-Fi Connectivity Drops:** Ensure your router is not too far from the oven. Consider using a mesh Wi-Fi system for larger homes.
- **App Not Responding:** Force-close the app and reopen it. If the issue persists, check for firmware updates in the app settings.
- **Temperature Inconsistencies:** Use an external oven thermometer to verify accuracy. Some ovens allow you to calibrate the temperature offset in the settings menu.
