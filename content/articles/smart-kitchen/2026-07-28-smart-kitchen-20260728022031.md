### Critical Factors to Evaluate

**1. Cooking Capacity and Type**
Consider your household size and cooking habits. Countertop ovens (like the June and Breville) are ideal for small families, couples, or individuals who cook frequently but don't need massive capacity. Full-size ovens (like the Samsung) are essential for large families, holiday cooking, or anyone who bakes multiple dishes simultaneously.

**2. AI and Smart Features**
Not all smart ovens are created equal. High-end models use computer vision and machine learning to identify food and adjust settings automatically. Mid-range models offer preset programs and basic app control. Entry-level smart ovens might only provide remote monitoring. Decide how much "thinking" you want your oven to do versus how much manual control you prefer.

**3. Connectivity and Ecosystem Integration**
If you're building a smart home, consider how the oven integrates with your existing ecosystem. Samsung ovens work best with SmartThings, while the June Oven plays nicely with Alexa and Google Assistant. Some ovens also integrate with IFTTT for custom automations, like preheating when your smart thermostat detects you're on your way home.

**4. Cooking Modes and Versatility**
Modern smart ovens are multifunctional. Look for models that offer air frying, dehydrating, proofing, slow cooking, and steam cooking in addition to standard bake and broil. The more functions, the more you can replace countertop appliances like air fryers, toasters, and dehydrators.

**5. Temperature Accuracy and Consistency**
Precision matters, especially for baking. High-end ovens use multiple sensors and algorithms to maintain temperature within a few degrees. Budget models may have wider fluctuations, which can affect delicate recipes like soufflés or macarons.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Considerations

**Countertop Ovens:** These are plug-and-play. Simply place the oven on a heat-resistant surface, plug it into a standard 120V outlet, and connect to your Wi-Fi network. Ensure there's adequate clearance (at least 4 inches on all sides) for ventilation.

**Full-Size Wall Ovens:** Professional installation is strongly recommended. You'll need a dedicated 240V circuit, and the oven must be properly vented. Most manufacturers offer installation services, or you can hire a licensed electrician. Expect the process to take 2–4 hours.

### Smart Home Integration

**Wi-Fi Setup:** All three recommended ovens connect to your home Wi-Fi network (2.4GHz band is most reliable). Download the manufacturer's app, create an account, and follow the on-screen instructions to pair the oven. Most modern ovens support WPA3 security protocols.

**Voice Control:**
- **Amazon Alexa:** "Alexa, preheat the June oven to 375 degrees."
- **Google Assistant:** "Hey Google, set the Samsung oven to convection bake at 350 degrees."
- **Apple HomeKit:** Only available on select models; check compatibility before purchasing.

**Automation Scenarios:**
- **Geofencing:** Set your oven to preheat automatically when your phone detects you're 15 minutes from home.
- **Routines:** Create a "Good Morning" routine that turns on the oven light, preheats for breakfast, and sends a notification to your phone.
- **IFTTT Integration:** Connect your oven to other smart devices. For example, have your smart lights flash when the oven timer goes off.

**App Features to Look For:**
- Remote temperature monitoring
- Cooking timer and notifications
- Recipe library with step-by-step guidance
- Energy usage tracking
- Firmware updates for new features

**Troubleshooting Tips:**
- If the oven won't connect, try moving your router closer or using a Wi-Fi extender.
- Ensure your oven's firmware is up to date for optimal performance.
- Reset the oven's network settings if you change your Wi-Fi password.
