### Connectivity Options

- **Hub-based (Zigbee/Thread):** Systems like Philips Hue use a central hub that communicates with bulbs via a low-power mesh network. This offers superior range, reliability, and the ability to control dozens of bulbs without straining your Wi-Fi. The downside is the initial hub cost and the need for a dedicated bridge.
- **Hub-free (Wi-Fi):** Bulbs like LIFX and Sengled connect directly to your home router. This simplifies installation but can congest your Wi-Fi network if you install many bulbs. Most modern routers handle 20-30 Wi-Fi bulbs without issues, but older routers may struggle.
- **Matter compatibility:** The new Matter standard is gaining traction in 2026. Philips Hue and LIFX both offer Matter-compatible versions, ensuring interoperability with future smart home devices.

### Brightness and Color Temperature

- **Lumens:** For general room lighting, aim for 800-1,100 lumens per bulb. Bedrooms and hallways can use lower brightness (500-800 lumens), while kitchens and home offices benefit from 1,000+ lumens.
- **Color temperature:** Measured in Kelvin (K), lower numbers (2700K) produce warm, cozy light, while higher numbers (5000K) mimic daylight. Tunable white bulbs let you adjust between these extremes throughout the day.
- **Color accuracy:** A CRI of 90 or higher is recommended for spaces where color appearance matters, such as living rooms and home offices.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Smart Light Bulb?

**Ideal candidates:**
- **Tech enthusiasts** who want full control over their home lighting
- **Energy-conscious homeowners** looking to reduce electricity consumption (LED smart bulbs use 80% less energy than incandescents)

**Less suitable for:**

- **Entry-level ($20-30 per bulb):** Sengled, Wyze, and Govee offer basic smart bulbs with Wi-Fi connectivity and essential scheduling features. These are great for testing smart lighting without a large investment.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Process

1. **Choose your ecosystem:** Decide whether you want a hub-based system (Philips Hue) or hub-free (LIFX, Sengled). Hub-based systems offer better scalability but require an initial setup step.
2. **Screw in the bulb:** Replace your existing bulb with the smart bulb. Ensure the fixture is compatible (some enclosed fixtures may trap heat).
3. **Download the app:** Each brand has its own app for initial setup. Follow the in-app instructions to connect the bulb to your Wi-Fi or hub.
4. **Configure voice assistants:** Link your smart bulb account to Alexa, Google Assistant, or Apple HomeKit. This typically takes less than a minute.
5. **Create scenes and schedules:** Use the app to set up morning wake-up routines, evening wind-down sequences, and vacation lighting schedules.

### Smart Home Integration Tips

- **Geofencing:** Most smart bulbs can turn on automatically when you arrive home and off when you leave. This saves energy and enhances security.
- **Voice control:** Use commands like "Alexa, set living room to 50% brightness" or "Hey Google, turn off all lights."
- **Routines:** Combine smart bulbs with other devices. For example, have your lights dim automatically when you start a movie on your smart TV, or turn on the bathroom light when your smart alarm goes off.
- **Sensors:** Pair motion-sensing bulbs (like the Sengled) with door/window sensors for advanced automation. For instance, lights can turn on when you open the front door after sunset.

### Troubleshooting Common Issues

- **Bulb not connecting:** Ensure your Wi-Fi is on the 2.4 GHz band (many smart bulbs don't support 5 GHz). Restart your router and try again.
- **Flickering:** This often indicates a dimmer switch incompatibility. Smart bulbs require a neutral wire or a compatible smart dimmer switch.
- **Slow response:** For Wi-Fi bulbs, check your router's bandwidth. For hub-based systems, ensure the hub is centrally located and not obstructed.
