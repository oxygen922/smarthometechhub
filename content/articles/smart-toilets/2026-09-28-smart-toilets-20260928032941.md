## Top 3 Bidet Seat Recommendations for 2026

### 1. TOTO Washlet S7A – Best Overall Premium Bidet Seat

**Price: Approximately $1,400–$1,600 (2026 estimate)**

If money is no object and you want the absolute best bidet seat experience available in the US market, the TOTO Washlet S7A is the gold standard. TOTO essentially invented the modern bidet seat, and the S7A represents decades of refinement.

**Performance and Features**

The S7A features TOTO's patented eWater+ system, which electrolyzes tap water to create a mild hypochlorous acid solution that cleans the wand and bowl after every use. This isn't just marketing fluff—it genuinely keeps the unit cleaner and reduces the need for harsh chemicals.

The heated seat is adjustable across five temperature levels, and the warm air dryer actually works (unlike many competitors where you'd need to sit for ten minutes to feel any effect). The S7A offers multiple spray modes including rear cleanse, front cleanse (bidet), and a oscillating "wide" spray that moves back and forth for thorough cleaning.

**What Sets It Apart**

The S7A includes a remote control with an LCD screen that's intuitive and responsive. It also features user presets, so multiple family members can save their preferred settings. The "Premist" function sprays a fine mist of water on the bowl before use, which prevents waste from sticking—a small detail that makes a big difference in keeping your toilet cleaner.

The unit also includes automatic open/close lid functionality, a nightlight, and a motion sensor that activates the lid when you approach. It's the closest thing to a fully automated bathroom experience you can get without remodeling.

**Pros:**
- Exceptional build quality and reliability
- eWater+ self-cleaning system
- Effective warm air dryer
- User presets for multiple people
- Quiet operation

**Cons:**
- Very expensive
- Requires a GFCI outlet near the toilet
- Remote requires mounting or counter space

**Who It's For:** Homeowners who want the absolute best and are willing to pay for it. If you're remodeling a master bathroom or want a luxury upgrade, this is the one.

## Key Features to Consider

Before you buy, it's important to understand what separates a good bidet seat from a great one. Here's a breakdown of the features that matter most:

| Feature | Why It Matters | Budget Models | Mid-Range | Premium |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-----------|---------|
| **Water Heating** | Determines how long you get warm water | Tank (limited) | Tankless (unlimited) | Tankless (unlimited) |
| **Heated Seat** | Comfort in cold months | Often included | Yes | Yes, adjustable |
| **Warm Air Dryer** | Reduces toilet paper use | Rarely | Sometimes | Yes, effective |
| **Deodorizer** | Neutralizes odors | No | Sometimes | Yes |
| **Remote Control** | Convenience and accessibility | No (side panel) | Often | Yes, with presets |
| **Self-Cleaning Nozzle** | Hygiene and maintenance | Basic | Yes | Advanced (UV or electrolyzed water) |
| **Adjustable Spray** | Personal comfort | Limited | Yes | Yes, multiple modes |
| **Night Light** | Usability at night | Sometimes | Often | Yes |
| **Energy Saving Mode** | Reduces electricity costs | Rarely | Sometimes | Yes |

**Water Heating: Tank vs. Tankless**

This is the single most important differentiator. Tank systems heat a small reservoir of water, giving you maybe 30-60 seconds of warm water before it runs cold. Tankless (also called "instantaneous") systems heat water on demand, providing unlimited warm water for as long as you need. If you've ever experienced a tank system running cold mid-use, you'll understand why tankless is worth the upgrade.

**Spray Options and Adjustability**

At minimum, you want adjustable water pressure and nozzle position. Premium models add oscillating sprays, pulsating modes, and separate feminine wash settings. If you have specific needs (post-surgery, mobility issues, etc.), more adjustability is better.

**Heated Seat and Dryer**

A heated seat is a game-changer in winter. The dryer is more of a nice-to-have—even the best dryers don't fully dry you, so you'll likely still use a small amount of paper. But they do reduce usage and add a feeling of luxury.

**Deodorizer**

This feature uses a carbon filter and fan to pull air through and neutralize odors. It's surprisingly effective and a great feature if your bathroom is shared or lacks ventilation.

## Buying Guide: Who Should Buy a Bidet Seat?

**Who Benefits Most**

- **Anyone seeking better hygiene:** Bidets clean more thoroughly than toilet paper alone.
- **People with mobility issues:** Bidet seats reduce the need to twist and reach.
- **Those with hemorrhoids or post-surgery needs:** Warm water and gentle spray can soothe and aid healing.
- **Eco-conscious households:** Bidets dramatically reduce toilet paper consumption.
- **Cold-climate residents:** A heated seat is a small daily luxury you'll appreciate every morning.

**Budget Considerations**

- **Under $300:** You'll get heated seat, warm water (tank), and basic spray. Good for first-timers.
- **$300–$700:** The sweet spot. Tankless heating, dryer, deodorizer, remote control.
- **$700–$1,600+:** Premium features, best build quality, advanced self-cleaning, presets, auto open/close.

**Compatibility Check**

Before buying, measure your toilet. Most bidet seats fit standard round or elongated bowls, but you need to confirm. Also check that you have a GFCI-protected outlet within reach of the toilet (typically within 3-4 feet). If not, you'll need an electrician—factor that into your budget.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

**Installation Overview**

Installing a bidet seat is a DIY-friendly project for most homeowners. Here's the general process:

1. **Turn off the water supply** to your toilet and flush to empty the tank.
2. **Remove the existing toilet seat** by unscrewing the bolts.
3. **Install the T-adapter** onto the water supply line to divert water to the bidet.
4. **Mount the bidet seat** using the included brackets and hardware.
5. **Connect the water hose** from the T-adapter to the bidet seat.
6. **Plug in the unit** to a GFCI outlet.
7. **Test and adjust** settings, checking for leaks.

Most installations take 30-60 minutes. If you're not comfortable with basic plumbing, a handyman can do it for $100-$200.

**Smart Home Integration**

Here's where things get interesting for tech enthusiasts. While bidet seats aren't typically as integrated into smart home ecosystems as, say, smart lights or thermostats, some models offer:

- **Voice control** via Amazon Alexa or Google Assistant (limited to basic functions on select models)
- **App connectivity** for firmware updates and usage tracking (rare but growing)
- **Motion sensors** for automatic lid operation (TOTO and some Kohler models)

The reality is that bidet seats are more "smart" in the sense of automated comfort features than in connectivity. That said, if you have a smart bathroom setup, a bidet seat with motion detection and auto-close lid fits right in.

**Maintenance Tips**

- Clean the nozzle regularly using the self-clean function and a soft brush.
- Replace the deodorizer filter every 6-12 months (if equipped).
- Descale the water heater annually if you live in a hard water area.
- Check the water connections for leaks every few months.
