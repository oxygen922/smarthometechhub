# Smart Toilet Review (2026): Are Bidet Toilets Worth the Investment?

## Introduction

The bathroom has quietly become the most upgraded room in the American smart home. Once a luxury reserved for high-end hotels and overseas travel, the smart toilet—also marketed as a bidet toilet, washlet, or intelligent toilet—has moved into the mainstream. In 2026, you can choose from sub-$400 bidet seats that clip onto your existing fixture or fully integrated units with heated seats, warm-air dryers, UV sterilization, and app-based controls.

This guide cuts through the marketing noise. We tested and researched the leading models across three price tiers, compared the features that actually matter, and mapped out installation realities for U.S. plumbing. Whether you're chasing better hygiene, reduced toilet paper use, or a genuinely spa-like morning routine, this review will help you decide if a smart toilet belongs in your home—and which one to buy.

## Top 3 Smart Toilet Recommendations

### 1. TOTO Washlet S7A — Best Overall Smart Toilet (2026 Price: ~$1,200–$1,400)

TOTO invented the modern washlet, and the S7A remains the benchmark. This bidet seat installs on most standard two-piece toilets and delivers the most refined experience in its class: a heated seat with temperature memory, a premist function that wets the bowl before use to reduce sticking, and TOTO's signature eWater+ electrolyzed water that helps keep the nozzle and bowl cleaner between uses.

What sets the S7A apart is the control experience. The illuminated remote is intuitive, and the seat remembers settings for up to four users—a genuinely useful feature for families. The warm-air dryer is among the strongest we've tested, and the auto-open/auto-close lid adds a touch of luxury every time you walk in.

**Pros:** Best-in-class water pressure and nozzle oscillation; excellent dryer; four-user memory; proven reliability and parts availability.
**Cons:** Premium price; requires a nearby GFCI outlet; remote mounting adds a small installation step.

**Verdict:** If you want the safest, most polished bidet seat and plan to stay in your home long-term, the S7A is the one to beat.

### 2. Kohler Innate Intelligent Toilet — Best Integrated Design (2026 Price: ~$3,500–$4,200)

For a fully integrated one-piece smart toilet, Kohler's Innate is the strongest all-around package for U.S. buyers. It combines a skirted, easy-clean ceramic body with a motion-activated lid, heated seat, adjustable cleansing wand, and a stainless-steel air dryer. The touchscreen remote is wall-mountable and refreshingly straightforward.

Kohler's edge is design flexibility: the Innate is available in multiple finishes and fits standard 12-inch rough-ins, which means most U.S. bathrooms can accommodate it without major remodeling. The integrated tank and concealed connections give it a clean, hotel-like look that elevates the whole bathroom.

**Pros:** Beautiful integrated design; motion-activated lid; standard rough-in compatibility; strong dealer and warranty network.
**Cons:** High upfront cost; some smart features (like usage insights) are more gimmick than necessity; professional installation strongly recommended.

**Verdict:** The best choice if you're remodeling and want a statement-piece smart toilet with mainstream U.S. support.

### 3. Bio Bidet BB-2000 — Best Value Smart Toilet Seat (2026 Price: ~$400–$550)

The BB-2000 is the value champion of the category and a perennial favorite among first-time bidet buyers. It offers a heated seat, adjustable warm water cleansing, a turbo dryer, and a wireless remote—features that cost twice as much elsewhere. The stainless-steel self-cleaning nozzle and side-panel backup controls are practical touches.

It's not as refined as the TOTO: the dryer is weaker, the seat feels less premium, and the remote is basic. But for buyers who want to test the smart toilet waters without a four-figure commitment, it's an outstanding entry point.

**Pros:** Excellent feature-to-price ratio; easy DIY installation on most elongated toilets; wireless remote included.
**Cons:** Weaker dryer; plasticky feel compared to premium seats; fewer advanced hygiene features.

**Verdict:** The smartest first purchase in the category—ideal for renters-friendly upgrades and budget-conscious households.

## Key Features to Consider

Before you buy, understand the differences that separate a good smart toilet from a frustrating one.

| Feature | Why It Matters | Budget Tier (~$400) | Mid Tier (~$1,200) | Premium (~$4,000) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm water cleansing | Core bidet function; adjustable pressure/temperature | Basic, adjustable | Precise, oscillating | Precise, oscillating + pulsating |
| Heated seat | Comfort in winter; energy use varies | Yes | Yes, with memory | Yes, with memory + auto |
| Warm-air dryer | Reduces toilet paper use | Weak | Strong | Strong |
| Auto open/close lid | Hands-free convenience | No | Yes | Yes, motion-activated |
| Self-cleaning nozzle | Hygiene essential | Yes (basic) | Yes (UV/eWater+) | Yes (UV + electrolyzed water) |
| Deodorizer | Odor control | Sometimes | Yes | Yes |
| Remote/app control | Usability | Remote | Remote + app | Remote + app + voice |
| Night light | Safety and convenience | Sometimes | Yes | Yes |
| Water heating | Continuous vs. tank | Tank | Tankless | Tankless |
| Power requirement | GFCI outlet within ~3 ft | Required | Required | Required |

**Our advice:** Prioritize warm water, a heated seat, and a self-cleaning nozzle. The dryer and auto-lid are nice-to-haves; app control is the least important feature for daily use.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Smart Toilet?

- **Hygiene-focused households:** Anyone dealing with mobility limitations, post-surgery recovery, or sensitive skin will find warm-water cleansing genuinely life-improving.
- **Toilet-paper reducers:** Families looking to cut paper waste and recurring costs.
- **Remodelers:** If you're already renovating a bathroom, an integrated unit adds resale appeal.
- **Renters and first-timers:** A bidet seat (like the BB-2000) delivers 80% of the experience for 10% of the price and moves with you.

### Who Should Wait?

- Households without a GFCI outlet near the toilet (add ~$150–$400 for an electrician).
- Anyone in a very short-term living situation.
- Buyers expecting app ecosystems to be as mature as lighting or thermostats—smart toilet apps are functional but basic.

### Budget Considerations (2026 Estimates)

- **Bidet seat:** $300–$1,400
- **Integrated smart toilet:** $2,500–$6,000 (plus installation)
- **Professional installation:** $200–$600 for seats; $500–$1,200 for integrated units
- **Electrical work:** $150–$400 if an outlet is needed

Factor in long-term savings: most households report cutting toilet paper use by 70–80%, which can offset a mid-tier seat within a few years.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Realities

Bidet seats are a DIY-friendly weekend project for most homeowners: remove the existing seat, attach a T-adapter to the water supply, mount the seat, and plug into a GFCI outlet. Expect 30–60 minutes. Integrated toilets are heavier and require proper rough-in measurement (12-inch is standard in the U.S.), a secure floor flange, and often a professional plumber—budget accordingly.

The one hurdle almost everyone hits: **power**. Smart toilets need a grounded GFCI outlet within roughly three feet of the fixture. If you don't have one, plan for an electrician visit before installation day.

### Smart Home Integration

In 2026, integration is improving but still uneven. Premium models from TOTO and Kohler offer companion apps for temperature presets, usage tracking, and firmware updates. Voice control via Alexa and Google Assistant is available on select models—useful for "open lid" commands, though most people default to the remote after week one.

Matter support remains rare in this category; don't buy a smart toilet expecting deep automation scenes. The realistic smart-home value is convenience and personalization, not ecosystem orchestration.
