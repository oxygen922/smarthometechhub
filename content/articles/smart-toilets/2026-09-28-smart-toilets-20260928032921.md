# Smart Toilet Review 2026: Are Bidets With Brains Worth the Splurge?

## Introduction

Let's be honest—few home upgrades sound less glamorous than a "smart toilet." But walk into any modern bathroom showroom in 2026, and you'll see why this category has quietly exploded. Today's smart toilets combine bidet washing, heated seats, warm-air drying, self-cleaning cycles, and app-based controls into a single fixture that replaces your tired old throne entirely. Unlike a bolt-on bidet seat, a true smart toilet integrates the tank, bowl, and electronics into one seamless unit—often with a sleek, skirted design that hides plumbing and looks genuinely luxurious.

I've spent the past several months testing and researching the leading models on the US market, from budget-friendly entry points to flagship Japanese-engineered units. In this review, I'll break down my top three recommendations, the features that actually matter, installation realities, and how these fixtures fit into a modern smart home. If you've been on the fence about whether a smart toilet is worth it in 2026, this guide should settle the question.

---

## Top 3 Smart Toilet Recommendations

### 1. TOTO Neorest NX1 — Best Overall Smart Toilet

**Estimated Price (2026): $8,500–$10,500**

If money were no object, the TOTO Neorest NX1 is the smart toilet I'd recommend without hesitation. TOTO practically invented the modern washlet, and the NX1 represents the pinnacle of that decades-long engineering lineage. The first thing you notice is the design: a low-profile, skirted silhouette with a matte or polished finish that looks more like sculpture than plumbing.

Performance-wise, the NX1 is exceptional. Its **Actilight** self-cleaning technology uses UV light and a titanium dioxide-coated bowl to break down waste and neutralize odors automatically after every use—no scrubbing required. The **Tornado Flush** system delivers a powerful, quiet, and highly water-efficient cycle (as low as 0.8 gallons per flush). The bidet functions include adjustable water temperature, pressure, and a **pre-mist** feature that coats the bowl before use to prevent residue from sticking.

The heated seat is genuinely comfortable, with temperature memory that learns your preferences. The warm-air dryer is one of the most effective I've tested—strong enough that you rarely need paper. Controls are handled via a slim remote and a companion app, with user profiles so multiple family members can save their own settings. The **EWATER+** electrolyzed water system keeps the wand and bowl sanitized between uses.

**Pros:** Unmatched build quality, self-cleaning bowl, whisper-quiet flush, excellent dryer
**Cons:** Eye-watering price, requires a nearby outlet, professional install strongly recommended

**Verdict:** The Neorest NX1 is the smart toilet benchmark. If your budget allows, nothing else matches its combination of hygiene, comfort, and refinement.

---

### 2. Kohler Numera — Best Mid-Range Smart Toilet

**Estimated Price (2026): $3,200–$4,000**

For most US households, the Kohler Numera hits the sweet spot between luxury and practicality. Kohler has been steadily refining its smart toilet lineup, and the Numera benefits from that maturity. It offers a full suite of bidet functions—front and rear wash, adjustable temperature and pressure, oscillating and pulsating modes—plus a heated seat and warm-air dryer.

What sets the Numera apart is its **integration with the Kohler Konnect ecosystem**. You can control the toilet via the Kohler app, set up voice commands through Alexa or Google Assistant, and even receive maintenance alerts. The **dual-flush** system (0.8/1.28 GPF) is efficient and quiet. The seat features a **stainless steel wand** with a self-cleaning function before and after each use, and the bowl uses Kohler's **Revolution Rim** technology for a more thorough, clog-resistant flush.

Installation is more forgiving than the TOTO flagship—Kohler's support network is extensive, and many models are available through major retailers with optional professional installation. The design is modern but slightly less dramatic than the Neorest, which may actually appeal to buyers who want a smart toilet that doesn't scream "spaceship bathroom."

**Pros:** Strong app and voice integration, reliable brand support, efficient dual-flush, reasonable mid-range price
**Cons:** Dryer less powerful than TOTO's, fewer user profiles, plastic remote feels less premium

**Verdict:** The Kohler Numera is the smart toilet I'd recommend to the largest number of buyers. It delivers 85% of the flagship experience for roughly 40% of the price.

### 3. Bio Bidet Bliss BB-2000 (Integrated) — Best Value Smart Toilet

**Estimated Price (2026): $1,800–$2,400**

If you want true smart toilet functionality without crossing the $3,000 threshold, the Bio Bidet Bliss BB-2000 integrated unit is the value champion. Bio Bidet has long been a favorite for affordable bidet seats, and their integrated toilet brings that value proposition to a full fixture.

You still get the essentials: **unlimited warm water** via a tankless heating system, adjustable wash modes (including a feminine wash), heated seat, warm-air dryer, and a **wireless remote** with a wall-mountable cradle. The **deodorizer** uses a carbon filter to neutralize odors, and the **night light** is a thoughtful touch for late-night trips. The bowl features a **siphon jet flush** that's effective and reasonably quiet.

Where the Bliss cuts corners is in materials and refinement. The seat and lid feel lighter-duty than TOTO or Kohler, the dryer is weaker, and the app integration is minimal compared to Kohler Konnect. But at this price, those trade-offs are easy to accept—especially for a first-time smart toilet buyer or a secondary bathroom.

**Pros:** Affordable entry point, unlimited warm water, full feature set, easy remote control
**Cons:** Less premium materials, weaker dryer, limited smart home integration

**Verdict:** The Bliss BB-2000 is the best smart toilet for budget-conscious buyers who still want the full bidet experience.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Smart Toilet?

A smart toilet makes the most sense for:

- **Comfort-seekers** who want a heated seat and warm-water bidet, especially in colder climates.
- **Households reducing toilet paper use**—bidet washing plus a dryer can dramatically cut paper consumption.
- **Mobility-limited users** who benefit from hands-free washing and drying.
- **Smart home enthusiasts** who want another connected device in their ecosystem.
- **Remodelers** upgrading a primary bathroom and willing to invest in a statement fixture.

You should probably skip a smart toilet if you're renting, planning to move within a year, or working with a very tight bathroom budget—a quality bidet seat on an existing toilet delivers much of the benefit for a fraction of the cost.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $500:** Bolt-on bidet seat (not a full smart toilet).
- **$1,500–$2,500:** Entry-level integrated smart toilets like the Bio Bidet Bliss.
- **$3,000–$4,500:** Mid-range models with strong app integration (Kohler Numera).
- **$8,000+:** Flagship Japanese-engineered units (TOTO Neorest).

Don't forget hidden costs: an **electrical outlet near the toilet** (an electrician may charge $150–$400 to install one), potential **floor flange adjustments**, and professional installation ($200–$600 depending on complexity).

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

Installing a smart toilet is more involved than swapping a standard toilet. You'll need:

1. **A grounded GFCI electrical outlet** within reach of the toilet's power cord (typically 3–4 feet).
2. **A standard water supply line** with adequate pressure.
3. **A compatible floor flange** and proper rough-in dimensions (usually 12 inches).

Many manufacturers recommend professional installation, and for flagship units like the Neorest, it's essentially mandatory. Mid-range and value models are more DIY-friendly if you're comfortable with basic plumbing and have the electrical work already done.

On the smart home side, integration varies widely. **Kohler's Konnect platform** is the most mature, supporting Alexa and Google Assistant voice commands, app-based control, and even integration with some home automation hubs. TOTO's app is polished but more self-contained. Budget models often rely solely on a physical remote with no app or voice control.

If smart home integration is a priority, verify compatibility *before* purchase—and check whether the manufacturer's app receives regular updates, since abandoned apps are a common frustration in this category.
