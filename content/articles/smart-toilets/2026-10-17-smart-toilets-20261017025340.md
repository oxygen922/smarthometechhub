# The Best Bidet Seats of 2026: Smart Toilet Upgrades Worth Every Penny

## Introduction

Let's be honest—most of us grew up thinking a bidet seat was a luxury reserved for fancy European hotels. Fast forward to 2026, and the smart bidet seat has become one of the most practical, affordable smart home upgrades you can make. These aren't your grandfather's cold-water attachments. Today's models offer heated seats, warm air dryers, adjustable water pressure, self-cleaning nozzles, and even voice control through Alexa and Google Assistant.

Whether you're remodeling a bathroom or simply want to elevate your daily routine, a bidet seat delivers comfort, hygiene, and long-term savings on toilet paper. In this review, we'll break down the top three bidet seats of 2026, explain the key features that matter, and help you decide which model fits your home and budget. By the end, you'll know exactly what to look for—and which seat deserves a spot on your toilet.

---

## Top 3 Product Recommendations

### 1. TOTO Washlet S7A – Best Overall

**Estimated Price (2026): $1,150–$1,300**

TOTO remains the gold standard in the bidet world, and the Washlet S7A is its most refined residential model to date. The S7A builds on the beloved S550e with a redesigned slim profile, faster heating elements, and an upgraded remote that feels more like a modern smart-home controller than a bathroom accessory.

What sets the S7A apart is its **Activated Ionized Water system**, which uses electrolyzed water to help clean the bowl and reduce the need for harsh chemicals. In our testing, this noticeably cut down on cleaning frequency. The **ewater+** misting keeps the nozzle fresh between uses, and the **pre-mist** feature coats the bowl before you go, reducing sticking and odor.

Comfort-wise, the S7A is exceptional. The heated seat warms up in under 30 seconds, the warm air dryer is genuinely effective (not the weak puff you get on budget models), and the **five spray settings**—including a front wash for feminine hygiene—are all adjustable via the remote or the side panel. The **auto-open/close lid** and **auto-flush** features make it feel like a true smart toilet experience without replacing your entire fixture.

Installation requires a dedicated GFCI outlet near the toilet, and TOTO recommends professional installation for the electrical work. Once set up, it paired instantly with our Alexa routine to raise the seat and turn on the nightlight at sunset. At just over $1,200, the S7A is a serious investment, but for buyers who want the best comfort and build quality, it's hard to beat.

**Pros:** Premium build, activated ionized water cleaning, excellent dryer, auto lid/flush
**Cons:** Premium price, requires nearby outlet, remote-dependent for some functions

---

### 2. Bio Bidet BB-2000 Bliss – Best Value

**Estimated Price (2026): $600–$700**

The Bio Bidet BB-2000 has been a crowd favorite for years, and the 2026 refresh keeps it firmly in the "best value" conversation. For roughly half the price of the TOTO, you get a heated seat, warm water, an adjustable warm air dryer, and a wireless remote—features that used to cost well over $1,000.

The BB-2000 uses a **hybrid heating system** that provides continuous warm water without the tank running cold mid-use. Its **three wash modes** (posterior, feminine, and turbo) cover the essentials, and the **oscillating and pulsating spray** options add a spa-like touch. The stainless steel nozzle self-cleans before and after each use, and the **deodorizer** with a replaceable carbon filter does a surprisingly good job keeping the bathroom fresh.

Where the BB-2000 shows its price point is in the details. The plastic housing feels less premium than TOTO's, the dryer is adequate but not powerful, and the seat takes a little longer to heat. The remote is functional but plasticky. That said, the core experience—warm water, warm seat, clean nozzle—is delivered reliably, and the **side-panel backup controls** mean you're never stranded if the remote battery dies.

Installation is straightforward for a confident DIYer: it mounts to standard elongated toilets, connects to your existing water supply with the included T-adapter, and plugs into a GFCI outlet. It doesn't offer app or voice control, but for buyers who want smart features without the smart-home complexity, that's arguably a plus.

**Pros:** Excellent feature-to-price ratio, hybrid heating, reliable brand support
**Cons:** Less premium materials, no app/voice integration, slower seat heating

### 3. Kohler PureWash E930 – Best Smart Home Integration

**Estimated Price (2026): $900–$1,000**

If you're building a connected home, the Kohler PureWash E930 is the bidet seat that speaks your language. It's the only model in this roundup with native **Kohler Konnect** support, meaning you can control it through the Kohler app, Amazon Alexa, or Google Assistant. We set up a routine that pre-heats the seat at 6 a.m. and another that activates the nightlight when the bathroom lights go off—genuinely useful touches.

Beyond connectivity, the E930 is a strong performer. The **stainless steel wand** offers adjustable position and pressure, with front and rear wash modes and a **massage/oscillate** setting. The heated seat has five temperature levels, and the warm air dryer is comparable to the TOTO's. Kohler's **self-cleaning UV sanitization** cycles the nozzle after each use, adding peace of mind.

The design is sleek and low-profile, available in white and biscuit to match Kohler fixtures. The included remote is wall-mountable and intuitive. The main trade-offs are the price (it sits between the Bio Bidet and TOTO) and the fact that full functionality requires the app—some users may find that friction annoying.

Installation is similar to the others: standard elongated or round mounting, T-adapter for water, and a GFCI outlet. Kohler's customer support is excellent, and the three-year limited warranty is competitive.

**Pros:** Best-in-class smart home integration, UV nozzle sanitization, sleek design
**Cons:** App required for some features, mid-premium price, no auto lid

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Bidet Seat?

Bidet seats appeal to a surprisingly broad range of households:

- **Comfort seekers** who dread cold toilet seats in winter and want a spa-like daily routine.
- **Eco-conscious buyers** looking to cut toilet paper consumption—most users report using 70–80% less paper within a month.
- **People with limited mobility** who benefit from remote controls and hands-free features.
- **Smart home enthusiasts** who want another device integrated into their Alexa or Google routines.
- **Anyone with sensitive skin or hygiene concerns**—warm water cleansing is gentler than paper.

### Who Should Skip It?

If your bathroom lacks a nearby electrical outlet and you're not prepared to hire an electrician, a bidet seat may be more hassle than it's worth. Renters may also want to check with landlords before installing. And if you're on a tight budget, a simple $40 cold-water attachment can deliver basic cleansing—just without the warmth and smart features.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $400:** Cold or tank-heated water, basic controls, no dryer. Fine for testing the waters.
- **$500–$800:** The sweet spot. Hybrid heating, warm air dryer, remote control, solid warranties. The Bio Bidet BB-2000 lives here.
- **$900–$1,300:** Premium comfort and smart integration. The Kohler E930 and TOTO S7A compete here.

Factor in **installation costs** ($150–$300 for an electrician if you need a new outlet) and **consumables** (filters, descaling solution) when budgeting.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### What to Expect During Installation

All three seats in this review mount to standard **elongated toilets** (round models are also available for TOTO and Bio Bidet). The process is similar across the board:

1. **Remove your existing seat** and clean the mounting area.
2. **Install the mounting plate** and T-adapter on your water supply line.
3. **Connect the water hose** to the bidet seat.
4. **Plug into a GFCI outlet**—this is the critical requirement. Most bathrooms don't have an outlet near the toilet, so plan for an electrician visit if needed.
5. **Mount the seat**, power on, and run the self-test.

DIY-friendly homeowners can handle steps 1–3 and 5 in about 45 minutes. The electrical work is where most people call a pro—budget $150–$300.

### Smart Home Integration

- **Kohler PureWash E930** is the clear winner here. Native Alexa and Google Assistant support lets you create routines like "Good morning" (heat seat, turn on nightlight) or "Goodnight" (turn off seat heat). The Kohler Konnect app also provides usage stats and firmware updates.
- **TOTO Washlet S7A** offers limited Alexa compatibility through TOTO's app, but it's not as seamless. Most control happens via the remote.
- **Bio Bidet BB-2000** has no smart home integration, which keeps things simple but limits automation.

For most users, smart integration is a nice-to-have rather than a must. But if you're deep into home automation, the Kohler is the obvious choice.
