# The Best Smart Toilets of 2026: A Complete Buyer's Guide and In-Depth Review

## Introduction

Let's be honest—the humble toilet hasn't changed much in a century. But in 2026, that's finally changing in a big way. Smart toilets have evolved from gimmicky novelties into genuinely useful fixtures that combine bidet functionality, heated seats, self-cleaning systems, and even health monitoring. If you've been curious about upgrading your bathroom, you're in the right place.

In this guide, I'll walk you through the three best smart toilets you can buy right now, break down the features that actually matter, and help you decide whether a smart toilet is worth the investment for your home. Whether you're remodeling a master bath or simply tired of cold seats on winter mornings, there's an option here for nearly every budget. I've tested units across price ranges, and I'll share what I've learned so you can make a confident, informed purchase.

## Top 3 Smart Toilet Recommendations

### 1. TOTO Neorest NX1 — Best Overall Smart Toilet

**Estimated Price (2026): $4,200–$4,800**

TOTO has long been the gold standard in the smart toilet space, and the Neorest NX1 is their current flagship. If you want the absolute best and budget isn't the primary concern, this is the unit to beat.

The NX1 combines a sleek, wall-hugging silhouette with TOTO's legendary Actilight self-cleaning technology, which uses UV light and a titanium dioxide coating to break down waste and bacteria after every use. In practice, this means you clean the bowl far less often—a genuine quality-of-life improvement.

The bidet experience is where the NX1 shines. You get an adjustable, warm-water spray with multiple cleansing modes (front, rear, and a soothing "soft" setting), plus a heated seat with temperature memory. The lid opens automatically via proximity sensors, and the flush is fully hands-free. The integrated tankless design also saves space and delivers a powerful, consistent flush.

**What I liked:** The build quality is exceptional, the self-cleaning genuinely works, and the heated seat warms up quickly. The remote is intuitive.

**What could be better:** The price is steep, and installation typically requires a dedicated electrical outlet near the toilet, which may mean hiring an electrician. Replacement parts and service can also be costly.

**Verdict:** The best smart toilet money can buy in 2026. Ideal for luxury remodels and anyone who wants a hands-off, spa-like bathroom experience.

### 2. Kohler Numera Smart Toilet — Best Mid-Range Value

**Estimated Price (2026): $2,300–$2,800**

Kohler's Numera sits in the sweet spot for buyers who want premium features without flagship pricing. It offers most of what makes smart toilets appealing, wrapped in a clean, modern design that fits a wide range of bathroom aesthetics.

The Numera includes a heated seat, adjustable warm-water bidet with oscillating and pulsating spray options, and a hands-free auto-flush. It also features a built-in nightlight, which sounds trivial until you experience it—no more blinding yourself during midnight trips to the bathroom.

One standout feature is Kohler's integration with its Konnect app, which lets you customize spray intensity, temperature, and seat warmth from your phone. It also supports voice control through Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant, so you can trigger a flush or adjust settings hands-free.

**What I liked:** Excellent feature-to-price ratio, reliable flushing, and the app actually works well. The self-cleaning UV system is a welcome addition at this price.

**What could be better:** The bidet wand requires occasional manual cleaning, and the app setup can be finicky on the first try. It's also slightly bulkier than the TOTO NX1.

**Verdict:** The best balance of features and price for most US households. If you want a smart toilet that feels premium without a luxury price tag, start here.

### 3. Bio Bidet Prodigy Smart Toilet — Best Budget-Friendly Option

**Estimated Price (2026): $1,100–$1,500**

For buyers dipping their toes into the smart toilet world, the Bio Bidet Prodigy delivers surprising value. It's not as refined as the TOTO or Kohler units, but it covers the essentials competently.

You get a heated seat, warm-water bidet with adjustable pressure and temperature, a wireless remote, and an auto-flush function. The tankless design keeps the footprint compact, making it a good fit for smaller bathrooms. Nightlight and deodorizer features round out the package.

**What I liked:** The price is accessible, installation is relatively straightforward, and the core bidet functions work reliably. For a first smart toilet, it's a low-risk entry point.

**What could be better:** The build feels more plasticky, the self-cleaning is less advanced (no UV), and the remote isn't as responsive as premium models. Long-term durability is a question mark compared to TOTO or Kohler.

**Verdict:** A solid starter smart toilet. If you're curious about bidet functionality but not ready to spend thousands, the Prodigy is a smart way to test the waters.

Before you buy, it helps to understand what separates a good smart toilet from a great one. Here's what to prioritize.

### The Features That Actually Matter

**Warm-water bidet and heated seat** are the two features you'll use every single day. Prioritize these above all else. Everything else is a bonus.

**Self-cleaning technology** is the second most impactful feature. UV and electrolyzed-water systems genuinely reduce how often you need to scrub the bowl. If you hate cleaning, this is worth paying for.

**Tankless design** is worth considering if you have limited space or want a consistent, powerful flush. It also eliminates the "running toilet" problems that plague traditional tanks.

**App and voice control** are nice-to-haves. They're genuinely useful for customizing settings, but I wouldn't base a purchase decision on them alone.

**Health monitoring** is an emerging category—some 2026 models can analyze urine for hydration and other basic metrics. It's promising but still early. Don't buy solely for this feature yet.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Smart Toilet?

Smart toilets make the most sense for:

- **Homeowners remodeling a bathroom** who want to add long-term value and comfort.
- **Anyone with mobility challenges** who benefits from hands-free operation and bidet hygiene.
- **People who dislike cold seats and manual cleaning** — the daily quality-of-life gains are real.
- **Households in colder climates** where a heated seat is a genuine luxury.

You might want to skip a smart toilet if:

- You're renting and can't modify the bathroom.
- Your bathroom lacks a nearby electrical outlet and you can't add one.
- You're on a tight budget and a $200 bidet seat attachment would meet your needs.

### Budget Considerations

Smart toilets span a wide range. Here's how to think about spending:

- **Under $1,500:** Entry-level models like the Bio Bidet Prodigy. You get the essentials but fewer refinements.
- **$2,000–$3,000:** The sweet spot. Kohler's Numera and similar models offer premium features without flagship pricing.
- **$4,000+:** Flagship territory. TOTO's Neorest line delivers the best build quality, self-cleaning, and longevity.

Don't forget **installation costs**. A smart toilet typically requires a dedicated GFCI electrical outlet near the fixture. If you don't have one, budget $150–$400 for an electrician. Professional installation of the toilet itself runs $200–$500 depending on your market.

### Long-Term Value

A quality smart toilet should last 10–15 years. When you amortize a $2,500 unit over a decade, that's roughly $20 per month for daily comfort and reduced cleaning. Framed that way, mid-range models offer compelling value.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### What Installation Involves

Installing a smart toilet is more involved than swapping a traditional one. Here's the typical process:

1. **Check your electrical setup.** You need a GFCI outlet within reach of the toilet's power cord (usually 3–4 feet). If you don't have one, hire an electrician first.
2. **Measure your rough-in.** The distance from the wall to the floor drain center must match the toilet's specification (commonly 12 inches in the US).
3. **Shut off the water supply** and remove the old toilet.
4. **Install the new unit**, connecting the water line and plugging in the power.
5. **Configure settings** via the remote or app.

Most manufacturers provide detailed instructions, and many offer professional installation. If you're not comfortable with plumbing and electrical work, hire a pro—it's worth it.

### Smart Home Integration

Modern smart toilets increasingly play well with broader smart home ecosystems:

- **Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant:** Voice control for flushing, bidet activation, and seat warming on supported models like the Kohler Numera.
- **Mobile apps:** Customize spray intensity, temperature, and seat settings. Some apps let you save profiles for multiple users.
- **Matter support:** A growing number of 2026 models support the Matter standard, improving cross-platform compatibility with Apple Home, SmartThings, and others.
- **Health data:** Some premium models can sync basic health metrics to companion apps, though this ecosystem is still maturing.

**A note on privacy:** If a smart toilet collects health data, review the manufacturer's privacy policy. Data is typically stored locally or in the vendor's cloud—know where yours goes.
