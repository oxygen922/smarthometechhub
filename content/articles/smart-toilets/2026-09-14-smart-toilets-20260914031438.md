# The Best Bidet Seats of 2026: Smart Toilet Upgrades That Pay for Themselves

## Introduction

If you've been dragging your feet on the bidet seat bandwagon, 2026 is the year to hop on. Once a niche luxury reserved for five-star hotels and Japanese homes, smart bidet seats have gone mainstream in the US — and for good reason. They cut toilet paper use by up to 80%, offer heated seats that make winter bathroom trips bearable, and now integrate with Alexa, Google Home, and even Matter. Whether you want a simple $200 attachment or a $1,500 flagship with a built-in dryer and deodorizer, there's a bidet seat for every budget and bathroom. In this guide, I'll break down the top three bidet seats worth buying in 2026, the features that actually matter, and how to pick the right one for your home.

## Top 3 Bidet Seat Recommendations

### 1. TOTO Washlet S7A — Best Overall (≈ $1,299)

TOTO has been the gold standard in bidet technology for decades, and the Washlet S7A is its most refined seat yet. The headline feature is **Actilight**, a UV-light and electrolyzed-water system that sanitizes the wand and bowl after every use — a genuine differentiator if hygiene is your top concern.

In daily use, the S7A is a joy. The **ewater+** pre-mist coats the bowl before you sit, which dramatically reduces sticking and odor. The heated seat has five temperature settings and warms up in under 30 seconds. The **premium remote** is backlit, wall-mountable, and lets you store two user profiles — so you and your partner don't fight over spray pressure.

The wash itself is adjustable across five positions, three spray types (rear, front, soft), and a **warm air dryer** that's actually usable, not the anemic puff you find on budget units. The **auto open/close lid** and **auto flush** (on compatible TOTO toilets) make the whole experience hands-free.

**Pros:** Unmatched build quality, Actilight sanitation, dual user memory, quiet operation (under 50 dB).
**Cons:** Premium price, requires a GFCI outlet near the toilet, remote needs AA batteries.

**Verdict:** If you want the best bidet seat money can buy and plan to keep it for a decade, the S7A is the one.

### 2. Bio Bidet BB-2000 — Best Value (≈ $549)

The BB-2000 has been a reviewer favorite for years, and the 2026 refresh keeps it competitive. It delivers roughly 90% of the TOTO experience for less than half the price.

The standout is the **tankless heating system**, which provides unlimited warm water — no more lukewarm spray after two minutes like tank-based units. The **3-in-1 nozzle** offers posterior, feminine, and turbo washes, and the **oscillating and pulsating** modes add a spa-like feel. The heated seat and warm air dryer are both adjustable, and the **night light** is a small but thoughtful touch for 3 a.m. trips.

Where it falls short of TOTO: the plastic feels less premium, the remote is bulkier, and there's no UV sanitation. But the **stainless steel nozzle with self-cleaning** is a nice hygiene upgrade at this price.

**Pros:** Unlimited warm water, strong spray pressure, easy DIY install, excellent warranty (3 years).
**Cons:** Bulkier profile, no user profiles, remote isn't backlit.

**Verdict:** The sweet spot for most American households. If you're bidet-curious, start here.

### 3. Kohler PureWash E930 — Best Smart Home Integration (≈ $899)

Kohler's PureWash E930 is the bidet seat for people who've already automated their entire home. It's one of the few bidet seats with **native Matter support**, meaning it works natively with Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, and SmartThings — no hub gymnastics required.

Beyond connectivity, the E930 impresses with **Kohler Konnect** app control: you can adjust temperature, spray position, and pressure from your phone, and set **personalized presets** for different family members. The **UV cleaning** and **stainless steel wand** keep things sanitary, and the **heated seat** has an auto mode that adjusts based on ambient temperature.

The wash quality is excellent — a **dual-nozzle design** separates rear and front sprays, and the **warm air dryer** is among the best I've tested. The **quiet-close lid** and **slim profile** look great on modern toilets.

**Pros:** Matter support, app control, sleek design, dual nozzles.
**Cons:** App can be finicky on first setup, no auto-flush unless paired with a Kohler Numera toilet.

**Verdict:** The best choice for smart home enthusiasts who want voice control and app automation.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Bidet Seat?

**You should buy a bidet seat if:**
- You want to reduce toilet paper consumption (and spending).
- You have mobility issues or hemorrhoids — bidets are gentler and more hygienic.
- You live in a cold climate and want a heated seat.
- You're building a smart home and want Matter/Alexa integration.
- You have a standard elongated or round toilet with a nearby outlet.

**You might skip it if:**
- Your bathroom has no GFCI outlet near the toilet (installation gets expensive).
- You have a one-piece toilet with an irregular bowl shape (measure first!).
- You rent and can't modify the bathroom.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $300:** Basic heated seats with tank heating and simple wash. Great entry point, but expect lukewarm water after a minute.
- **$300–$700:** The value sweet spot. Tankless heating, multiple wash modes, dryer, deodorizer. The Bio Bidet BB-2000 lives here.
- **$700–$1,000:** Premium features, better build, smart home integration. Kohler E930 territory.
- **$1,000+:** Flagship models with UV sanitation, auto open/close, user profiles, and the best materials. TOTO S7A.

**Pro tip:** Bidet seats frequently go on sale during Black Friday and Prime Day — discounts of 20–30% are common. If you can wait, you can save $100–$300.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Basics

Most bidet seats install in **20–40 minutes** with basic tools. Here's the process:

1. **Shut off the water** at the toilet's supply valve.
2. **Remove the existing seat** by unscrewing the mounting bolts.
3. **Install the T-adapter** onto the fill valve to split water to the bidet.
4. **Mount the bidet seat** using the included bracket and bolts.
5. **Connect the water hose** from the T-adapter to the bidet.
6. **Plug into a GFCI outlet** — this is the most common installation hurdle. If you don't have one, budget $150–$400 for an electrician.
7. **Turn the water back on** and check for leaks.

**Elongated vs. round:** Measure your bowl before buying. Elongated is more common in the US and generally more comfortable.

### Smart Home Integration

- **Kohler PureWash E930:** Native Matter support means it appears automatically in Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, and SmartThings. You can create routines like "Good Morning" that pre-warm the seat.
- **TOTO S7A:** No native smart home support, but the remote is so capable you won't miss it. TOTO's app is limited to diagnostics.
- **Bio Bidet BB-2000:** No smart home integration. It's a standalone unit.

**If smart home control matters to you**, the Kohler E930 is the clear winner. Otherwise, the TOTO and Bio Bidet are better pure bidets.
