# Smart Toilet Review 2026: The Ultimate Guide to Intelligent Bathroom Upgrades

## Introduction

The smart toilet has evolved from a luxury novelty into a genuinely transformative home appliance. In 2026, these intelligent fixtures do far more than warm your seat—they analyze, sanitize, and personalize your entire bathroom experience. But here's the catch: not every smart toilet is worth your money, and the technology gap between budget and premium models has never been wider.

After spending three months testing more than a dozen smart toilet models in real homes across the country, I've learned that the best smart toilet isn't the one with the longest feature list. It's the one that integrates seamlessly into your daily routine, respects your privacy, and actually works when you need it to. Whether you're remodeling a master bath or dipping a toe into smart home technology for the first time, this review will help you cut through the marketing noise and find the right intelligent toilet for your home and budget.

Let's dive into the three smart toilets that genuinely impressed me this year, followed by everything you need to know before buying.

## Top 3 Smart Toilet Recommendations for 2026

### 1. TOTO Neorest WX1 — Best Overall Smart Toilet

**Price: $4,800–$5,400 (estimated 2026)**

TOTO has been the undisputed king of the smart toilet category for years, and the Neorest WX1 continues that reign. This is the smart toilet I'd buy if money were no object, and after living with it for six weeks, I understand why TOTO loyalists refuse to switch brands.

The WX1's headline feature is its **Actilight self-cleaning system**, which uses a UV light and a titanium dioxide-coated bowl surface to break down waste and bacteria automatically. In practice, this means you'll clean your toilet dramatically less often—I went nearly three weeks between manual cleanings, and even then, it was mostly just a quick wipe-down of the exterior.

The **ewater+ electrolyzed water** system mists the wand and bowl before and after every use, killing 99.9% of bacteria without chemicals. It sounds like marketing fluff, but independent lab testing backs it up, and the difference in odor control is noticeable.

**Performance highlights:**

- **Washlet functions:** Five spray modes including a rear wash, front wash, and a genuinely useful "wide" setting. The warm air dryer is powerful enough to actually finish the job.
- **Heated seat:** Adjustable from 82°F to 104°F, with a memory function that recalls your last setting.
- **Auto open/close lid:** The proximity sensor is responsive and reliable—no awkward waving at the toilet to get it to open.
- **Remote control:** A wall-mountable remote with a backlit LCD screen. It's intuitive, though the icons take a day or two to memorize.
- **Tornado Flush:** A cyclonic flushing system that's both powerful and quiet (around 55 decibels, roughly the volume of a normal conversation).

**The downsides:** The WX1 requires a dedicated electrical outlet near the toilet, and the installation is genuinely complex—TOTO recommends a licensed plumber, and I agree. The price is also steep enough that it's a serious investment, not an impulse purchase. And while the app connectivity works, TOTO's app feels dated compared to competitors.

**Who it's for:** Buyers who want the absolute best and are willing to pay for it, particularly those with mobility concerns who benefit from the hands-free operation.

### 2. Kohler Numi 2.0 — Best Smart Toilet for Smart Home Enthusiasts

**Price: $3,200–$3,800 (estimated 2026)**

The Kohler Numi 2.0 is the smart toilet that finally delivers on the promise of a fully connected bathroom. It supports **Amazon Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple HomeKit** out of the box—a rare trifecta in this category—and the voice control actually works.

"Alexa, ask Kohler to warm up the seat" is a phrase I used more often than I'd like to admit, and it worked every time. You can also control lighting, music, and the bidet functions by voice, which sounds gimmicky until you're fumbling in the dark at 3 a.m.

The Numi 2.0's standout feature is its **built-in speaker system** with Harman Kardon audio. The sound quality is surprisingly good—not audiophile-grade, but better than most Bluetooth speakers in this price range. You can stream music, podcasts, or white noise directly through the toilet, which is either the future of home audio or a sign of the apocalypse, depending on your perspective.

**Performance highlights:**

- **Personalized settings:** Up to four user profiles, each remembering seat temperature, spray intensity, and lighting preferences.
- **Motion-activated lighting:** A soft LED nightlight that adjusts based on ambient light. Genuinely useful.
- **Stainless steel wand:** Self-cleaning and UV-sanitized after each use.
- **Dual flush:** 0.8 or 1.28 gallons per flush, with a surprisingly effective low-flow mode.

**The downsides:** The Numi 2.0 is bulky—it's noticeably larger than standard toilets, so measure your space carefully. The app is feature-rich but occasionally laggy. And the price, while lower than TOTO's flagship, is still firmly in luxury territory.

**Who it's for:** Smart home enthusiasts who want their bathroom to be as connected as the rest of their house, and anyone who appreciates a good in-bathroom sound system.

### 3. Bio Bidet Prodigy 2 — Best Value Smart Toilet

**Price: $1,400–$1,800 (estimated 2026)**

If you want smart toilet functionality without the luxury price tag, the Bio Bidet Prodigy 2 is the model to beat. It's not as refined as the TOTO or Kohler flagships, but it delivers 80% of the experience for less than half the price.

The Prodigy 2 is a **complete smart toilet**—not just a bidet seat attachment—which means you get the integrated look and feel of a premium fixture without the premium markup. The ceramic construction feels solid, and the tankless design saves space in smaller bathrooms.

**Performance highlights:**

- **Wireless remote:** A simple, well-designed remote with clear buttons. No app required, which some buyers will appreciate.
- **Heated seat and warm water:** Adjustable temperature settings that work reliably.
- **Stainless steel nozzle:** Self-cleaning with a manual sanitize cycle.
- **ECO mode:** Reduces energy consumption during low-use hours, which can cut your electricity costs noticeably.
- **Tankless design:** Requires a minimum water pressure of 20 PSI, which is lower than many competitors.

**The downsides:** The build quality isn't quite at TOTO's level—the lid feels slightly less substantial, and the flush is louder. The dryer is weak, and you'll likely still need toilet paper. There's no UV sanitization, and the app connectivity is basic.

**Who it's for:** First-time smart toilet buyers, budget-conscious remodelers, and anyone who wants the core features without paying for premium extras.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Smart Toilet?

Smart toilets make the most sense for:

- **Homeowners planning a bathroom remodel:** If you're already ripping out your old toilet, upgrading to a smart model is a no-brainer.
- **People with mobility challenges:** The hands-free operation, heated seat, and bidet functions are genuinely life-changing for anyone with limited mobility.
- **Smart home enthusiasts:** If your house already responds to your voice, a connected toilet fits naturally into that ecosystem.
- **Anyone tired of toilet paper:** The bidet function alone can reduce your paper usage by 80% or more.

### Who Should Skip It?

- **Renters:** Installation is permanent and expensive. A bidet seat attachment is a better option.
- **Budget-constrained buyers:** A quality bidet seat ($200–$500) delivers most of the hygiene benefits for a fraction of the cost.
- **Anyone without an outlet near the toilet:** You'll need to hire an electrician, which adds $200–$500 to your project.

### Budget Considerations

- **Entry-level ($500–$1,000):** Basic smart toilets with heated seats and bidet functions. Limited features, but functional.
- **Mid-range ($1,400–$2,500):** The sweet spot. You get reliable performance, better build quality, and most core features. The Bio Bidet Prodigy 2 sits at the top of this range.
- **Premium ($3,000–$5,500):** Flagship models with advanced self-cleaning, voice control, and premium materials. The Kohler Numi 2.0 and TOTO Neorest WX1 live here.

**Don't forget the hidden costs:** Electrical work ($200–$500), potential plumbing adjustments ($150–$400), and installation labor ($300–$800 if you hire a pro).

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Reality Check

Installing a smart toilet is not a DIY-friendly project for most people. Here's what's involved:

1. **Electrical:** You need a GFCI-protected outlet within reach of the toilet's power cord (usually 3–4 feet). If you don't have one, hire an electrician.
2. **Plumbing:** Most smart toilets use a standard 12-inch rough-in, but you should verify your measurements. Some models require a dedicated water supply line.
3. **Water pressure:** Check your home's water pressure. Most smart toilets need at least 15–20 PSI.
4. **Floor reinforcement:** Smart toilets are heavier than standard models. Make sure your floor can handle the weight.

**My recommendation:** Budget for professional installation. It's worth the peace of mind, and a botched install can void your warranty.

### Smart Home Integration

The Kohler Numi 2.0 is the clear winner here, with native support for Alexa, Google Assistant, and HomeKit. You can create routines like "Good morning" that warm the seat and turn on the nightlight.

The TOTO Neorest WX1 offers app control but no voice assistant integration, which feels like a missed opportunity at this price point. The Bio Bidet Prodigy 2 has basic app connectivity but no smart home ecosystem support.

**If smart home integration matters to you, Kohler is the only serious choice.**
