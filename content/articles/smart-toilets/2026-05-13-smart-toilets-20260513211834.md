### 1. Toto S7 Washlet+ (2026 Edition) – The Premium Powerhouse

Toto continues to dominate the high-end bidet market, and the S7 Washlet+ is their most refined offering yet. **Pros:** Unmatched build quality, health-monitoring sensors (urine analysis via a future firmware update), and a self-cleaning nozzle that sanitizes after every use.
**Cons:** Premium price point; installation requires a GFCI outlet (not included); the seat is slightly bulky for smaller toilets.

### 2. Brondell Swash 1400 – The Best Value for Tech Lovers

**Price:** $599 (MSRP)

Brondell has carved out a reputation for delivering high-end features at mid-range prices, and the Swash 1400 is their 2026 flagship. This model introduces “SmartSit” technology—a pressure sensor that automatically adjusts water temperature and spray intensity based on your weight and seating position. It’s a gimmick? Surprisingly, no. The algorithm adapts quickly and makes the experience feel truly personalized, especially for households with multiple users.

**Performance:** The Swash 1400 offers a wide, heated seat with an ergonomic design that fits most elongated toilets. Its “Wide Wash” mode uses a fan-shaped spray for a gentler experience, while the “Target Wash” focuses on precise cleaning. The remote includes a color display (a first for this price point) and supports Bluetooth for app-based customization. Smart home integration is robust: it works with Alexa and Google Home, but notably lacks Apple HomeKit support. The app allows you to set user profiles, track water usage, and even schedule a “warm-up” cycle before you wake up.

**Pros:** Excellent value for features; SmartSit technology is genuinely useful; easy-to-clean removable nozzle; five-year warranty.
**Cons:** No health monitoring; the app can be glitchy on older smartphones; installation instructions could be clearer.

**Best For:** Tech-savvy households that want advanced features without breaking the bank, particularly families with multiple users.

**Pros:** Ultra-slim profile; beautiful design; quiet operation; easy installation (no special tools required).
**Cons:** Fewer customization options than competitors; no health features; limited voice control.

**Best For:** Design-conscious buyers who prioritize a minimalist look and don’t need the most advanced smart features.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy a Bidet Seat?

- **Eco-conscious consumers:** Bidet seats reduce toilet paper usage by 80-90%, saving trees and reducing plumbing clogs. If sustainability is a priority, any model on this list will help.
- **Health-focused individuals:** The Toto S7’s health monitoring and Brondell’s SmartSit technology can assist with conditions like hemorrhoids, IBS, or postpartum recovery.
- **Tech enthusiasts:** If you already have a smart home ecosystem, look for models with broad integration (Alexa + Google + Apple). The Toto S7 is the most future-proof.
- **Budget-minded buyers:** The Brondell Swash 1400 offers 90% of the premium experience for half the price. Skip the Kohler if budget is tight—it’s a design piece, not a value leader.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $400:** You’ll find basic models from brands like Bio Bidet or Luxe, but they lack heated seats and smart features. Pass unless you’re on a strict budget.
- **$400–$800:** Sweet spot for most buyers. The Brondell Swash 1400 ($599) is the best-in-class here. You get heated seats, adjustable spray, and smart home support.
- **$800–$1,500:** Premium territory. The Toto S7 is the gold standard, but consider the Kohler E930 if design matters more than tech features.

**Pro Tip:** Factor in installation costs ($150–$300 if you hire a plumber). Most DIY-ready models include a T-valve and hoses, but electrical work (GFCI outlet) may add $200–$500 if not already present.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Overview

Installing a bidet seat is a DIY-friendly project for most homeowners, but it requires careful preparation. Here’s the process in four steps:

1. **Turn off water supply** and flush the toilet to empty the tank.
2. **Remove the existing toilet seat** (usually two bolts).
3. **Install the T-valve** onto the toilet’s water supply line, then connect the bidet’s hose.
4. **Mount the bidet seat** onto the toilet bowl using the provided brackets and bolts. Plug into a GFCI outlet.

**Time required:** 30–60 minutes for most models. The Kohler E930 is the easiest due to its slim design; the Toto S7 may take longer due to the eWater+ system.

**Common pitfalls:** Ensure your toilet has an electrical outlet within 3 feet (code requirement). If not, hire an electrician. Also, check that your toilet’s shape (round vs. elongated) matches the bidet seat—most modern models are elongated.

### Smart Home Integration

All three top models support voice control, but the depth varies:

- **Toto S7:** Full integration via Apple HomeKit (Siri), Alexa, and Google. You can create routines like “Good Morning” that pre-heats the seat and runs the deodorizer.
- **Brondell Swash 1400:** Works with Alexa and Google, but no Apple. The app allows for user profiles, so each family member can have their own settings.
- **Kohler PureWash E930:** Limited to basic commands. For example, “Alexa, turn on the bidet” works, but you can’t adjust spray temperature by voice.

**Future-proofing tip:** Look for Matter compatibility (a new smart home standard). The Toto S7 is confirmed to support Matter via a firmware update in late 2026, which will ensure interoperability with any future smart home ecosystem.
