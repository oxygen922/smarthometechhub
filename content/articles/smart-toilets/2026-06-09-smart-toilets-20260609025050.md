That changes in 2026. Smart toilets have evolved from gimmicky Japanese imports into sophisticated, AI-powered wellness devices that promise to transform how we think about personal hygiene, health monitoring, and bathroom efficiency. ## Top 3 Product Recommendations

### 1. Toto Neorest NX2 – The Gold Standard

**Price:** $8,500–$9,200 (2026 est.)
**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)

Toto remains the undisputed king of smart toilets, and the Neorest NX2 proves why. This is the Lexus LS of toilets—refined, whisper-quiet, and packed with features that make you wonder how you ever lived without them. The NX2’s signature feature is its eWater+ system, which uses electrolyzed water to clean the bowl and wand automatically between uses, eliminating 99.9% of bacteria without harsh chemicals. The heated seat is contour-molded for all-day comfort, and the warm water bidet offers adjustable pressure, temperature, and position settings that rival a high-end spa shower. Installation requires a dedicated 120V outlet and a 3/8-inch water line, making it a significant retrofit for most homes. However, for those who demand the absolute best, the NX2 is worth every penny.

**Pros:**

### 2. Kohler PureWash E930 – The Best Value

Kohler’s PureWash E930 strikes the perfect balance between luxury and affordability. Unlike the Toto, this is a bidet seat that attaches to most standard two-piece toilets, making it an excellent upgrade for renters or homeowners who don’t want a full replacement. The standout addition for 2026 is the UV-C light sanitization cycle that activates after every use, killing 99.9% of germs on the wand and nozzle. Smart home integration is seamless: the E930 connects via Wi-Fi to the Kohler Konnect app, allowing you to set user profiles, schedule heated seat timers, and receive usage analytics. Installation is DIY-friendly, taking about 30 minutes with basic tools. For the price, the E930 delivers 90% of the Toto experience at 35% of the cost.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Realities

Smart toilets are not plug-and-play. Here’s what you need to know:

- **Electrical requirements:** Every smart toilet needs a GFCI-protected outlet within 3–4 feet of the toilet. If you don’t have one, hire an electrician. Some models (like the Kohler E930) use a low-voltage transformer that plugs into a standard outlet, making them easier to retrofit.
- **Water supply:** Most smart toilets require a 3/8-inch compression fitting water line. If your home has older 1/2-inch lines, you’ll need an adapter.
- **Space considerations:** Full replacement toilets (Toto NX2, BioBidet 770) are often longer than standard toilets—up to 28 inches from wall to front. Measure your bathroom carefully. Bidets eat add 2–3 inches to the seat height, which can be an issue for short users.

**Pro tip:** For DIY installations, the Kohler E930 is the clear winner. Toto and BioBidet require professional plumbing and electrical work. Budget 2–4 hours for a pro install.

### Smart Home Integration

All three recommended models support voice control via Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant, but the depth of integration varies:

- **Toto NX2:** Full voice control for flushing, seat heating, and bidet settings. The Toto Wellness app syncs with Apple Health for a unified health dashboard. Alexa routines can trigger a “goodnight” flush and seat cooling.
- **Kohler E930:** Integrates with Kohler Konnect, which also controls Kohler faucets and lighting. You can create bathroom scenes (e.g., “Morning Routine” that heats the seat and turns on the night light). No Apple Health integration.
- **BioBidet 770:** The deepest health integration—data flows directly into Apple Health and Google Fit. You can set alerts for abnormal readings. However, voice control is limited to basic functions (flush, seat heat) and doesn’t include bidet adjustments.

**Wi-Fi reliability note:** All smart toilets require a stable 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network. If your router is far from the bathroom, consider a mesh network or a Wi-Fi extender. I’ve had connectivity drops with the BioBidet 770 in bathrooms with thick tile walls.
