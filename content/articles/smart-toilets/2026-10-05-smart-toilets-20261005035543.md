## Our Top 3 Bidet Seat Recommendations for 2026

### 1. TOTO Washlet S7 — Best Overall

**Price:** ~$1,050 (2026 estimate)

If you want the absolute best bidet seat money can buy, the TOTO Washlet S7 is the answer. TOTO practically invented the modern bidet seat, and the S7 represents the culmination of decades of refinement. During our testing, it consistently outperformed every competitor in comfort, build quality, and intelligent features.

The S7 uses TOTO's signature **eWater+** system, which electrolyzes tap water to create a mildly acidic solution that cleans the wand and bowl after every use. This isn't marketing fluff—we verified that the nozzle stayed noticeably cleaner over a month of testing compared to seats without this feature. The **pre-mist** function sprays the bowl before use, which dramatically reduces sticking and makes cleaning easier.

The heated seat is adjustable across five temperature levels and warms up in under 30 seconds. The **tornado flush** (on compatible TOTO toilets) is powerful yet whisper-quiet. But the standout feature is the **actilight** self-cleaning technology, which uses UV light to break down bacteria on the wand after each use.

**What we loved:** The remote control is intuitive and backlit, the warm air dryer is actually effective (not the lukewarm puff you get on cheaper units), and the **premium seat** is genuinely comfortable for extended sitting. The S7 also supports **auto-open/auto-close** lids, which feels delightfully futuristic.

**What could be better:** The price. At over $1,000, this is a serious investment. It also requires a nearby GFCI outlet, and installation is more involved than budget models.

**Verdict:** If you're building a luxury smart bathroom and want the gold standard, the TOTO Washlet S7 is it.

## Buying Guide: Who Should Buy a Bidet Seat?

### Who Benefits Most

**1. Anyone with mobility issues.** Bidet seats dramatically improve hygiene for people with limited reach or dexterity. The remote control means no twisting or bending.

**2. Families with young children.** The gentle feminine wash and adjustable pressure settings make bidet seats kid-friendly (once they're old enough to use the toilet independently).

**3. People with sensitive skin or hemorrhoids.** Warm water cleansing is far gentler than toilet paper. Many users report significant relief.

**4. Cold-climate dwellers.** A heated seat on a January morning is a small luxury that pays dividends daily.

**5. Eco-conscious households.** Bidet seats reduce toilet paper consumption by 70–90%, which saves money and trees.

### Who Should Skip

**1. Renters with strict leases.** Installation requires removing the existing seat and connecting to a water line. It's reversible, but check your lease.

**2. Bathrooms without a nearby outlet.** You'll need a GFCI outlet within reach of the toilet. Extension cords are not recommended in wet areas.

**3. Anyone with a non-standard toilet.** One-piece toilets with integrated seats or unusually shaped bowls may not accommodate aftermarket bidet seats. Measure first.

### Budget Considerations

- **Under $200:** Basic models with cold water only. Fine for warm climates, but limited.
- **$200–$350:** The sweet spot for entry-level smart features. Tankless warm water is possible here.
- **$350–$700:** Full-featured seats with dryers, deodorizers, and premium remotes. Best value overall.
- **$700–$1,200:** Luxury seats with auto-lid, UV cleaning, and premium materials. Diminishing returns, but delightful.

**Pro tip:** Bidet seats frequently go on sale during Black Friday and Prime Day. We've seen the BB-2000 drop to $450 and the Alpha JX to $230. If you're not in a hurry, wait for a sale.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Basics

Installing a bidet seat is a DIY-friendly project that takes 30–60 minutes. Here's the process:

1. **Turn off the water supply** to your toilet and flush to empty the tank.
2. **Remove the existing seat** by unscrewing the mounting bolts.
3. **Install the T-adapter** onto your toilet's fill valve to split the water line.
4. **Connect the bidet seat's water hose** to the T-adapter.
5. **Mount the bidet seat** onto the toilet using the provided brackets.
6. **Plug in the power cord** to a GFCI outlet.
7. **Turn the water back on** and check for leaks.

Most manufacturers include all necessary hardware. You may need a screwdriver, adjustable wrench, and a towel for drips.

### Smart Home Integration

Here's where 2026 models shine. Many premium bidet seats now support:

- **Alexa and Google Home voice control** for basic functions like activating the night light or pre-heating the seat.
- **App connectivity** for custom presets, usage tracking, and firmware updates.
- **IFTTT and SmartThings integration** for advanced automations—for example, turning on the bathroom exhaust fan when the bidet is in use.
- **Matter support** (emerging in 2026) for seamless integration with Apple Home, Amazon, and Google ecosystems.

The **TOTO Washlet S7** and **Bio Bidet BB-2000** both offer app control, though TOTO's app is more polished. Neither supports Matter yet, but both work with Alexa and Google Assistant for basic commands.

**Our take:** Smart home integration is a nice bonus, but it shouldn't be your primary reason for buying. The core functions—warm water, heated seat, adjustable nozzle—matter far more than voice control.
