| Feature | Why It Matters | What to Look For |

| **CADR** | Measures how quickly the purifier cleans air | Higher is better; match to room size (CADR should be at least 2/3 of room sq ft) |

| **Filter Replacement Cost** | Ongoing expense | Budget $30-100 annually depending on model |

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Air Purifier?

Air purifiers benefit nearly everyone, but they're especially valuable for:

- **Allergy sufferers:** If you wake up congested or sneeze constantly during pollen season, a HEPA purifier can dramatically reduce symptoms.
- **Pet owners:** Dander and fur are no match for a quality purifier with a pre-filter.
- **Urban dwellers:** City air contains higher levels of PM2.5, VOCs, and other pollutants.
- **Wildfire-prone regions:** During smoke events, an air purifier can create a safe indoor haven.
- **Parents of young children:** Kids are more susceptible to airborne pollutants.
- **Home office workers:** Clean air improves cognitive function and reduces fatigue.

### Budget Considerations

**Under $200:** Expect solid performance for small-to-medium rooms. Brands like Levoit and Winix offer excellent value. You'll sacrifice some sensor accuracy and build quality.

**$200-$500:** This sweet spot offers the best balance of performance, coverage, and smart features. Coway, Blueair, and Honeywell dominate here.

**$500+:** Premium models from Dyson, IQAir, and Molekule offer superior build quality, advanced sensors, and often additional functionality (heating, cooling, etc.).

**Ongoing Costs:** Don't forget filter replacements. A $200 purifier with $80 annual filters costs more over five years than a $400 purifier with $40 filters. Factor this into your decision.

### Sizing Your Purifier

A common mistake is buying a purifier that's too small for the room. As a rule of thumb:

- **Small rooms (up to 300 sq ft):** CADR of 100-150
- **Medium rooms (300-600 sq ft):** CADR of 150-250
- **Large rooms (600-1,000 sq ft):** CADR of 250-350
- **Open floor plans (1,000+ sq ft):** CADR of 350+ or multiple units

Remember: manufacturers often quote coverage based on a single air exchange per hour. For effective purification, you want four to five air exchanges per hour, which means the real-world coverage is often half of what's advertised.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setting Up Your Air Purifier

Installation is refreshingly simple for most modern air purifiers:

1. **Unbox and remove all packaging:** Don't forget to remove the plastic wrap around the filters—a common mistake.
2. **Choose the right location:** Place the purifier at least 6 inches from walls and furniture to ensure proper airflow. Avoid corners.
3. **Install the app:** Download the manufacturer's app (MyDyson, IoCare, VeSync, etc.) and create an account.
4. **Connect to Wi-Fi:** Most purifiers require a 2.4GHz network. If your router broadcasts both 2.4GHz and 5GHz, you may need to separate them temporarily.
5. **Pair with voice assistants:** Enable the appropriate skill in Alexa or Google Home and link your account.

### Smart Home Integration Tips

- **Automate based on air quality:** Using IFTTT or native app automation, you can trigger other devices when air quality drops. For example, turn on a smart fan or send a notification.
- **Schedule modes:** Set the purifier to run on high during the day and switch to sleep mode at night.
- **Monitor remotely:** Check air quality while you're away and pre-clean the house before you return.
- **Integrate with HVAC:** Some premium purifiers can communicate with smart thermostats to coordinate whole-home air management.

### Compatibility Notes

- **Apple HomeKit:** Limited support. Dyson is the most reliable option for HomeKit users.
- **Amazon Alexa:** Widely supported across all major brands.
- **Google Assistant:** Also widely supported, though occasionally less responsive than Alexa.
- **Samsung SmartThings:** Growing support, but check compatibility before purchasing.
