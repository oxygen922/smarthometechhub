## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Air Purifier?

Air purifiers are beneficial for a wide range of households, but they're especially valuable for:

- **Allergy sufferers:** If you deal with seasonal allergies, pollen, or dust mites, a HEPA air purifier can significantly reduce symptoms.
- **Pet owners:** Pet dander and odors are common triggers. Look for units with strong carbon filtration.
- **Residents in wildfire-prone areas:** During wildfire season, PM2.5 levels can spike dramatically. A high-CADR purifier is essential.
- **Urban dwellers:** If you live near highways or industrial zones, VOCs and particulate matter are a concern.
- **Families with young children or elderly members:** Clean air supports respiratory health for vulnerable populations.
- **Remote workers:** If you spend most of your day at home, investing in air quality is investing in your health and productivity.

### Budget Considerations

**Under $200:** You can find capable air purifiers for small rooms. The Blueair 411i Max is a standout in this range. Expect coverage up to 600 square feet and basic smart features.

**$200–$500:** This is the sweet spot for most households. The Levoit Vital 300S offers excellent performance for medium to large rooms. You'll get better filtration, higher CADR, and more advanced smart features.

**$500–$1,000:** Premium models like the Coway ProX 400 deliver top-tier performance for large spaces. You'll get superior filtration, quieter operation, and robust smart home integration.

**Ongoing Costs:** Don't forget to factor in filter replacement costs. Budget $50–$150 per year depending on the model. Some units offer washable pre-filters that can extend the life of your HEPA filter.

### Room Size and CADR

The general rule of thumb is to choose a purifier with a CADR rating that is at least two-thirds of your room's square footage. For example, a 300-square-foot room needs a CADR of at least 200. For open floor plans, consider a unit with a higher CADR or multiple smaller units.

### Filtration Needs

- **Dust and pollen:** True HEPA is sufficient.
- **Smoke and VOCs:** Look for a unit with a substantial activated carbon filter.
- **Odors:** Carbon filtration is key. More carbon equals better odor removal.
- **Germs and bacteria:** Some purifiers include UV-C or ionization, but these are optional and can produce ozone. Stick with HEPA and carbon for safety.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setting Up Your Air Purifier

Most modern air purifiers are designed for easy setup. Here's what to expect:

1. **Unbox and remove packaging:** Remove all plastic wrap from filters before first use.
2. **Choose the right location:** Place the unit at least 6 inches away from walls and furniture to ensure proper airflow. Avoid corners, which can restrict circulation.
3. **Plug in and power on:** Most units will run a self-check and display current air quality.
4. **Connect to Wi-Fi:** Download the manufacturer's app and follow the in-app instructions. You'll typically need a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi network.
5. **Link to smart assistants:** Use Alexa or Google Home to enable voice control and routines.

### Smart Home Integration Tips

- **Automate based on air quality:** Use IFTTT or native app routines to turn on the purifier when PM2.5 levels exceed a threshold.
- **Schedule for peak times:** Run your purifier during cooking, cleaning, or high pollen hours.
- **Integrate with HVAC:** Some premium purifiers can sync with smart thermostats for whole-home coordination.
- **Monitor remotely:** Check air quality from your phone while at work or on vacation.
- **Use voice commands:** "Alexa, set air purifier to auto mode" or "Hey Google, turn on the bedroom purifier."

### Placement Matters

For best results, place your air purifier in the room where you spend the most time—typically the bedroom or living room. If you have an open floor plan, consider placing the unit centrally or using multiple units for better coverage.
