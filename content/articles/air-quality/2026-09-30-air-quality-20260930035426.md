## Key Features to Consider

Before buying any air purifier, you need to understand what actually matters. Here's a breakdown of the features that make a real difference.

| Feature | Why It Matters | What to Look For |
|---|---|---|
| **CADR Rating** | Measures how quickly a purifier cleans air (higher = faster) | 300+ for large rooms; 200+ for bedrooms |
| **HEPA Grade** | Determines particle capture efficiency | True HEPA H13 or H14 |
| **Activated Carbon** | Removes odors, smoke, and VOCs | At least 1–2 lbs of carbon for odor-heavy homes |
| **Room Coverage** | Indicates maximum effective square footage | Match to your largest room, not total home size |
| **Noise Level** | Affects sleep and daily comfort | Under 25 dB on low; under 55 dB on high |
| **Filter Cost** | Long-term operating expense | $40–$100 per year is typical |
| **Smart Features** | Enables automation and remote control | Wi-Fi, Alexa/Google, auto mode, air quality sensor |
| **Energy Use** | Impacts electricity bills | 20–60 watts typical; Energy Star certified is best |

### CADR: The Most Important Number

Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) tells you how many cubic feet of clean air the unit produces per minute. For a bedroom, aim for a CADR of at least 200. For a living room or open space, look for 300 or higher. Ignore marketing claims about "room size" and check the CADR—it's an independent, standardized measurement.

### Filter Quality and Cost

True HEPA H13 is the gold standard for particle filtration. Avoid units that claim "HEPA-like" or "HEPA-type" filtration—these are marketing terms without standardized performance. Also factor in replacement costs: a cheap purifier with expensive filters can cost more over three years than a premium unit with affordable filters.

### Smart Home Integration

Most modern purifiers offer Wi-Fi and voice assistant support. The quality of the app matters more than you'd think—a clunky app makes daily use frustrating. Look for real-time air quality monitoring, auto mode, and scheduling. If you use Apple HomeKit, check compatibility carefully, as many purifiers only support Alexa and Google.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Air Purifier?

**Allergy sufferers:** If you deal with seasonal allergies, pet dander, or dust sensitivity, a HEPA purifier can significantly reduce symptoms. Look for units with high CADR for pollen and dust.

**Wildfire-affected regions:** If you live in California, Oregon, Washington, or other areas prone to wildfire smoke, an air purifier with substantial activated carbon is essential. Smoke contains both particles and gases—you need both HEPA and carbon filtration.

**Pet owners:** Pet dander and odors are two separate problems. HEPA handles the dander; carbon handles the odors. Choose a purifier with a generous carbon layer.

**Urban apartment dwellers:** If you live near busy roads or industrial areas, VOCs and NO2 are concerns. Look for purifiers with gas-phase filtration and air quality sensors that detect more than just PM2.5.

**New parents:** Nurseries benefit enormously from clean air. Prioritize quiet operation (under 25 dB) and auto mode so you don't have to fiddle with settings at 3 a.m.

### Budget Considerations

**Under $150:** You can find decent small-room purifiers from brands like Levoit and Winix. Expect coverage up to 300 square feet, basic filtration, and limited smart features.

**$150–$350:** This is the sweet spot. You get True HEPA filtration, good CADR ratings, Wi-Fi connectivity, and reasonable filter costs. The Levoit Vital 300S lives here.

**$350–$600:** Large-room coverage, dual-fan designs, and better build quality. The Coway Airmega 400S is the standout.

**$600+:** Premium features like heating/cooling, advanced sensors, and design-forward aesthetics. The Dyson Gen2 is the flagship example.

### Long-Term Costs Matter

Don't just look at the sticker price. Calculate three-year total cost of ownership: purchase price plus filters plus electricity. A $200 purifier with $80 filters every six months costs $680 over three years. A $350 purifier with $40 filters every eight months costs $530. Sometimes spending more upfront saves money.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setup Is Usually Simple

Most modern air purifiers require minimal assembly: remove the filter from its plastic wrap, insert it, plug in the unit, and connect to Wi-Fi via the companion app. Expect 10–15 minutes for full setup, including app configuration.

### Wi-Fi and Voice Control

All three recommended purifiers support 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (note: most don't support 5 GHz networks, which is a common setup frustration). Once connected, you can control them via Alexa or Google Assistant with simple commands like "Alexa, set the air purifier to auto mode."

### Automation Ideas

- **Morning routine:** Have your purifier switch to high speed when your smart alarm goes off, then drop to low after 30 minutes.
- **Cooking mode:** Trigger turbo mode when your smart stove or kitchen motion sensor activates.
- **Wildfire alerts:** Use IFTTT to boost purification when local air quality index (AQI) exceeds 100.
- **Away mode:** Automatically reduce fan speed when everyone leaves home (via geofencing).
- **Sleep schedule:** Set a routine that dims lights and switches the purifier to sleep mode at 10 p.m.

### Placement Tips

Place your purifier where air circulates freely—at least a few inches from walls and furniture. Avoid corners, which restrict airflow. For bedrooms, position it near the door or in the center of the room if possible. For open living spaces, a central location maximizes coverage.
