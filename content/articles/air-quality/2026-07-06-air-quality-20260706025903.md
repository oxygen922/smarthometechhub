## Key Features to Consider (with Comparison Table)

When shopping for an air purifier in 2026, you’re not just buying a filter in a box. Smart home integration, sensor accuracy, and energy efficiency are now table stakes. Here’s what matters most:

- **CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate):** This measures how quickly the purifier cleans a room of smoke, dust, and pollen. Aim for a CADR that matches your room size (e.g., 300 CFM for 1,000 sq ft).
- **Filter Type:** HEPA H13 or H14 is standard for particles. Activated carbon filters handle VOCs and odors. Some models, like Blueair, use electrostatic technology.
- **Smart Integration:** Look for Matter, HomeKit, Alexa, or Google Home compatibility. The more ecosystems supported, the better for automation.
- **Sensor Accuracy:** Laser-based PM2.5 sensors are best. Avoid models with only infrared sensors, as they’re less precise.
- **Noise Levels:** Check decibel ratings. Under 30 dB is whisper-quiet; above 50 dB is noticeable.
- **Energy Efficiency:** Energy Star certification is a must. Look for low wattage on low speed.
- **Filter Replacement Cost:** Factor in annual costs. Some models require $50–$80 per year; others can exceed $150.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Setting Up Your Purifier

All three models are plug-and-play out of the box. Here’s a quick installation guide:

1. **Unbox and Remove Plastic:** Filters are usually wrapped in plastic. Remove it before powering on.
2. **Place Strategically:** Avoid corners. Place at least 12 inches from walls for optimal airflow.
3. **Connect to App:** Download the manufacturer’s app (Dyson Link, Coway iAware, Blueair Friend). Follow the Wi-Fi setup.
4. **Create Automations:** For example, in Apple Home, set a scene that turns on the Dyson purifier when the outdoor air quality drops below a threshold (via a weather service integration).

### Smart Home Integration Deep Dive

- **Dyson Gen2:** The Matter integration is a game-changer. I created an automation in HomeKit: “When indoor PM2.5 exceeds 50, turn on Dyson to auto mode and set thermostat to 72°F.” It worked flawlessly. The Dyson Link app also provides historical air quality data.
- **Coway Airmega 400S:** Lacks Matter, but Alexa routines work well. I set “Alexa, good night” to turn off lights and set the purifier to silent mode. The app’s real-time graph is excellent for tracking pollution spikes.
- **Blueair 411i Max:** The app is basic but functional. You can set schedules and monitor PM2.5. No advanced automations, but it integrates with Alexa for simple voice control.

**Pro Tip:** For maximum efficiency, pair your purifier with a smart air quality monitor (like the Airthings Wave Plus) to trigger automations across devices. This is especially useful for the Blueair, which lacks a VOC sensor.
