## Key Features to Consider

When choosing a smart air purifier for wildfire smoke, not all features are created equal. Here's what to prioritize:

- **CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate):** This measures how quickly the purifier can clean the air. For smoke, look for a CADR of at least 200 for medium rooms and 300+ for large spaces.
- **HEPA Filter Type:** True HEPA (H13 or H14) is essential for capturing smoke particles. Avoid "HEPA-type" or "HEPA-like" filters, which are less effective.
- **Sensor Quality:** A laser-based PM2.5 sensor is more accurate than infrared sensors. Some units also detect VOCs, formaldehyde, and CO2.
- **Smart Integration:** Look for Wi-Fi connectivity, app support, and voice assistant compatibility. Real-time air quality data is a game-changer.
- **Noise Level:** Wildfire events can last days or weeks, so a quiet unit is crucial. Check decibel ratings for low and high speeds.
- **Filter Replacement Cost:** HEPA filters need replacement every 6-12 months. Factor in ongoing costs—some units have filters that cost $50, while others exceed $100.

## Installation & Smart Home Integration

### Installation Process

Setting up a smart air purifier is straightforward, but there are a few key steps to ensure optimal performance:

1. **Unbox and Place Strategically:** Remove all packaging and place the unit at least 3 feet away from walls and furniture. Avoid corners, as airflow is restricted.
2. **Remove Plastic Wrap:** This is a common mistake! Many purifiers have plastic wrap around the HEPA filter that must be removed before use.
3. **Connect to Wi-Fi:** Download the manufacturer's app, create an account, and follow the on-screen instructions to connect the purifier to your home network. Most units use a 2.4 GHz band for stability.
4. **Calibrate Sensors:** Some purifiers require a 10-minute calibration period after setup. Close windows and doors during this time.
5. **Set Automation:** Create schedules (e.g., run at high speed during wildfire alerts) and link to smart home routines.

### Smart Home Integration

The true power of a smart air purifier lies in its integration with your existing ecosystem. Here's how the top three models stack up:

- **Dyson:** Seamless with Apple HomeKit, allowing you to create automations like "If outdoor air quality drops below 50 AQI, turn on the purifier." It also works with IFTTT for advanced recipes.
- **Coway:** Native support for Alexa and Google Assistant. You can say, "Alexa, set the Coway to auto mode," and it will adjust based on sensor readings.
- **Blueair:** Similar integration with Alexa and Google. The Blueair app also offers a "Family View" feature, allowing multiple users to monitor air quality.

For advanced users, consider linking your purifier to a smart thermostat (like Nest or Ecobee) to automatically circulate air when pollution spikes. Some purifiers also integrate with weather apps to preemptively activate during wildfire alerts.
