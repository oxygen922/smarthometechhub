## Key Features to Consider

When evaluating air purifiers, several factors significantly impact real-world performance. Here's what matters most:

| Feature | Why It Matters | What to Look For |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **CADR Rating** | Indicates how quickly the unit cleans air | 200+ CFM for bedrooms, 300+ for living rooms |
| **Filter Type** | Determines what pollutants are captured | True HEPA H13 or H14 for particles; substantial carbon for odors/VOCs |
| **Room Coverage** | Ensures adequate cleaning for your space | Match to room size; consider open floor plans |
| **Noise Level** | Affects usability in bedrooms and living areas | Under 30 dB for sleep; under 55 dB for daytime |
| **Smart Features** | Adds convenience and automation | App control, voice assistants, auto mode |
| **Filter Cost** | Impacts long-term ownership costs | $30-80 annually typical; check availability |
| **Energy Use** | Affects electricity bills | Under 50W typical; energy star certified preferred |

### CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate)

CADR measures the volume of filtered air delivered per minute, expressed in cubic feet per minute (CFM). Higher CADR means faster cleaning. As a rule of thumb, choose a purifier with a CADR rating at least two-thirds of your room's square footage. For a 300-square-foot room, look for 200+ CFM.

### Filtration Stages

Most quality purifiers use multi-stage filtration:
1. **Pre-filter**: Captures large particles (hair, dust); often washable
2. **HEPA filter**: Captures 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns and larger
3. **Activated carbon**: Absorbs odors, smoke, and VOCs
4. **Optional**: UV-C, ionizers, or photocatalytic oxidation (effectiveness varies)

### Smart Home Integration

Modern purifiers increasingly offer app control, voice assistant compatibility, and automation. Consider whether you want:
- Remote monitoring and control
- Scheduling capabilities
- Integration with other smart home devices
- Real-time air quality reporting

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy an Air Purifier?

**Definitely consider if you:**
- Live in areas with wildfire smoke or high pollution
- Suffer from allergies or asthma
- Have pets (especially multiple shedding animals)
- Live in older homes with potential mold or dust issues
- Want to reduce exposure to VOCs from furniture, cleaning products, or cooking

**May not need if you:**
- Have excellent HVAC filtration and live in clean-air regions
- Rarely experience indoor air quality issues
- Have limited budget and minimal air quality concerns

### Budget Considerations

**Budget ($100-200):** The Coway Airmega Mighty represents this tier well. You get solid filtration without smart features. Filter costs are reasonable, and build quality is proven.

**Mid-Range ($200-350):** The Levoit Core 400S Pro exemplifies this segment. Expect smart features, better sensors, and improved design alongside strong filtration.

**Premium ($400+):** Dyson and similar brands offer design, multi-functionality, and comprehensive monitoring. You're paying for aesthetics and features as much as purification performance.

**Long-term costs:** Factor in filter replacements ($30-100 annually depending on model) and energy consumption (typically $20-50/year). Some units with washable pre-filters reduce ongoing expenses.

### Room Size Matching

Don't trust manufacturer coverage claims blindly—they often assume ideal conditions. For best results:
- Bedrooms (100-200 sq ft): Any quality purifier works
- Living rooms (200-400 sq ft): Mid-range units with 200+ CFM CADR
- Open floor plans (400+ sq ft): Multiple units or high-CADR models

### Physical Setup

All three recommended purifiers require minimal installation—essentially unboxing, removing filter packaging, and plugging in. Place units:
- At least 6 inches from walls for proper airflow
